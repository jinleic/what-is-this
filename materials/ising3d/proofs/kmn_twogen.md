# Complete-bipartite two-sum algebras by collective spin

**[THEOREM][COMPUTATION].** For

\[
A=\sum_{v\in K_{m,n}}X_v,\qquad
B=\sum_{u\in L,\,v\in R}Z_uZ_v
   =\left(\sum_{u\in L}Z_u\right)\left(\sum_{v\in R}Z_v\right),
\]

the characteristic-zero Lie algebra generated by the two sums has the new exact dimensions

\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{Lie}\langle iA,iB\rangle_{K_{4,4}}=137,
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{Lie}\langle iA,iB\rangle_{K_{4,5}}=471.
\]

**[THEOREM][COMPUTATION].** Both new dimensions clear the fixed-basis quadratic ceilings, because `137>8(15)=120` and `471>9(17)=153`; the earlier complete-bipartite exceptions remain `44<45` for `K_{2,3}` and `63<66` for `K_{3,3}`.

**[UNRESOLVED].** No eventual universal threshold is proved or refuted.  The collective-symmetry upper bound itself becomes larger than the quadratic ceiling, and finite exact anchors are not an all-size lower bound.

## 1. Multiplicity-free Schur--Weyl reduction

**[LEMMA].** Let `W_r=Sym^r(Q^2)`, of dimension `r+1`, where `r` is twice the spin.  Schur--Weyl duality gives

\[
(\mathbb Q^2)^{\otimes m}
 \cong \bigoplus_{\substack{0\le r\le m\\r\equiv m\pmod2}}
 M_{m,r}\otimes W_r.
\]

Every collective spin operator acts as the identity on `M_{m,r}`, so repeated multiplicity copies cannot change linear independence of Lie words.

**[LEMMA].** On `W_r\otimes W_s`, in the polynomial basis `e_0,...,e_r`, put

\[
X_re_a=a e_{a-1}+(r-a)e_{a+1},\qquad H_re_a=(r-2a)e_a.
\]

The two complete-bipartite generators restrict, up to harmless nonzero physical normalization, to the exact integral matrices

\[
A_{r,s}=X_r\otimes1+1\otimes X_s,
\qquad B_{r,s}=H_r\otimes H_s.
\]

**[THEOREM].** The full two-sum algebra is represented faithfully by the direct sum of these blocks over all allowed `(r,s)`.  When `m=n`, tensor swap makes `(r,s)` and `(s,r)` equivalent, so one may retain only `r<=s` without losing rank.

**[LEMMA].** The unreduced `S_m x S_n` matrix-coordinate count is

\[
\sum_r(r+1)^2\sum_s(s+1)^2
 =\binom{m+3}{3}\binom{n+3}{3}.
\]

This is a representation theorem, not a fitted orbit count.

## 2. Parity and bilinear-form containers

**[LEMMA].** Define exact integral matrices on `W_r` by

\[
R_re_a=e_{r-a},\qquad Z_re_a=(-1)^a e_a,
\qquad
G_r=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{L_r}{\binom r0},\ldots,
\frac{L_r}{\binom rr}\right),
\]

where `L_r` is the least common multiple of the binomial coefficients.  On `W_r\otimes W_s`, define

\[
P=R_r\otimes R_s,\qquad
C=Z_rR_r\otimes Z_s,\qquad
K=(G_r\otimes G_s)C.
\]

Direct multiplication gives

\[
[P,A_{r,s}]=[P,B_{r,s}]=0,
\quad
A_{r,s}^{T}K+KA_{r,s}=0,
\quad
B_{r,s}^{T}K+KB_{r,s}=0,
\]

and

\[
PC=(-1)^{r+s}CP,
\qquad K^T=(-1)^rK.
\]

**[COMPUTATION].** `e194_kmn_reduction.py` checks every displayed matrix identity over the integers for all 23 spin sectors needed by `K_{2,3}`, `K_{3,3}`, `K_{3,4}`, `K_{4,4}`, and `K_{4,5}`.

**[LEMMA].** If `r+s` is odd, the `P=+1` and `P=-1` spaces both have dimension

\[
q=\frac{(r+1)(s+1)}2,
\]

and the form `K` pairs them nondegenerately.  A `K`-skew matrix commuting with `P` is determined by one `q x q` matrix; hence the sector container is `gl(q)` and has dimension `q^2`.

**[LEMMA].** If `r,s` are even and unequal, `K` is symmetric on the two parity spaces of dimensions `(d+1)/2` and `(d-1)/2`, where `d=(r+1)(s+1)`.  The sector container is

\[
\mathfrak{so}\!\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)
\oplus
\mathfrak{so}\!\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right).
\]

**[LEMMA].** If `r,s` are odd and unequal, each parity space has dimension `q=d/2`, the restricted form is alternating, and the sector container is two copies of the symplectic algebra on a `q`-dimensional vector space.  Its dimension is `q(q+1)`.

**[LEMMA].** For a balanced equal-spin block `r=s`, tensor swap `S` supplies one more commuting involution.  On `P=+1`, the form restricts separately to the two swap eigenspaces; on `P=-1`, it pairs the two swap eigenspaces, producing one general-linear factor.

**[LEMMA].** For even `r`, set

\[
a=\left(\frac{r+2}{2}\right)^2,
\qquad b=\left(\frac r2\right)^2,
\qquad c=\frac{(r+1)^2-1}{4}.
\]

The equal-spin container is `so(a)+so(b)+gl(c)`, with dimension

\[
\frac{a(a-1)}2+\frac{b(b-1)}2+c^2.
\]

**[LEMMA].** For odd `r`, set

\[
a=\frac{(r+1)(r+3)}4,
\qquad b=\frac{(r+1)(r-1)}4,
\qquad c=\frac{(r+1)^2}{4}.
\]

The equal-spin container consists of symplectic algebras on vector dimensions `a,b` and one `gl(c)`, with dimension

\[
\frac{a(a+1)}2+\frac{b(b+1)}2+c^2.
\]

## 3. Infinite-family upper bounds

**[THEOREM].** Suppose `m,n` have opposite parity.  Let

\[
a_t=\left\lceil\frac t2\right\rceil,
\qquad
S_t=\sum_{\substack{r>0,\ r\le t\\r\equiv t\pmod2}}(r+1)^2
   =\binom{t+3}{3}-\mathbf 1_{\{t\ {\operatorname{even}}\}}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\dim\mathfrak h_{m,n}
 \le U_{m,n}:=1+\frac{S_mS_n}{4}-a_ma_n.}
\]

**[LEMMA].** The formula sums `q_{r,s}^2-1` over every positive-spin `gl(q_{r,s})` sector and adds one global central direction.  Commutators have zero projection to every general-linear center; exact trace identities show that `B` has zero center signature here and all remaining center and zero-spin projections come from the single seed `A`.  Thus separate sector centers must not be counted independently.

**[THEOREM].** For `K_{m,m}`, sum the semisimple dimensions of the displayed sector containers over unordered positive labels `r<=s`.  The global center contributes at most one dimension when `m` is odd and at most two when `m` is even; the second even direction is the common `A` action on zero-spin blocks.  This gives a finite closed representation-theoretic upper bound for every balanced size.

**[COMPUTATION].** The balanced upper equals `63` at `m=3` and `137` at `m=4`; the opposite-parity upper equals `44,167,471` for `(m,n)=(2,3),(3,4),(4,5)`.  Each value is computed from the sector formulas, independently of modular row reduction.

## 4. Exact modular anchors

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_p}W_p
\le \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}W
\le U_{m,n}.
\]

A modular rank is a rational lower bound, never a rational upper bound.

## 5. Local terms are a different algebra

**[THEOREM][EXTERNAL].** The local-term generating set

\[
\{iX_v:v\in V\}\cup\{iZ_uZ_v:uv\in E\}
\]

is not subject to collective `S_m x S_n` invariance.  By the branching theorem in `proofs/clifford_grade_classification.md`, every Hamiltonian complete-bipartite graph in the table has local-term dimension

\[
D_{\rm local}(N)=
\begin{cases}
2^{2N-2}-(-1)^{N/2}2^{N-1},&N\text{ even},\\
2^{2N-2}-1,&N\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
\qquad N=m+n.
\]

**[THEOREM].** Consequently, the small complete-bipartite two-sum dimensions do not contradict the exponential local-term branching algebra; they concern different generating sets.

## 6. What complete-bipartite symmetry says about an eventual threshold

**[LEMMA].** For the Hamiltonian family `K_{m,m+1}`, the largest positive-spin block has

\[
q=\frac{(m+1)(m+2)}2,
\qquad U_{m,m+1}\ge q^2.
\]

For `m>=3`,

\[
4\left(q^2-(2m+1)(4m+1)\right)
=m\left(m^3+6m^2-19m-12\right)>0.
\]

The cubic in parentheses equals `12` at `m=3` and has positive successive difference `3m^2+15m-12` thereafter.

**[THEOREM — method limitation].** The proved complete-bipartite symmetry container already lies above the quadratic ceiling for every `K_{m,m+1}` with `m>=3`.  It therefore cannot, by itself, produce an infinite below-ceiling counterfamily to an eventual universal threshold.

**[COMPUTATION].** The exact finite rows `K_{3,4}`, `K_{4,4}`, and `K_{4,5}` actually clear their ceilings, while `K_{2,3}` and `K_{3,3}` remain the exact finite counterexamples.

**[UNRESOLVED].** Equality with the representation upper is not proved for all `m`, no monotonicity in `m` is proved, and no value of a universal `n_0` follows from these computations.
