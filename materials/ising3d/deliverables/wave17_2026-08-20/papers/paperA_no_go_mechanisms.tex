\begin{abstract}
The three-dimensional nearest-neighbour Ising model remains unsolved. This draft consolidates exact, finite, and explicitly scoped obstructions to several standard routes to an exact solution. For a finite layer graph $\Gamma=(V,E)$ we use $A=\sum_{v\in V}X_v$ and $B=\sum_{(ij)\in E}Z_iZ_j$, with transfer representative $V=\exp(K^*A)\exp(KB)$ up to a scalar. We prove the closed Dolan--Grady defect formula, a tridiagonal no-go for representative 3D layers, the induced-claw obstruction to term-wise Majorana bilinearity, and the exact Pauli-string centralizer theorem. We then give basis-independent spectral certificates: an exact Sylvester-inertia proof for the $2\times3$ layer, an independent two-prime pair-product certificate for the $2\times6$ layer, and an all-size anisotropic theorem for every graph with a branching vertex. An arbitrary $8\times8$ identical-$L$ tetrahedron/RLLL ansatz is ruled out over characteristic zero, while the full translation-invariant scalar Kac--Ward family remains unresolved. Every statement is separated from finite computation, external input, and open scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Status and scope}
The result is negative but load-bearing: it excludes named mechanisms, not exact solvability by every possible representation. ``Theorem'' below means a proof in the stated finite or algebraic scope; ``computation'' means an exact finite calculation with a checked artifact; ``external'' means a cited result not established by this repository. None of the results below derives the thermodynamic-limit free energy, critical exponents, magnetization, or correlation functions.

For a layer graph $\Gamma$, the standard transfer factors are
\[
 A=\sum_{v\in V}X_v,\qquad B=\sum_{(ij)\in E}Z_iZ_j,
\]
and $V=\exp(K^*A)\exp(KB)$ up to a scalar. A one-dimensional layer has maximum degree at most two; a genuine three-dimensional cubic layer has a vertex of degree at least three.

\section{Local algebraic obstructions}
\subsection{Dolan--Grady defect}
The Dolan--Grady relations are
\[
[A,[A,[A,B]]]=16[A,B],\qquad [B,[B,[B,A]]]=16[B,A].
\]
They generate the Onsager algebra and are the algebraic core of the original two-dimensional solution.

\begin{theorem}[Dolan--Grady defect]
For every finite simple graph $\Gamma$, with $d(v)=\deg(v)$ and neighbourhood $N(v)$,
\begin{align}
[A,[A,[A,B]]]-16[A,B]&=0,\\
[B,[B,[B,A]]]-16[B,A]&=R(\Gamma),\\
R(\Gamma)&=24i\sum_{v\in V}Y_v\left((d(v)-2)\sum_{u\sim v}Z_u
 +2\sum_{\{u,u',u''\}\subset N(v)}Z_uZ_{u'}Z_{u''}\right).
\end{align}
Consequently $R(\Gamma)=0$ if and only if every vertex has degree $0$ or $2$. Its quartic part vanishes if and only if $\Delta(\Gamma)\leq2$. The number of four-body Pauli terms is exactly $\sum_v\binom{d(v)}3$, the number of induced claws in the associated frustration graph.
\end{theorem}

The proof is local. Only edges incident to the selected vertex contribute to the nested commutators; the unique $Y_v$ prevents cancellation between different vertices. Hence a bulk square-lattice layer, and therefore the standard 3D transfer layer, fails (DG2) by an explicit quartic operator. The exact term-by-term identity was checked on sixteen graph families, including paths, cycles, stars, grids, torus layers, and a cubic graph.

\subsection{Term-wise Majorana and claw obstruction}
The standard Pauli generator set has frustration graph equal to the subdivision $S(\Gamma)$: $X_i$ is adjacent precisely to $Z_iZ_j$ for incident edges, while the $X$ terms commute with one another and all $ZZ$ terms commute with one another.

\begin{theorem}[Claw obstruction]
If a set of Hermitian Pauli terms is mapped injectively, after a unitary change of basis, to Majorana bilinears $i\gamma_p\gamma_q$, its frustration graph has no induced $K_{1,3}$. Therefore the standard layer generator set can be term-wise Majorana-bilinear only when $\Delta(\Gamma)\leq2$. In particular, every square-grid layer containing a degree-three or degree-four vertex fails this representation.
\end{theorem}

The argument is combinatorial: two bilinears anticommute exactly when their two-element index sets intersect in one element. Three pairwise commuting neighbours of one bilinear would force two of their index sets to share one element, a contradiction. This theorem is about the displayed generating set; it does not by itself exclude a radically different decomposition.

A quantitative version addresses an enlarged Gaussian realization. If the generated real Lie algebra $\g$ embeds into the span of bilinears of $m$ fermionic modes (that is, $2m$ Majorana operators), then
\[
 \dim\g\leq\dim\mathfrak{so}(2m)=m(2m-1),\qquad
 m\geq\frac{1+\sqrt{1+8\dim\g}}4.
\]
This is a dimension obstruction, not a claim that no larger nonlocal embedding can ever exist.

\subsection{Tridiagonal/Askey--Wilson relations}
The general tridiagonal relation is tested through
\[
T_0=\beta T_1+\gamma^*T_2+\rho^*T_3,
\quad
(T_0,T_1,T_2,T_3)=([B,B^2A+AB^2],[B,BAB],[B,BA+AB],[B,A]).
\]
It is linear in the three unknown parameters for fixed $A,B$.

\begin{theorem}[Representative tridiagonal no-go]
For the boundaryless torus layers $3\times3$, $3\times4$, $4\times4$, $3\times5$, $4\times5$, and $5\times5$, and for the open $2\times3$ and $3\times3$ layers, the second tridiagonal relation has no solution over $\Q$ (hence no solution over $\C$). The result is invariant under every affine redefinition $A'=aA+s$, $B'=bB+t$ with $ab\neq0$.
\end{theorem}

For the $4\times4$ torus, one Pauli coefficient gives a complete one-line certificate:
\[
 W=Z_0Y_5Z_6Z_{10},\qquad [T_0]_W=-48,
 \qquad [T_1]_W=[T_2]_W=[T_3]_W=0.
\]
The other failing layers carry explicit one- or four-row witnesses. The witness is the same quartic $Y_vZ_{u_1}Z_{u_2}Z_{u_3}$ that appears in the Dolan--Grady defect.

\subsection{Pauli-string centralizer and flux scope}
\begin{theorem}[Pauli centralizer]
Let $\Gamma$ be connected and let
\[
 G=\{X_i:i\in V\}\cup\{Z_iZ_j:(ij)\in E\}.
\]
The Pauli strings commuting with every element of $G$ are exactly
\[
 \{I,\;\prod_{i\in V}X_i\}.
\]
The same centralizer holds for the summed pair $\{A,B\}$ within the Pauli group.
\end{theorem}

Writing a Pauli string as $(a\mid b)\in\mathbb F_2^{2n}$, commutation with every $X_i$ forces $b=0$, and commutation with every bond forces $a_i=a_j$ along every edge. Connectedness leaves only the two displayed strings. This closes a Kitaev-style family of Pauli fluxes on the same Hilbert space, but not all possible non-Pauli symmetries, enlarged-space constructions, or ordinary lattice translation sectors.

\section{Basis-independent spectral no-go results}
Algebraic failure of the obvious generators does not exclude ``free fermions in disguise.'' The following tests use only the spectrum.

\subsection{Gaussian subset-product criterion}
If a positive-spectrum Gaussian operator on $m$ modes has single-particle factors $u_1,\ldots,u_m\geq1$, its spectrum is
\[
 \{\lambda_0\prod_{k\in S}u_k:S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\}.
\]
If the three lowest eigenvalues are distinct, then $\lambda_1\lambda_2/\lambda_0$ must also be an eigenvalue. For rational $t=\tanh(K^*/2)$, the symmetrized transfer representative is, up to a positive scalar,
\[
 R=P_t\operatorname{diag}(q^{(b_k-\epsilon)/2})P_t,
 \quad P_t(k,l)=t^{\operatorname{Ham}(k,l)},\quad q=\frac{1+t^2}{2t},
\]
which is exact rational symmetric positive definite.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem S: exact $2\times3$ spectral no-go]
At $t=1/3$ (equivalently $q=\exp(2K)=5/3$), the open $2\times3$ layer transfer operator is not a fermionic Gaussian operator on six modes in any Majorana basis. Exact rational inertia counts at the two outward-rounded endpoints of the forced-eigenvalue interval are both $8$, so the interval contains no eigenvalue, while a six-mode Gaussian spectrum would contain $\lambda_1\lambda_2/\lambda_0$.
\end{theorem}

The control chains of lengths four and six show the opposite inertia pattern, as expected for the 2D free-fermion model. Equal inertia counts prove absence; unequal counts only show that some eigenvalue lies in the interval and are not a proof of exact presence.

\subsection{The $2\times6$ parity-half certificate}
For a 12-mode Gaussian, any subset of one parity half has at most
\[
 A_{\rm same}(12)=\sum_{\substack{2\le r\le12\\r\ \mathrm{even}}}\binom{12}{r}2^{12-r}=261625
\]
distinct within-half pair products. The physical $(P=+1,\rho=+1)$ block of the open $2\times6$ layer has dimension $1056$ and $\binom{1056}{2}=557040$ pair slots.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem $2\times6$-NG]
At $t=1/3$, the physical $P=+1$ sector of the open $2\times6$ layer is not either parity half of any 12-mode Gaussian subset-product multiset. On both primes $1000003$ and $2000003$, the exact pair-polynomial computation gives
\[
 \deg\gcd(C,C')\le295414<295415
 =\binom{1056}{2}-A_{\rm same}(12).
\]
Thus the block has at least $261626$ distinct within-half pair products, at least one above the Gaussian ceiling.
\end{theorem}

This is a one-sided modular lower-bound argument over $\Q$, not a claim that the exact number of distinct products is $261626$. The saved Euclidean tail was independently replayed with bit-identical remainders; the standalone verifier performs 38 checks, including artifact-key and digest-chain checks.

\subsection{All-size anisotropic theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Theorem F: all-size anisotropic no-go]
Let $G$ be any finite simple graph with a vertex of degree at least three and $n=|V|\ge4$. For the inhomogeneous rational layer family with independent site parameters $t_v$ and bond parameters $w_e$, there is a nonzero polynomial $\rho_G$ with
\[
 \deg\rho_G\le 4(2n+|E|)S(S-1),\qquad S=\binom{2^n}{2},
\]
such that whenever $\rho_G\neq0$, the eigenvalues have at least
\[
 3^n-2^n+48\,3^{n-4}
\]
distinct unordered slot-pair products. A full $n$-mode Gaussian multiset has at most $3^n-2^n$, so the generic inhomogeneous layer is not Gaussian.
\end{theorem}

The exceptional set is a proper algebraic hypersurface. This theorem is genuinely anisotropic: it does not cover the physical isotropic curve, where the present invariant reaches the Gaussian ceiling on the tested claw core. The named isotropic $2\times3$, $2\times4$, and $3\times3$ points are covered separately by finite or coupling-generic certificates; no isotropic all-size theorem is claimed.

\section{Tensor and Kac--Ward ansatzes}
\subsection{Identical-$L$ tetrahedron/RLLL equation}
Let $L(q)$ be the verified six-leg Ising tensor and consider
\[
 R_{123}L_{145}(q)L_{246}(q)L_{356}(q)
 =L_{356}(q)L_{246}(q)L_{145}(q)R_{123},
\]
with an arbitrary $8\times8$ matrix $R$ and no auxiliary grading assumption.

\begin{theorem}[Ungraded tetrahedron no-go]
Over every characteristic-zero field, if $q\notin\{0,1,-1\}$, the only arbitrary $8\times8$ solution of the identical-$L$ equation is $R=0$. Therefore no invertible auxiliary $R$ exists for finite ferromagnetic weights $0<q<1$.
\end{theorem}

The proof concatenates two complementary $64\times64$ minors whose common factor is
\[
 q^{109}(q-1)^{92}(q+1)^{77};
\]
the remaining factors are coprime by an explicit Bezout identity. The scope is limited to identical local factors, this vertex-type equation, and auxiliary dimension eight.

\subsection{Kac--Ward status}
A full translation-invariant scalar Kac--Ward transition has 30 directed-direction weights, reduced to 25 variables on a generic five-edge gauge chart. The exact 2D planar identity is reproduced as a control.

\begin{theorem}[Positive-real scalar Kac--Ward no-go]
No assignment of nonnegative real values to all 30 allowed direction transitions satisfies the coefficientwise scalar Kac--Ward identity on every free cubic box. In fact, the order-four trace equation on the free $2\times2\times2$ box is
\[
 F_4=48+8(M_{xy,+}+M_{xy,-}+M_{xz,+}+M_{xz,-}+M_{yz,+}+M_{yz,-}),
\]
which is strictly positive on the nonnegative orthant. The stored rational Positivstellensatz identity certifies infeasibility on all 32 selected gauge strata.
\end{theorem}

The full complex scalar family remains unresolved. Exact order-$4,6,8$ systems have finite-field points and modular Groebner attempts timed out; a numerical construction point fails an independent $3\times3\times3$ order-eight holdout, but that does not prove ideal emptiness. Matrix-valued weights, position-dependent weights, spin-structure sums, and higher auxiliary dimensions remain outside this paper's no-go claims.

\section{Conclusion}
The results jointly exclude the standard Onsager/Dolan--Grady route on branching layers; the full tridiagonal relation family for representative 3D layers; term-wise Majorana bilinearity of the standard generators; same-space Pauli-string flux decompositions; full-layer Gaussian spectra at the certified named points and generically in the anisotropic family; the displayed identical-$L$ eight-dimensional tetrahedron ansatz; and the nonnegative-real scalar Kac--Ward corner.

They do not exclude an exact solution based on a larger Gaussian restriction, non-Pauli conserved quantities, a different matrix-valued or dynamical tensor ansatz, a nonlocal transformation, or an as-yet unknown mechanism. The full 3D Ising problem is therefore still open.
