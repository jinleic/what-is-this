# The 3D Ising layer transfer operator is not spectrally free-fermionic

**Status tags.** **[THEOREM]** proved here, **[LEMMA]** proved here, **[COMPUTATION]** exact
machine computation in this repo, **[EXTERNAL]** proved elsewhere and cited.

Scripts: `experiments/e37_spectral_gaussianity.py` (high-precision survey),
`experiments/e38_gaussianity_certificate.py` (exact rational certificate).
Artifacts: `results/spectral/gaussianity.json`, `results/spectral/gaussianity_certificate.json`.
Test: `tests/test_spectral_gaussianity.py`.

## 1. Why this is not implied by the other no-go results

The four earlier obstructions in this repository
(`proofs/algebraic_obstruction.md`, `proofs/dolan_grady_defect.md`,
`proofs/tridiagonal_nogo.md`, `proofs/kac_ward_3d_obstruction.md`) are all statements about an
*algebra*: the Lie algebra generated by the transfer factors is not a quadratic Clifford algebra,
does not satisfy Dolan--Grady, admits no tridiagonal relation, and so on.

None of them excludes the *free fermions in disguise* scenario **[EXTERNAL]** (Fendley 2019), in
which a spin chain has exactly the spectrum of a free-fermion model even though the obvious
generators do not close on a quadratic algebra. In that scenario the model is still exactly
solvable, and every algebraic obstruction above would be a statement about a badly chosen
generating set rather than about the model.

This note narrows that escape route sharply, though it does not seal it completely -- see the
scope in sec. 7. The test is **basis-independent**: it looks only at the multiset of eigenvalues,
so it is insensitive to Jordan--Wigner ordering, local rotations, relabelling, or any other change
of mode basis. What it rules out is that `V` is itself a Gaussian operator on `n` modes; what
survives is `V` as a restriction of a larger Gaussian, and parity-projected pairs in bases where
fermion parity is not the physical spin flip.

## 2. The criterion

**[LEMMA] (spectral Gaussianity).** Let `W = C exp(Q)` be a fermionic Gaussian operator on `m`
modes, `Q` quadratic in Majoranas, `C > 0`, **and assume `W` has positive real spectrum**,
equivalently that the canonical single-particle parameters `gamma_k` are real. Then the `2^m`
eigenvalues of `W` are

        lambda_s = C exp( (1/2) sum_{k=1..m} s_k gamma_k ),      s in {-1,+1}^m,

so writing `t(lambda) = log lambda - log lambda_min`, the multiset `{ t(lambda) }` is exactly the
multiset of the `2^m` subset sums of the `m` nonnegative numbers `gamma_k`.

*Proof.* Bring `Q` to canonical form by an orthogonal transformation of the Majoranas; `W` becomes
a product of `m` commuting single-mode factors, and the eigenvalues multiply. Reality of the
`gamma_k` is exactly the positive-spectrum hypothesis. Replacing `gamma_k` by `|gamma_k|` and
absorbing the sign into `C` we may take every `gamma_k >= 0`, and then the all-minus sign choice
realises the minimum. []

**The hypothesis is satisfied here.** Section 3 shows the layer operator `V` is similar to
`S = exp(K* A/2) exp(K B) exp(K* A/2)`, which is real symmetric positive definite. So `V` has real
positive spectrum and the lemma applies to it. Without that hypothesis the lemma is false: a
general Gaussian may have complex eigenvalues and no ordering, and the subset-sum statement would
not even be well posed.

We use the contrapositive. Equivalently, and this is the form that avoids logarithms entirely:

**[LEMMA] (multiplicative form).** With `lambda_0 <= lambda_1 <= ... ` the sorted spectrum and
`u_k = exp(gamma_k) >= 1`, a Gaussian spectrum is `{ lambda_0 * prod_{k in S} u_k : S subset [m] }`.
**Assume the three lowest eigenvalues are distinct** (`lambda_0 < lambda_1 < lambda_2`). Then sorting
forces

        lambda_1 / lambda_0 = u_(1),        lambda_2 / lambda_0 = u_(2),

the two smallest single-particle factors, because every `u_k >= 1` makes any product over a set of
size `>= 2` at least as large as each of its factors, so the second and third smallest eigenvalues
must be singletons. Since `{u_(1), u_(2)}` is itself an admissible two-element subset,

        **lambda_1 * lambda_2 / lambda_0  MUST be an eigenvalue.**

*Proof.* As above; the only point needing care is degeneracy. If `u_(1) = u_(2)` the two smallest
singletons coincide in value but still correspond to distinct indices, so the two-element subset
exists and the conclusion is unchanged. Requires `m >= 2` and that the three lowest eigenvalues are distinct. This is strictly weaker
than full spectral simplicity, which is NOT assumed and NOT established (it would require enclosing
all 64 eigenvalues). The hypothesis suffices because:

* `lambda_0 < lambda_1` excludes zero modes: a zero mode `u_k = 1` gives `lambda_0 = lambda_1`,
  so distinctness forces every `u_k > 1`.
* `lambda_1 < lambda_2` excludes a repeated smallest factor: if the two smallest nonzero factors
  coincided (`u_(1) = u_(2)`), the singletons `{u_(1)}` and `{u_(2)}` would produce the same
  eigenvalue, giving `lambda_1 = lambda_2`. So `u_(1) < u_(2)`.
* With `u_(1) > 1` and `u_(1) < u_(2)`, no 2-element product can intervene (every such product is
  `>= u_(1) u_(2) >= u_(2)`), so the three lowest values are `1, u_(1), u_(2)` from the empty set
  and two singletons, and `lambda_1/lambda_0 = u_(1)`, `lambda_2/lambda_0 = u_(2)`.

Note that one zero mode gives `lambda_0 = lambda_1` (NOT `lambda_0 = lambda_1 = lambda_2`); the
correct statement is the one above. Higher eigenvalues may still be degenerate from duplicate
factors or product coincidences, and this does not affect the argument.

The hypothesis is verified for the `2x3` layer: certified enclosures of the three smallest
eigenvalues are disjoint, with relative gaps of **53.0%** and **40.9%**. []

This is the entire certificate. It involves no logarithm, no transcendental function, and no
floating point: it is a statement about ratios of eigenvalues.

## 3. Exact rationality of the layer operator

**[LEMMA].** Let `t = tanh(K*/2)` be **rational**. Then the layer transfer operator is, up to a
positive scalar, an exact rational symmetric positive definite matrix.

*Proof.* `exp(K* A/2) = prod_i (cosh(K*/2) + sinh(K*/2) X_i) = (1-t^2)^{-n/2} prod_i (1 + t X_i)`,
and `<k| prod_i (1 + t X_i) |l> = t^{hamming(k,l)}`, so `P_t` is rational. From
`K* = -(1/2) log tanh K` we get `exp(-2K) = tanh K* = 2t/(1+t^2)`, hence

        q := exp(2K) = (1 + t^2) / (2t),   rational.

The diagonal of `B = sum_{(ij)} Z_iZ_j` has entries `b_k` all congruent mod 2 to the bond count
`|E|`, so with `eps = |E| mod 2`, `exp(K b_k) = exp(K eps) * q^{(b_k - eps)/2}` where the first
factor is a **global** scalar. Therefore

        S = exp(K* A/2) exp(K B) exp(K* A/2) = (positive scalar) * R,
        R = P_t diag( q^{(b_k - eps)/2} ) P_t,

with `R` exactly rational, symmetric, positive definite. `S` is similar to `V = exp(K* A) exp(K B)`.
A global positive scalar multiplies every eigenvalue equally and therefore cancels from the ratios
in sec. 2. []

The run uses `t = 1/3`, giving `q = exp(2K) = 5/3`, i.e. `K = (1/2) log(5/3) = 0.25541...`, a
physically relevant point near the 3D critical coupling.

## 4. Certification by Sylvester's law of inertia

**[LEMMA].** For a rational shift `sigma`, exact symmetric Gaussian elimination of `R - sigma I`
produces pivots whose number of negative entries equals `#{ eigenvalues of R below sigma }`. If no
pivot vanishes, `sigma` is not an eigenvalue and the count is exact. **[EXTERNAL]** Sylvester's law
of inertia.

Two consequences drive the computation:

* bisection on rational shifts gives certified rational enclosures of individual eigenvalues;
* **absence** of any eigenvalue in `[a,b)` is certified by just **two** counts: if
  `#{lambda < a} = #{lambda < b}` then the interval contains none.

The second is what makes the certificate cheap. Refuting Gaussianity requires showing the forced
eigenvalue `lambda_1 lambda_2 / lambda_0` is *missing*, and that is two inertia counts.

Soundness details that were necessary to get right, and are implemented:

* a count is only ever attributed to the shift **actually evaluated**; when a trial shift hits a
  degenerate pivot the code moves the shift and reports the moved value, rather than silently
  perturbing behind the caller's back;
* the final absence window is rounded **outward**, so the tested interval is a superset of the
  predicted one and an `absent` verdict transfers to the original interval;
* positive definiteness is asserted by `count_at(R, 0) == 0`.

## 5. Result

**[THEOREM S].** At `t = tanh(K*/2) = 1/3`, i.e. `exp(2K) = 5/3`, the transfer operator of the
`2x3` open-grid layer -- the smallest genuine 3D Ising layer, having vertices of degree 3 -- is
**not** a fermionic Gaussian operator on `n = 6` modes, in any choice of Majorana modes.

*Proof.* By sec. 3 the operator is a positive scalar times the exact rational matrix `R`. Certified
rational enclosures of the three smallest eigenvalues of `R` (relative width `1.8e-8`) give the
forced value `lambda_1 lambda_2 / lambda_0` in the rational interval

        [ 0.0347241721739 , 0.0347241728052 ].

Two exact inertia counts at the outward-rounded endpoints both return **8**. Hence no eigenvalue of
`R` lies in that interval. By the Lemma of sec. 2 a Gaussian operator on 6 modes must have one
there. []

**The two directions are not symmetric, and only one of them is a proof.**

* *Absence is rigorous.* If the counts agree at both ends, the window contains **no** eigenvalue.
  Since the window encloses the predicted value, the predicted value is definitely not an
  eigenvalue, and Gaussianity is refuted. This is the direction used for the 3D layer.
* *Presence is only consistency.* If the counts differ, the window contains **some** eigenvalue --
  but not necessarily one equal to the predicted value, since the window has positive width. So a
  differing count does **not** certify that the forced eigenvalue is exactly present.

**Control, same script, same arithmetic.** For the open chains `n = 4` and `n = 6` -- which are 2D
Ising strips and *are* free-fermionic -- the counts differ across the window (`5` vs `6`, and `7`
vs `8`), i.e. an eigenvalue does lie where a Gaussian requires one. Read with the caveat above,
this is a consistency check, not a second theorem: its purpose is to show the machinery does not
reject everything put into it. The full subset-sum reconstruction of sec. 6, which matches to
`2.03e-58`, is the much stronger positive statement for the 1D case.

## 6. Supporting high-precision survey

`experiments/e37_spectral_gaussianity.py` runs the full subset-sum reconstruction, which is
strictly more informative than the single forced eigenvalue, at 60 digits. **[COMPUTATION]**

| layer | verdict | diagnostic |
|---|---|---|
| open chain `n = 4,6,8,10` (2D Ising strip) | Gaussian, exactly `n` generators | residual `2.03e-58` at 60 dps |
| `2x2` layer `= C_4`, 2-regular | 2D-like, as Theorem DG predicts | sector reflection defect `8.9e-15` |
| open grid `2x3`, `2x4`, `3x3` | **not** Gaussian | first-failure gap `1.6e-2 .. 3.6e-2` at `K = 0.3` |
| torus `2x3`, `3x3` | **not** Gaussian | fails even reflection symmetry, defect `1.17`, `1.28` |

Two features are worth recording.

*The test tracks the degree criterion, not the lattice label.* The `2x2` "3D layer" is really the
4-cycle `C_4`, which is 2-regular and therefore carries no quartic Dolan--Grady defect
(`proofs/dolan_grady_defect.md`). The spectral test classifies it with the 2D cases without being
told. The first genuine failure appears exactly when a degree-3 vertex appears.

*The verdict is uniform in the coupling, and the sweep needs arbitrary precision.* At 40 digits,
across `K = 0.05 .. 2.0` and spanning both sides of the 3D critical coupling, the open chain is
spectrally Gaussian at every value tested and the `2x3` layer at none:

| `K` | control residual (1D chain) | failure gap (3D layer) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | `5.77e-33` | `3.50e-2` |
| 0.1 | `5.39e-35` | `6.85e-2` |
| 0.2 | `4.40e-37` | `1.53e-1` |
| 0.221654626 | `2.37e-38` | `1.89e-1` |
| 0.3 | `7.74e-38` | `1.58e-2` |
| 0.5 | `4.59e-38` | `3.58e-3` |
| 1.0 | `1.25e-36` | `5.11e-4` |
| 2.0 | `5.34e-34` | `1.17e-7` |

Two honest caveats. First, float64 is *not* adequate for this sweep: at `K = 0.05` the large
`K* = -(1/2) log tanh K` spreads the spectrum over many orders of magnitude and the 1D control
residual degrades to `5.8e-8`, so in double precision the control fails for purely numerical
reasons. The table above is therefore computed at 40 digits. Second, the failure gap **shrinks
markedly deep in the ordered phase**: at `K = 2.0` it is `1.17e-7`, seven orders of magnitude
below its value near criticality. It still exceeds the control residual there by a factor of about
`2e26`, so the verdict is not in doubt, but anyone extending this to larger `K` must raise the
working precision accordingly rather than assuming the margin stays large.

*A weaker but fully basis-independent necessary condition.* Any single Gaussian satisfies
`lambda_S lambda_{S^c} = C^2`, so its log-spectrum is symmetric about its midpoint. The torus
layers violate this outright (defect `1.17` and `1.28` against `1.7e-10` for the strips), which
refutes single-family Gaussianity for them without any reconstruction at all.

## 7. Scope -- what is and is not excluded

**Excluded.**

* `V` is not a Gaussian operator on `n` modes, in any Majorana basis. Certified exactly for the
  `2x3` open layer; established at 60 digits for `2x4`, `3x3`, and the `2x3`, `3x3` tori.
* The trivial ancilla extension `V (x) I_{2^a}` is likewise excluded, because a generator equal to
  zero merely doubles every multiplicity, so `V (x) I_{2^a}` is Gaussian on `n+a` modes precisely
  when `V` is Gaussian on `n` modes.

**Not excluded -- recorded honestly as open.**

* `V` arising as the **restriction of a larger Gaussian** `W` on `n+a` modes to an invariant
  subspace. Then `spec(V)` is a submultiset of `spec(W)`, and a submultiset of a subset-sum
  multiset need not itself be a subset-sum multiset. This route is untouched by the argument here.
* A **parity-projected pair** of Gaussians in some basis. For the torus layers the *physical*
  spin-flip decomposition `P = prod_i X_i` does not rescue them: reflection symmetry fails inside
  each physical sector. But that only rejects that particular sector decomposition. Concluding
  more would require proving that the physical `P` must coincide with fermion parity in every
  candidate Gaussian structure, which is not proved here.
* All-`K` and all-size statements. Theorem S is proved at one exact rational coupling on one layer;
  the survey extends it numerically, not as a theorem.

## 8. Relation to the other results

The obstruction appears exactly where Theorem DG says the quartic defect appears: at the first
degree-3 vertex. `C_4` passes, `2x3` fails. This is independent evidence for the same local
mechanism, arrived at without ever forming a commutator -- Theorem DG is an algebraic identity,
this is a spectral fact.

It is *not* the same statement, and must not be merged with the others in any summary. The
algebraic no-gos say a particular generating set fails to close. Theorem S says the spectrum itself
is wrong for a free-fermion model on `n` modes, which removes the most natural form of the
disguised-free-fermion escape while leaving the two variants listed in sec. 7 open.
