## Frozen 2D-only protocol

### Ratio PRG

For three consecutive sizes and a one-term shift

\[
\theta_1(L)=\theta_e+A L^{-p},
\]

the edge and amplitude cancel from the drop ratio. The local exponent $p_L$ is the positive solution of

\[
\frac{\theta_1(L-1)-\theta_1(L)}
     {\theta_1(L)-\theta_1(L+1)}
=
\frac{(L-1)^{-p_L}-L^{-p_L}}
     {L^{-p_L}-(L+1)^{-p_L}}.
\]

Equivalently, define the pair amplitudes

\[
A_{L-1,L}(p)=
\frac{\theta_1(L-1)-\theta_1(L)}{(L-1)^{-p}-L^{-p}},
\qquad
A_{L,L+1}(p)=
\frac{\theta_1(L)-\theta_1(L+1)}{L^{-p}-(L+1)^{-p}}.
\]

The same PRG value is the phenomenological **crossing**
\(A_{L-1,L}(p_L)=A_{L,L+1}(p_L)\). Thus the recorded ratio and
crossing estimators are algebraically identical rather than two correlated fits.

For an isotropic $d$-dimensional volume, integrated zero counting gives

\[
\theta_1(L)-\theta_e\asymp L^{-d/(1+\sigma)},
\qquad
\sigma=\frac{d}{p_\infty}-1.
\]

**[EMPIRICAL CALIBRATION]** The exact open-square sequence $L=4,\ldots,15$ supplies ten local centers $L=5,\ldots,14$. The effective powers drift monotonically from

\[
p_5=1.4704783151949138093\ldots
\quad\text{to}\quad
p_{14}=1.8970052725817917130\ldots,
\]

so an uncorrected local estimate is visibly unresolved.

### Known correction structure and Bulirsch–Stoer

Open boundaries permit analytic boundary corrections beginning at $1/L$. The protocol therefore fixes, before reading any 3D exponent information,

\[
h=\frac1L,\qquad \omega=1,
\qquad
p_L=p_\infty+\frac{a}{L}+\frac{b}{L^2}.
\]

Depth-two Bulirsch–Stoer is Neville extrapolation to $h=0$. If $T_i^{(0)}=p_{L_i}$, then

\[
T_i^{(k)}=T_{i+1}^{(k-1)}+
\frac{T_{i+1}^{(k-1)}-T_i^{(k-1)}}
     {(L_{i+k}/L_i)^\omega-1}.
\]

The rolling depth-two values move from $\sigma=-0.0842962296\ldots$ on centers $5,6,7$ through $-0.1652193439\ldots$ on centers $8,9,10$, and to $-0.1859971955\ldots$ on centers $12,13,14$. This drift is retained rather than hidden.

The central overdetermined quadratic correction fit over every center gives

\[
\widehat\sigma_{2d}=-0.1503243693382576465\ldots.
\]

## Exact three-size identifiability barrier

### Exact angle enclosures

For each of the six relevant angles (two couplings times three sizes), the certificate reconstructs the reduced integer polynomial $Q(t)$ with $t=\cos\theta$.

1. Around the stored refined $t$ it forms the rational interval $t_0\pm10^{-70}$.
2. Exact rational Horner evaluation gives opposite signs of $Q$ at the endpoints.
3. Exact Sturm counting gives exactly one root in that interval.
4. Around the stored angle it forms $\widehat\theta\pm10^{-60}$.
5. Rational alternating Taylor sums through cosine orders 80 and 81 prove
   \[
   \cos(\widehat\theta+10^{-60})<t_-<t_*<t_+
   <\cos(\widehat\theta-10^{-60}).
   \]

**[THEOREM]** Each exact first angle is therefore inside its stated radius-$10^{-60}$ rational interval. No floating-point root value is needed for the adversarial-model inequalities.

### Interpolation lemma

Let $L_1,L_2,L_3$ be distinct positive sizes, let $e$ be any proposed edge, and choose any $p>0$. There is a unique quadratic

\[
C(u)=c_0+c_1u+c_2u^2
\]

such that

\[
C(1/L_i)=L_i^p(\theta_i-e),\qquad i=1,2,3.
\]

This is ordinary interpolation at the three distinct nodes $1/L_i$. Consequently

\[
\Theta_p(L)=e+L^{-p}C(1/L)
\]

fits all three observations exactly. This lemma alone shows why three points cannot control a leading exponent when two analytic correction terms are admitted.

### Two globally admissible models

Set $L_i=2,3,4$ and fix the same edge in both models,

\[
e=\frac1{50}.
\]

Choose $p=2$ and $p=4$. The corresponding three-dimensional edge exponents are exactly

\[
\sigma_2=\frac{3}{2}-1=\frac12,
\qquad
\sigma_4=\frac{3}{4}-1=-\frac14.
\]

The artifact stores the complete exact fractions, not these rounded displays. Because interpolation is linear in the three angles, the radius-$10^{-60}$ angle intervals propagate to exact rational boxes for every $c_j$.

For all four coefficient boxes, exact rational inequalities certify

\[
c_0>0,\qquad c_2>0,
\qquad \operatorname{disc} C=c_1^2-4c_0c_2<0.
\]

Thus $C(u)>0$ for every real $u$. Moreover

\[
\Theta_p'(L)=-L^{-p-3}Q_p(L),
\]

where

\[
Q_p(L)=p c_0L^2+(p+1)c_1L+(p+2)c_2.
\]

The exact interval upper bound for $\operatorname{disc}Q_p$ is negative in all four cases, while its leading coefficient is positive. Hence $Q_p(L)>0$ for every real $L$ and

\[
\Theta_p(L)>\frac1{50},
\qquad
\Theta_p'(L)<0
\quad (L>0).
\]

**[THEOREM]** At each certified-high-temperature coupling, both exact-angle-dependent coefficient triples exist inside the stored boxes, fit the exact $L=2,3,4$ angles, approach the same positive edge from above, and decrease for every $L>0$, yet their exponents differ by $3/4$. The present sizes therefore cannot identify $\sigma$ under this correction class.

The conclusion is conditional on neither a benchmark exponent nor a numerical fit. To defeat the barrier one needs at least one of:

- additional isotropic cube sizes;
- a proved sign or magnitude restriction excluding one coefficient box;
- a theorem reducing the allowed correction structure.

## Fixed-field strip/cylinder extension

A transfer calculation can extend length without retaining all field-polynomial coefficients. For a $2\times2$ layer state $s$, define

\[
w_s(z)=x^{b(s)}z^{d(s)},\qquad x=\frac23,
\]

where $b(s)$ is the number of broken in-layer bonds and $d(s)$ is the number of down spins. One layer step is

\[
v'_{s}=w_s(z)\sum_t x^{\operatorname{Hamming}(s,t)}v_t.
\]

At fixed $z=e^{i\theta}$ this is a 16-state recurrence. Multiplying by $z^{-N/2}$ makes the spin-reversal-symmetric partition function real on the unit circle. A positive normalization after each layer prevents dynamic-range loss and cannot change a sign.

**[UNRESOLVED / GEOMETRIC LIMIT]** Increasing the last dimension at fixed $2\times2$ cross-section takes a quasi-one-dimensional limit. The data therefore demonstrate computational reach but do not weaken the exact cube identifiability barrier.
