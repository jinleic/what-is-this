# The precise algebraic obstruction in three dimensions

Status of each statement is marked: **[THEOREM]** (proved here), **[LEMMA]** (proved here),
**[EXTERNAL]** (proved elsewhere, cited), **[COMPUTATION]** (exact machine computation in this
repo, script named), **[CONJECTURE]**.

---

## 0. The object

Write the classical Ising model on `L_x x L_y x L_z` with transfer along `z`.  Summing the
`z`-bonds by the standard identity `exp(K_z s s') = (2 sinh 2K_z)^{1/2} exp(K_z^* s s')` in the
`sigma^x` basis, with the dual coupling

    K_z^*  =  - (1/2) log tanh K_z ,        equivalently   sinh 2K_z sinh 2K_z^* = 1,

gives the standard operator form (Schultz-Mattis-Lieb; see `notes/onsager_derivation.md` for the
2D case reproduced in code here)

    Z = (2 sinh 2K_z)^{n L_z / 2} * Tr ( V^{L_z} ),      n = L_x L_y,

    V = V_field * V_layer ,
    V_field = exp( K_z^* * A ),      A = sum_{i in layer} X_i,
    V_layer = exp( B ),              B = sum_{<ij> in layer} K_{ij} Z_i Z_j.

`X_i, Z_i` are Pauli operators on the `n` layer sites.  The layer graph `Gamma` is:

* `d = 2` classical (1D layer): `Gamma` = path (open) or cycle (periodic).  Max degree `<= 2`.
* `d = 3` classical (2D layer): `Gamma` = square grid.  Max degree `3` (edge) or `4` (bulk).

**Everything below is a statement about this one difference: `deg <= 2` versus `deg >= 3`.**

## 1. Pauli strings, the symplectic picture

Encode `Q_v = X^{a} Z^{b}`, `v = (a|b) in F_2^{2n}`.

**[LEMMA 1]** `Q_v Q_w = (-1)^{b_v . a_w} Q_{v+w}` and therefore

    [Q_v, Q_w] = ( (-1)^{b_v . a_w} - (-1)^{b_w . a_v} ) Q_{v+w}
               = 0                        if <v,w> = 0,
               = +- 2 Q_{v+w}             if <v,w> = 1,

where `<v,w> = b_v . a_w + b_w . a_v (mod 2)` is the symplectic form.

*Proof.* `Z^b X^{a'} = (-1)^{b.a'} X^{a'} Z^b` on each qubit, extended multiplicatively. The
commutator statement follows by subtracting the two orderings. `[]`

**[COROLLARY 2]** The real Lie algebra generated by a set `G` of Hermitian Pauli strings is
*spanned by Pauli strings*, namely by the closure `S(G)` of `G` under the partial operation
`(v,w) -> v+w` defined when `<v,w> = 1`.  In particular `dim_R (DLA) = |S(G)|`, an integer computable
by a purely combinatorial breadth-first search in `F_2^{2n}`.

Implemented in `ising.clifford.dla_pauli_closure`.

## 2. The claw obstruction: what exactly fails in 3D

**Definition.** For a set `{h_a}` of Hermitian Pauli strings, the *frustration graph* `G` has
vertex set `{a}` and an edge `{a,b}` iff `h_a` and `h_b` anticommute (Chapman-Flammia,
arXiv:2003.05465, Def. 2 / Eq. (4)).

**Definition.** `{h_a}` is *term-wise Majorana-bilinear* if there exist Majorana operators
`gamma_1,...,gamma_{2m}` (`{gamma_p, gamma_q} = 2 delta_{pq}`) and an injective assignment
`a -> {p(a), q(a)}` of two-element subsets with `h_a  =  i gamma_{p(a)} gamma_{q(a)}` after a
unitary change of basis.  (This is exactly the map of Chapman-Flammia Eq. (20)-(21).)

**[LEMMA 3]** `i gamma_p gamma_q` and `i gamma_r gamma_s` anticommute iff
`|{p,q} cap {r,s}| = 1`, and commute iff the intersection has size `0` or `2`.

*Proof.* Move `gamma_r gamma_s` through `gamma_p gamma_q` using `gamma_x gamma_y = - gamma_y gamma_x`
for `x != y` and `gamma_x^2 = 1`.  Each of the two transpositions of a distinct index contributes
`(-1)^2 = +1`; sharing exactly one index leaves a single uncancelled sign. `[]`

**[THEOREM 4 — claw obstruction].**
*If `{h_a}` is term-wise Majorana-bilinear then its frustration graph `G` contains no induced
`K_{1,3}` (no "claw"): there is no vertex with three pairwise non-adjacent neighbours.*

*Proof.* Suppose `a` is adjacent to `b, c, d` and `b, c, d` are pairwise non-adjacent.  Put
`e_a = {p(a), q(a)}` and likewise `e_b, e_c, e_d`.  By Lemma 3, adjacency to `a` gives
`|e_b cap e_a| = |e_c cap e_a| = |e_d cap e_a| = 1`.  Since `|e_a| = 2`, by pigeonhole two of
`e_b, e_c, e_d` meet `e_a` in the *same* element; say `e_b cap e_a = e_c cap e_a = {p}`.  Write
`e_b = {p, beta}`, `e_c = {p, gamma}`.  Injectivity of `a -> e_a` forces `e_b != e_c`, so
`beta != gamma` and `|e_b cap e_c| = 1`, i.e. `b` and `c` anticommute — contradicting that they
are non-adjacent. `[]`

(Equivalently: the frustration graph must be a *line graph* `L(R)` for the "hopping graph" `R`
— Chapman-Flammia Theorem 1, an if-and-only-if for injective maps of this type — and line graphs
are claw-free by Beineke's theorem.  Theorem 4 is the self-contained half we actually need.)

**[THEOREM 5 — the 2D/3D dichotomy].**
*Let the layer graph `Gamma` have vertex set `V`, edge set `E`, and consider the standard generator
set `{X_i : i in V} cup {Z_i Z_j : (ij) in E}` of the layer transfer operator.  Then:*

*(a) `X_i` anticommutes with `Z_j Z_k` iff `i in {j,k}`; `X_i` and `X_j` always commute;
`Z_iZ_j` and `Z_kZ_l` always commute.  Hence the frustration graph is exactly the **incidence
graph** of `Gamma`: it is the subdivision `S(Gamma)` of `Gamma` (each edge of `Gamma` becomes a
degree-2 vertex joined to its two endpoints).*

*(b) `S(Gamma)` contains an induced claw iff `Gamma` has a vertex of degree `>= 3`.*

*(c) Consequently the generator set is term-wise Majorana-bilinear only if `Delta(Gamma) <= 2`,
i.e. only if every layer component is a path or a cycle.*

*(d) For hypercubic lattices this says exactly: the construction works for the 2D classical Ising
model (1D layer, `Delta = 2`) and fails for the 3D classical Ising model (2D layer, `Delta >= 3`)
for every layer with more than two sites in each direction.*

*Proof.* (a) `X_i` and `Z_jZ_k` anticommute iff they disagree in an odd number of tensor slots;
they disagree only at slot `i`, and only if `i in {j,k}`.  The `X`'s mutually commute and the
`ZZ`'s mutually commute since each family is diagonal in a common basis.  So edges of the
frustration graph join exactly `X_i` to the `Z_iZ_j` incident at `i`; that is the subdivision
graph.  (b) In `S(Gamma)` the neighbours of the vertex `X_i` are the `deg(i)` edge-vertices at `i`,
and edge-vertices are pairwise non-adjacent by (a); so `deg_Gamma(i) >= 3` gives an induced claw.
Conversely, the only vertices of `S(Gamma)` of degree `>= 3` are the `X_i` with
`deg_Gamma(i) >= 3` (edge-vertices have degree exactly 2), so if `Delta(Gamma) <= 2` then
`S(Gamma)` has maximum degree 2 and is a disjoint union of paths and cycles, which are claw-free.
(c) is Theorem 4 plus (b).  (d) is (c) applied to the path/cycle vs. grid layer graphs. `[]`

**[COMPUTATION 6]** `experiments/e07_dla.py` verifies (b) directly on every layer used in this
repository: no claw for any 1D chain or ring, and an explicit induced claw for every 2D grid with a
degree-3 vertex, with the witness triple printed.

**Remark (why this is sharp and not a hand-wave).**  Theorem 5 identifies the failure as a
*local, combinatorial* property — the coordination number of the layer — not as "the algebra gets
complicated".  It also explains why the 2D solution is not fragile: a path and a cycle are both
line graphs (`P_m = L(P_{m+1})`, `C_m = L(C_m)`), so both open and periodic 2D Ising admit the
bilinear map; the periodic case has an even hole (`C_{2n}`) which is why it needs the two
parity sectors of Kaufman's formula but remains free-fermionic.

**Remark (scope).** Theorem 5 is about *this* generating set.  A different decomposition of the
same operator into Pauli terms could in principle have a claw-free frustration graph.  Section 3
removes that loophole.

## 3. A generating-set-independent, quantitative no-go

**[THEOREM 7]** *Let `g` be the real Lie algebra generated by `{i h_a}` (the dynamical Lie algebra
of the term set).  Suppose there exist an ancilla space, an isometry/unitary `U`, and Majorana
operators `gamma_1..gamma_{2m}` such that `U (h_a ⊗ 1) U^\dagger` lies in the real span of the
bilinears `{i gamma_p gamma_q}` for every `a`.  Then*

    dim g  <=  dim so(2m)  =  m(2m-1),      hence   m  >=  (1 + sqrt(1 + 8 dim g))/4 .

*Proof.* The real span `Q` of `{i gamma_p gamma_q : p<q}` is closed under commutation (Lemma 3
plus `[i gamma_p gamma_q, i gamma_q gamma_r] = -2 i gamma_p gamma_r`) and is isomorphic to
`so(2m)`, of dimension `C(2m,2) = m(2m-1)`.  Conjugation by `U` is an injective homomorphism of Lie
algebras, `g -> u(H')`.  Its image is generated by elements of `Q`, hence contained in `Q`.
Injectivity gives `dim g <= dim Q`. `[]`

This is *independent of the choice of Pauli decomposition* (the DLA depends only on the set of
terms, and adding ancillas does not change it) and it is *quantitative*: it converts an exactly
computed integer into a lower bound on the number of fermionic modes any Gaussian representation
must use.

Observed exact pattern for 2D layers (`[CONJECTURE]`, matched by all computed cases):
`n` odd: `dim g = 4^{n-1} - 1 = dim su(2^{n-1})`;
`n = 8`: `dim g = 2^{n-1}(2^{n-1}-1) = 2 dim so(2^{n-1})`;
`n = 6`: `dim g = 2^{n-1}(2^{n-1}+1) = 2 dim sp(2^{n-1})`.
In every case `dim g` grows like `4^{n}` up to a constant, i.e. the DLA is a *maximal* algebra
compatible with the `Z_2` spin-flip symmetry, not a `so(poly)`.

**[COROLLARY 9]** For the 3x3 layer, any Gaussian (Majorana-bilinear) representation of the layer
transfer operator of the 3D Ising model — allowing arbitrary ancillas and arbitrary unitary change
of basis — requires at least `m = 128` fermionic modes (256 Majoranas), against `2n = 18` Majoranas
in the 2D analogue.  **This is a finite bound on a finite layer.**  No asymptotic theorem about the
family of layers is proved here: the depth-graded growth evidence in
`reports/final_technical_report.md` sec. 4.2 is consistent with exponential growth but the
all-`L` statement remains open (sec. 7.5), and `D_{2L} >= 2^L` is certified only for `L = 2..6`.
What CAN be said is that the finite lower bound `m >= 128` already far exceeds any polynomial in
`n = 9` at this size, so a representation faithful to `n` qubits is excluded on this layer.

## 4. The sharper question: the two-generator (Onsager) algebra

Theorem 7 rules out Gaussian representations, but Onsager's own 1944 solution did not use
free fermions: it used the fact that the *two* operators `A = sum_i X_i` and
`B = sum_{<ij>} Z_iZ_j` generate a small Lie algebra (the Onsager algebra), which suffices to
diagonalise `V = e^{K^* A} e^{B}` even though the individual terms are never needed.
This is the correct generator set for the transfer matrix, and it is much smaller than the term
set, so Theorem 7 does not apply to it.

`experiments/e08_onsager_algebra.py` computes `dim <A,B>_Lie` exactly over `F_p`, `p = 2^61-1`.

| layer | `dim <A,B>_Lie` |
|---|---|
| open chain, `n = 2..16` | `n^2` (verified `4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81,100,121,144,169,196,225,256`) |
| ring, `n = 3..12` | `3n - 1` (verified `8,11,14,17,20,23,26,29,32,35`) |
| grid `2x2` (= the 4-cycle) | `11` |
| grid `2x3` | `263` |
| grid `2x4` | `2952` |
| `P_6` + one extra bond | `470`  (vs `36` for `P_6`) |
| `P_7` + one extra bond | `3426` (vs `49` for `P_7`) |
| `P_8` + one extra bond | `6397` (vs `64` for `P_8`) |

**[COMPUTATION 10]** In 1D the algebra is *quadratic* in the layer size: `n^2` for the open chain
and `3n-1` for the ring.  This is the exact, machine-verified statement of Onsager integrability
for the finite chain.  For the tested 2D layers the dimensions are `11, 263, 2952, 8034` and the
depth-graded profiles grow geometrically rather than linearly (see
`reports/final_technical_report.md` sec. 4.2); whether that growth is exponential in the layer
area is an open question.

**[ADVERSARIAL CORRECTION — recorded because it refutes a natural but false claim]**
The claw diagnostic and the dimension diagnostic do **not** coincide in general.
`experiments/e13_minimal_obstruction.py` finds layer graphs that contain claws yet have *small*
`<A,B>` algebras: the star `K_{1,3}` gives `24`, `K_{1,4}` gives `33`, and `K_4` gives `15` — all
comparable to the claw-free `P_4` (`16`) and `C_4` (`11`).  These are highly symmetric exceptional
graphs whose automorphism group collapses the algebra; they are not members of a growing lattice
family.  The correct statements are therefore:

* claw-free (`Delta <= 2`) `==>` `dim <A,B>` polynomial (`n^2` or `3n-1`) — verified to `n = 16`;
* claw present `==>` no term-wise Majorana-bilinear map (Theorem 4) and a nonzero *quartic*
  Dolan-Grady defect (`proofs/dolan_grady_defect.md`), but the dimension of `<A,B>` must be
  measured separately and is NOT implied;
* for the growing lattice families that actually matter, `dim <A,B>` is measured on the
  computed sizes to grow much faster than polynomially, and **a single added bond suffices**:
  `P_n` gives `n^2`, while `P_n` plus one extra bond gives `470` (`n=6`), `3426` (`n=7`),
  `6397` (`n=8`). Whether this faster-than-polynomial growth is genuinely exponential in the
  layer area is an open question; `D_{2L} >= 2^L` is certified only for `L = 2..6`
  (`proofs/ladder_growth.md`).

**One added bond destroys the Onsager structure.**  That is the sharpest empirical form of the
obstruction, and it is exactly consistent with Theorem DG: the added bond creates the first
degree-3 vertex and hence the first quartic Dolan-Grady defect.

## 5. Closing the last escape route: there are no conserved fluxes

Theorem 7 forbids a Gaussian representation of the *finite* `3x3` layer with fewer than 128
modes.  It does
not by itself forbid the **Kitaev mechanism**: a spin model can be non-Gaussian globally and still
exactly solvable if it has many commuting conserved "flux" operators, because the Hamiltonian then
block-diagonalises and each block may separately be quadratic in fermions.  This is precisely what
a "higher-dimensional Jordan-Wigner with an emergent `Z_2` gauge field" would have to supply.

**[THEOREM 11 — no fluxes].** *Let `Gamma` be a connected graph and
`G = {X_i : i in V} u {Z_iZ_j : (ij) in E}`.  The set of Pauli strings commuting with every element
of `G` is exactly `{ I , prod_{i in V} X_i }`.*

*Proof.*  Write the string as `Q_{(a|b)}`.  Commuting with `X_i = Q_{(e_i|0)}` requires
`<(a|b),(e_i|0)> = b_i = 0` for every `i`, hence `b = 0` and the string is `X^a`.  Commuting with
`Z_iZ_j = Q_{(0|e_i+e_j)}` requires `a_i + a_j = 0 (mod 2)` for every edge, so `a` is constant on
each connected component; on a connected graph `a = 0` or `a = (1,...,1)`. `[]`

**[LEMMA 12].** For Pauli strings, `[P, sum_a h_a] = 0` iff `P` commutes with every `h_a`
separately: the commutator is `sum_{a : {P,h_a}=0} 2 P h_a`, and the strings `P h_a` are pairwise
distinct for distinct `a`, so no cancellation between terms is possible.  Hence the centraliser of
the *summed* pair `{A,B}` equals the centraliser of the term set.

**[COMPUTATION 13]** `experiments/e27_flux_sectors.py` verifies Theorems 11 and Lemma 12 against
brute-force enumeration over all `4^n` Pauli strings for chains, rings, and `2x3`, `2x4`, `3x3` and
`3x3`-torus layers: the centraliser has exactly 2 elements in every case.

**Consequence, stated with its exact scope.**  Kitaev-type conserved fluxes *are* Pauli strings
(in the honeycomb model the plaquette operators `W_p` are products of single-site Paulis), so
Theorem 11 says there are none here: the layer operator admits no family of commuting Pauli-string
fluxes and therefore no `2^{#plaquettes}` flux decomposition of the Kitaev kind.  This closes the
Kitaev route, and closes it for a reason unrelated to dimension: it fails identically in 1D too,
where solvability instead comes from Majorana bilinearity (Theorem 5).

**[SCOPE — corrected after an adversarial finding].**  Theorem 11 classifies the centraliser
*inside the Pauli group*.  It does NOT say the Hilbert space has only two invariant sectors: the
full operator commutant is strictly larger.  An explicit counterexample found during the audit is
the one-site lattice translation `U` on a cyclic layer, which commutes with both `A` and `B`, has
`U^3 = I` on `C_3`, and is linearly independent of `{I, prod_i X_i}` (nonzero exact Gram
determinant).  Momentum sectors therefore exist — but they are space-group symmetries, not
`Z_2` fluxes, they do not grow in number like `2^{#plaquettes}`, and they do not induce a
Gaussian block structure.  Flux mechanisms using non-Pauli operators, or Pauli operators on an
enlarged Hilbert space with ancillas, are also NOT excluded by Theorem 11.

What is established is therefore precise and narrower than "no gauge structure exists": *for these
generators, on the same Hilbert space, there is no nontrivial Pauli-string gauge structure at all.*
