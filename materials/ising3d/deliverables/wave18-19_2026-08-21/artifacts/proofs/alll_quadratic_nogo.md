## 1. Result and exact scope

**[THEOREM 1 — all-`L` same-space no-go].** Let `L >= 2`, put `n = 2L`, and let

```text
g_L = Lie_R { i X_v : v in V(2 x L) } union
            { i Z_u Z_v : {u,v} an open-ladder bond }.
```

There are `n = 2L` field terms and `3L-2` bond terms, hence `5L-2` local terms in total. For every
`L >= 2`,

```text
dim_R g_L >= n(2n-1) + 1 > n(2n-1) = dim so(2n).                 (1)
```

Consequently the local terms cannot all be quadratic forms in `2n` Majoranas (`n` fermionic
modes), after any unitary change of basis on the physical `2^n`-dimensional Hilbert space. The same
conclusion holds even if one permits a reducible exact CAR representation on that physical space.

**[THEOREM 2 — quantitative all-`L` bound].** Define

```text
C_L = (1/2) binom(4L, 2L),    L odd,
C_L =       binom(4L, 2L+2),  L even,

D_L = 56,                         L = 2,
D_L = 2(2L)(4L-1) + C_L,          L >= 3.                         (2)
```

Then `dim_R g_L >= D_L`. Therefore every exact quadratic realization satisfying the intertwining
hypothesis in Theorem 4 below requires

```text
m >= M_L := min {q in Z_{>=0} : q(2q-1) >= D_L}
           = ceil((1 + sqrt(1+8D_L))/4).                           (3)
```

In particular, for every `L >= 3`,

```text
m > 2^(2L) / sqrt(5(4L+1)).                                      (4)
```

Thus the required number of fermionic modes grows exponentially in `L`; it is not repaired by a
fixed, polynomial, or otherwise modest number of ancillary modes.

**[THEOREM 3 — the claw-free missing case].** At `L=2` the layer graph is the four-cycle, has
maximum degree two, and the induced-claw obstruction of `proofs/algebraic_obstruction.md` does not
apply. Nevertheless the explicit grade-`2` and grade-`6` families below give
`dim g_2 >= 56 > 28 = dim so(8)`, and (3) gives `m >= 6` rather than the four modes available on the
physical four-qubit space. The known characteristic-zero equality `dim g_2=56` shows this lower
bound is sharp at `L=2`.

**[UNRESOLVED — what is not claimed].** No all-`L` equality for `dim g_L` is used or claimed here.
`proofs/alternation_law.md` certifies equality with its exponential dimension formula only at
`L=2,3,4,5`; its all-`L` saturation step is explicitly open in that note. The present proof needs
only one central-rung grade family. It does not duplicate the separate all-grade saturation work.

## 2. The necessary dimension condition

**[LEMMA 4 — quadratic Lie ceiling].** Let `gamma_1,...,gamma_{2m}` satisfy
`{gamma_a,gamma_b}=2 delta_ab`. The real anti-Hermitian quadratic span

```text
q_m = span_R { gamma_a gamma_b : 1 <= a < b <= 2m }
```

is closed under commutators, is isomorphic to `so(2m)`, and has dimension

```text
dim q_m = binom(2m,2) = m(2m-1).                                 (5)
```

If a unitary basis change sends every Hermitian local term to a real linear combination of
`i gamma_a gamma_b`, then conjugation embeds `g_L` in `q_m`. Hence

```text
dim g_L <= m(2m-1),
```

and solving `2m^2-m-dim g_L >= 0` gives (3).

*Proof.* Two quadratic monomials commute when their index pairs meet in zero or two indices. If they
meet in one index, the CAR give, up to sign,

```text
[gamma_a gamma_b, gamma_b gamma_c] = 2 gamma_a gamma_c.
```

Thus the span is closed and has the standard matrix-unit brackets of `so(2m)`. The monomials are
linearly independent, so its dimension is `binom(2m,2)`. Unitary conjugation is an injective Lie
homomorphism and sends the algebra generated by the local terms into this closed span. The positive
root of `2m^2-m-dim g_L` is `(1+sqrt(1+8 dim g_L))/4`. `square`

**[LEMMA 5 — modes available on the physical Hilbert space].** If `2m` exact Majoranas act on a
nonzero complex Hilbert space of dimension `2^n`, then `m <= n`; if that space is the full
irreducible Fock representation, then `m=n`.

*Proof.* The commuting involutions `K_j=i gamma_{2j-1} gamma_{2j}` have joint sign spaces indexed by
`{+-1}^m`. Multiplication by `gamma_{2j-1}` is an isomorphism between two sign spaces that differ
only in sign `j`. Since at least one joint sign space is nonzero, all `2^m` are nonzero and have the
same dimension. Hence `2^m` divides the Hilbert-space dimension `2^n`, so `m<=n`. Irreducibility
makes every joint sign space one-dimensional. `square`

**[THEOREM 6 — enlarged invariant-code version].** Let `H` be the physical space and let `K` carry
`2m` Majoranas. Suppose an isometry `U:H -> K` has a code `U(H)` invariant under quadratic
operators `q_a in q_m` and

```text
q_a U = U (i h_a)
```

for every anti-Hermitian local generator `i h_a`. Restriction to the invariant code maps the Lie
algebra generated by the `q_a` onto `g_L`; therefore `dim g_L <= dim q_m` and (3) still holds. The
same is immediate under the stronger identity `U(i h_a)U^dagger=q_a`.

**[UNRESOLVED — non-invariant compression].** A mere compression identity
`U^dagger q_a U=i h_a`, without invariance of `U(H)`, is not covered: in general the commutator of
compressions is not the compression of the commutator. This proof neither constructs nor excludes a
larger-space realization once `m >= M_L`. It supplies a necessary mode count, not a sufficiency
theorem.

## 3. A Hamiltonian path and all path bilinears

**[LEMMA 7 — canonical path Majoranas].** Order the ladder sites along the perimeter Hamiltonian
path

```text
p_1,...,p_n = top(1),...,top(L),bottom(L),...,bottom(1).
```

Define `N=2n=4L` Pauli Majoranas, with phases fixed arbitrarily, by

```text
gamma_{2j-1} = X_{p_1} ... X_{p_{j-1}} Z_{p_j},
gamma_{2j}   = X_{p_1} ... X_{p_{j-1}} Y_{p_j}.                   (6)
```

They satisfy the CAR, and their packed Pauli vectors form a basis of `F_2^{2n}`. The `N-1`
consecutive bilinears are, up to nonzero scalars, the literal local terms

```text
G_{2j-1} = X_{p_j},
G_{2j}   = Z_{p_j} Z_{p_{j+1}}.                                  (7)
```

*Proof.* The usual Jordan-Wigner prefix in (6) gives anticommutation for different indices and
squares to one. Also `gamma_{2j-1} gamma_{2j}` is proportional to `X_{p_j}`, while
`gamma_{2j} gamma_{2j+1}` is proportional to `Z_{p_j}Z_{p_{j+1}}`. Binary independence follows
constructively: XORing the pair at site `j` gives `X_{p_j}`, and then (6) recursively recovers each
`Z_{p_j}`. `square`

**[LEMMA 8 — exact bracket word for every path bilinear].** For `1 <= a < b <= N`, define
recursively

```text
H_{a,a+1} = G_a,
H_{a,b}   = [G_{b-1}, H_{a,b-1}]       (b >= a+2).                (8)
```

Every leaf in (8) is a local term, every displayed bracket is nonzero, and

```text
H_{a,b} \doteq gamma_a gamma_b,                                  (9)
```

where `\doteq` means equality up to a nonzero scalar. The `binom(N,2)=n(2n-1)` strings in (9) are
linearly independent.

*Proof.* The base case is (7). If (9) holds at `b-1`, then `G_{b-1}` has Majorana support
`{b-1,b}` and `H_{a,b-1}` has support `{a,b-1}`. They meet in exactly one index, so their bracket
is a nonzero scalar times `gamma_a gamma_b`. This proves (9) by induction. Distinct two-element
Majorana supports give distinct packed Pauli vectors because the Majorana vectors in Lemma 7 are a
binary basis; distinct Pauli strings are Hilbert-Schmidt orthogonal. `square`

**[THEOREM 9 — the requested polynomial witness].** The unused left rung

```text
R_1 = Z_{top(1)} Z_{bottom(1)}
```

is a local generator and is not one of the grade-`2` strings (9): along the path it is the
Clifford monomial with support `{2,3,...,N-1}`, of grade `N-2`. Therefore (9) together with `R_1`
is an explicit family of

```text
binom(N,2)+1 = n(2n-1)+1
```

independent elements of `g_L`. This proves (1), with the exact word (8) for every path-family member
and the one-letter word `R_1` for the last member.

## 4. The perimeter-cycle strengthening

**[LEMMA 10 — all complementary bilinears].** The rung `R_1` is, up to scalar,

```text
R_1 \doteq Gamma_{[N] \ {1,N}},
```

where `Gamma_S` denotes the ordered product of the Majoranas indexed by `S`. For every
`1 <= a < b <= N`, the following exact bracket recipe is nonzero and evaluates to the indicated
string:

```text
D_{a,b}^{(0)} = R_1,
D_{a,b}^{(1)} = [H_{1,a}, D_{a,b}^{(0)}]       if a>1, else D_{a,b}^{(0)},
D_{a,b}       = [H_{b,N}, D_{a,b}^{(1)}]       if b<N, else D_{a,b}^{(1)},

D_{a,b} \doteq Gamma_{[N] \ {a,b}}.                              (10)
```

*Proof.* The path-bond product telescopes:

```text
prod_{j=1}^{n-1} (Z_{p_j}Z_{p_{j+1}}) = Z_{p_1}Z_{p_n}=R_1.
```

The corresponding disjoint Majorana pairs are `{2,3},{4,5},...,{N-2,N-1}`, giving the stated
support. In the first optional bracket of (10), `{1,a}` meets the current support in `a` only, so it
replaces the hole `1` by the hole `a`. The second optional bracket similarly replaces hole `N` by
hole `b`. Lemma 8 expands each control `H` into local-term leaves. `square`

**[THEOREM 11 — ring lower bound].** The grade-`2` family (9) and grade-`N-2` family (10) are
disjoint for `n>=4`. Hence, for every `L>=2`,

```text
dim g_L >= 2 binom(N,2) = 2n(2n-1).                              (11)
```

At `L=2`, (11) is `dim g_2>=56`, proving Theorem 3 without using a claw.

## 5. A central-rung binomial family

**[LEMMA 12 — the Clifford swap rule].** Let `S` have even cardinality. For distinct `a,b`,

```text
[gamma_a gamma_b, Gamma_S] = 0                       if |{a,b} cap S| is 0 or 2,
[gamma_a gamma_b, Gamma_S] \doteq Gamma_{S triangle {a,b}}
                                                        if |{a,b} cap S| is 1.  (12)
```

*Proof.* Moving the two control Majoranas through an even monomial contributes a commutation sign
`(-1)^t`, where `t=|{a,b} cap S|`. Thus the commutator is nonzero exactly for odd `t`; here that
means `t=1`. Cancelling the repeated Majorana leaves the symmetric-difference support. `square`

**[LEMMA 13 — central-rung support].** A rung in column `c` joins path positions

```text
j=c,                 k=n-c+1.
```

It is the product of the path bonds from `j` through `k-1`, so it is one Majorana monomial of grade

```text
q_c = 2(k-j) = 2(n-2c+1).                                       (13)
```

Choose

```text
c=(L+1)/2                 for L odd, giving q_c=2L=n,
c=L/2                     for L even, giving q_c=2L+2=n+2.       (14)
```

**[LEMMA 14 — an explicit word for every selected grade string].** Let `S_0` be the Majorana
support of the chosen rung in (14), and let `S` be any support with `|S|=|S_0|`. Write

```text
S_0 \ S = {a_1<...<a_r},        S \ S_0 = {b_1<...<b_r}.
```

Define

```text
W_0(S)=R_c,
W_t(S)=[H_{min(a_t,b_t),max(a_t,b_t)}, W_{t-1}(S)],   1<=t<=r.   (15)
```

Then every bracket in (15) is nonzero and `W_r(S) \doteq Gamma_S`. Thus (8) and (15) together are
an explicit iterated-local-bracket word for each selected member.

*Proof.* Before step `t`, the current support contains `a_t` and not `b_t`; the pairs are disjoint,
so earlier swaps do not change that fact. The control in (15) therefore meets the current support in
exactly one index. Lemma 12 replaces `a_t` by `b_t`. Induction gives the claim. `square`

**[THEOREM 15 — conservative central count].** For even `L`, retain all grade-`n+2` supports in
Lemma 14, giving `binom(4L,2L+2)` independent strings. For odd `L`, where the grade is the middle
grade `n`, retain one lexicographically smaller support from every complement pair `{S,S^c}`,
giving exactly `binom(4L,2L)/2` independent strings. This deliberate halving avoids requiring any
claim about how the two middle exterior-power/Hodge components combine. In both cases the retained
strings are distinct Pauli strings by Lemma 7 and belong to `g_L` by (15).

**[THEOREM 16 — proof of the strong dimension bound].** For `L>=3`, the central grade in (14) is
distinct from grades `2` and `N-2` in Theorem 11. The three families are therefore disjoint and
linearly independent, giving `dim g_L>=D_L` in (2). For `L=2`, the selected central grade is already
the complementary grade in Theorem 11, so it is not counted twice and `D_2=56`.

**[LEMMA 17 — elementary growth bound].** For every `L>=2`,

```text
C_L >= 2^(4L+1) / (5(4L+1)).                                   (16)
```

*Proof.* The largest of the `4L+1` binomial coefficients in `(1+1)^(4L)` is the central one, so
`binom(4L,2L)>=2^(4L)/(4L+1)`. For odd `L`, halving leaves a factor `1/2 >= 2/5`. For even `L`,

```text
binom(4L,2L+2) / binom(4L,2L)
 = (2L)(2L-1)/((2L+1)(2L+2)) >= 2/5
```

for `L>=2` (equality at `L=2`). Combining the inequalities gives (16). `square`

**[THEOREM 18 — proof of exponential mode growth].** Lemma 4 and Theorem 16 give
`m(2m-1)>=D_L>=C_L`. Since `m(2m-1)<2m^2`, (16) implies

```text
m^2 > C_L/2 >= 2^(4L)/(5(4L+1)),
```

which is (4).

## 7. What the dimension route adds beyond the claw theorem

**[THEOREM 23 — precise comparison].** For `L>=3`, an interior ladder vertex has degree three, so
`proofs/algebraic_obstruction.md` Theorem 4 already forbids mapping the individual local terms
injectively to *single* monomials `i gamma_p gamma_q`: their frustration graph has an induced claw.
That statement remains stronger than the dimension route under its narrow monomial hypothesis,
because it forbids such an assignment even if arbitrarily many Majoranas are supplied.

**[THEOREM 24 — genuine additions].** The dimension route adds three results not supplied by the
claw argument:

1. It permits every local term to be an arbitrary real linear combination of Majorana bilinears,
   not just one bilinear monomial.
2. It gives the quantitative enlarged-space lower bound (3), exponential as in (4).
3. It decides `L=2`, where the frustration graph is claw-free and the claw theorem is silent.

**[UNRESOLVED — other generator sets and spectral questions].** This note concerns the
`5L-2`-generator local-term algebra. It makes no claim about the smaller two-generator algebra
`Lie<A_L,B_L>` with `A_L=sum_v X_v` and `B_L=sum_e Z_uZ_v`; replacing individual terms by sums changes
the Lie algebra. It also does not decide whether one transfer matrix, its spectrum, one parity
sector, or a non-invariant compression can look Gaussian. Those are different hypotheses from
simultaneous quadratic realization of all local terms. The spectral pair-product results in
`proofs/allsize_gaussian.md` are therefore independent, not inputs to this proof.
