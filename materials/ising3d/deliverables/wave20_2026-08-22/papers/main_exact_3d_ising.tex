\begin{abstract}
The three-dimensional nearest-neighbour Ising model is not solved, and nothing below claims to solve
it. This paper consolidates what an exact, machine-audited research program has actually established:
a family of independent no-go theorems that close named routes to solvability, exact
characteristic-zero structure theory for the Lie algebras generated by layer transfer terms, and an
exactly certified computational base of series coefficients and rigorous critical bounds.

The obstructions fall into five logically independent classes. \emph{Local-algebraic}: a closed-form
Dolan--Grady defect that vanishes exactly when every layer vertex has degree $0$ or $2$, and a
tridiagonal (Askey--Wilson) no-go for representative three-dimensional layers.
\emph{Representation-theoretic}: exact characteristic-zero decompositions of the two-generator layer
algebras at $2\times3$, $2\times4$ and $3\times3$, which combined with the Date--Roan classification
of Onsager-algebra quotients show that these algebras are not Onsager quotients in any disguise; and
an exact classification of the local-term algebra for \emph{every connected simple graph}. Connected
bipartite graphs split into path, even-cycle and branching quadratic-root classes, with no
Hamiltonian-path hypothesis; connected non-bipartite graphs split into odd cycles and the full
noncentral even algebra. In particular, every open $2\times L$ ladder is
$\mathfrak{so}_{2^{2L-1}}^{\,2}$ for even $L$ and
$\mathfrak{sp}_{2^{2L-1}}^{\,2}$ for odd $L$, for every $L\ge2$, yielding an unconditional
isotropic all-$L$ local-term free-fermion no-go. \emph{Spectral and basis-independent}: exact
certificates through the open $2\times6$ layer, an all-size anisotropic theorem, and a new
every-coupling isotropic theorem: every finite non-bipartite graph fails the full subset-product
condition for all $0<t<1$, by a strictly positive odd-Eulerian trace invariant. The invariant
vanishes identically on bipartite graphs, so the physical rectangular-grid gap remains explicit.
\emph{Conserved-quantity}: the only finite-support operator on $\Zb^3$ commuting with the mixed
generator is a scalar, and the Pauli-string centralizer of the layer generating set is
$\{I,\prod_i X_i\}$. \emph{Tensor and planarity}: the identical-factor tetrahedron equation has only
the zero auxiliary matrix away from $q\in\{0,\pm1\}$, the positive-real scalar Kac--Ward cone is
empty, and $31$ of $32$ rational Kac--Ward branches are empty over $\Qb$.

The computational base is exact: the simple-cubic reduced free energy is computed to $v^{28}$ at high
temperature and $x^{56}$ at low temperature with per-box Chinese-remainder uniqueness certificates.
A deterministic $41{,}424$-state memory-$12$ walk automaton with an exact positive integer
Collatz certificate proves $\mu<4.747526$, and the critical coupling is therefore certified to lie in
$[0.2138368062108697304302094293879379891333,\,0.2527310098586630030260020266135701299926]$.
Every statement carries an explicit status tag, every universal claim is separated from the finite
computation that motivated it, and the withdrawn claims are listed as prominently as the proved ones.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{What is claimed and what is not}
The two-dimensional Ising model is solvable because several distinct structures coincide: the
transfer generators satisfy the Dolan--Grady relations and generate the Onsager
algebra~\cite{dolangrady1982,davies1990_onsager,davies1991_onsager}, the local terms map to Majorana
bilinears~\cite{schultzmattisl1964}, the model is planar so the Kac--Ward determinant
applies~\cite{kacward1952}, and the spectrum is a free-fermion subset-product
multiset~\cite{onsager1944,kaufman1949}. In three dimensions each of these structures fails, but
``fails'' is a mathematical assertion that must be proved for a precisely stated mechanism, not
asserted from the absence of a known solution. Computational hardness results~\cite{istrail2000} say
that no efficiently computable partition function exists for general three-dimensional instances;
they do not by themselves exclude a closed form for the translation-invariant simple-cubic model.

This paper states and proves the failures, one mechanism at a time, with exact arithmetic and with
scope sentences that are meant to be adversarial against their own conclusions. What is
\emph{not} claimed anywhere: a thermodynamic-limit free energy, magnetisation, correlation function,
spectrum, exact critical coupling, or exact critical exponent. No result below excludes an exact
solution based on a Gaussian restriction of a larger system, non-Pauli or quasilocal conserved
quantities, matrix-valued or dynamical tensor ansatzes, nonlocal transformations, or a mechanism not
yet formulated.

\subsection{Status tags}
Every mathematical statement in the underlying repository, and every statement here, carries one of:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{[THEOREM]} --- proved in the stated scope, which is always finite, algebraic, or
      explicitly universal with a proved induction.
\item \textbf{[LEMMA]} --- proved auxiliary statement.
\item \textbf{[COMPUTATION]} --- an exact finite calculation with a stored, independently replayable
      artifact. A finite computation is never promoted to an all-sizes theorem without a proved
      induction.
\item \textbf{[EXTERNAL]} --- established elsewhere and cited, used as input or as a
      post-computation witness, never as a fitting target.
\item \textbf{[UNRESOLVED]} --- honestly open, including cases where a resource wall was measured.
\end{itemize}
A measured timeout is never a theorem. The benchmark value $K_c=0.221654626$~\cite{ferrenberg2018}
is used for comparison only; it is never used to fit, select, tune, or validate a derivation.

\section{Setup and conventions}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Model and transfer representation}
The model is the ferromagnetic nearest-neighbour Ising model on the simple cubic lattice,
$H=-J\sum_{\langle xy\rangle}\sigma_x\sigma_y$ with $\sigma_x\in\{\pm1\}$, $K=\beta J$,
$v=\tanh K$, $x=e^{-2K}$ and $u=x^2$. The reduced free energy is normalised as in the repository
specification: at high temperature $\phi(v)=\log2+\sum_n f_n v^n$, and at low temperature
$\phi(K)=3K+\sum_{n\ge1}c_nx^n$.

Transfer is taken along one axis, so that each layer is a two-dimensional graph $\Gamma=(V,E)$ and
the layer-to-layer operator is, up to a positive scalar,
\begin{equation}
V_\Gamma=e^{K^*A}e^{KB},\qquad
A=\sum_{v\in V}X_v,\qquad
B=\sum_{(ij)\in E}Z_iZ_j,
\label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
with $X,Y,Z$ the Pauli matrices on $(\Cb^2)^{\otimes|V|}$ and $K^*$ the dual coupling defined by
$e^{-2K^*}=\tanh K$~\cite{schultzmattisl1964}. Writing $t=\tanh(K^*/2)$ and $q=(1+t^2)/(2t)=e^{2K}$
makes $V_\Gamma$ an exact rational matrix at rational $t$, which is what allows every spectral
statement below to be made with no floating point.

The single structural feature that distinguishes three dimensions is elementary and decisive: a
one-dimensional layer graph has $\Delta(\Gamma)\le2$, whereas a genuine cubic layer contains a vertex
of degree at least three. Several theorems below are exactly the statement that some solvability
mechanism is equivalent to $\Delta(\Gamma)\le2$.

\subsection{Two different algebras}\label{sec:twoalgebras}
Two Lie algebras appear throughout and must not be confused.
\begin{definition}
The \emph{two-generator algebra} is $\langle A,B\rangle_{\rm Lie}$, generated by the two sums in
\eqref{eq:transfer}. The \emph{local-term algebra} is
$\alg_\Gamma=\langle X_v\ (v\in V),\ Z_iZ_j\ ((ij)\in E)\rangle_{\rm Lie}$, generated by the
individual terms.
\end{definition}
For the open $2\times3$ layer the two-generator algebra has dimension $263$ and the local-term
algebra has dimension $1056$; at $2\times4$ the dimensions are $2952$ and $16256$. Statements about
one are not statements about the other, and modular witnesses computed for one have been
mis-attributed to the other in earlier drafts of this program. Both algebras are relevant, for
different mechanisms: the two-generator algebra is the object of the Onsager/Dolan--Grady route,
while the local-term algebra is the object of term-wise free-fermion
routes~\cite{chapman2020,elman2021,chapman2023}.

\subsection{Exact-arithmetic protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
Three conventions carry the weight of every numerical claim below.

\paragraph{Two primes, one direction.}
Ranks and greatest-common-divisor degrees computed modulo a prime bound the characteristic-zero
quantity in exactly one direction: a modular rank is a lower bound for the rational rank, and a
modular $\gcd$ degree of a monic-normalised pair is an upper bound for the characteristic-zero
$\gcd$ degree. Agreement at two primes is a cross-check, never a proof of rational equality.
Substituting a bound into the wrong side is the single error class that has cost this program a
theorem, and one withdrawn result (\S\ref{sec:corrections}) is exactly that mistake.

\paragraph{Monic specialization.}
\begin{lemma}[Monic-in-parameter upgrade]\label{lem:monic}
Let $F(z;\tau)\in\Zb[\tau][z]$ be monic in $z$, and let $G=\gcd(F,\partial_zF)$ computed over the
fraction field. Then for every specialization $\tau\mapsto\tau_0$, $\deg\gcd(F(\cdot;\tau_0),
\partial_zF(\cdot;\tau_0))\ \ge\ \deg G$. Consequently one evaluated point bounds the generic value,
and the resultant $\rho=\mathrm{Res}_z(F/G,\partial_zF/G)$ confines the exceptional parameters to the
finite zero set of a single nonzero polynomial.
\end{lemma}
This lemma is what converts finite point certificates into coupling-generic theorems, and it is
applied only in the direction it permits.

\paragraph{Verification independence.}
Every deliverable consists of a producer script, a standalone verifier that re-derives the decisive
quantity from raw inputs without importing the producer's conclusions, an artifact with content
digests, and a tagged note. Cross-artifact references digest the semantic \texttt{data} section with
sorted keys rather than whole-file bytes, because timestamp refreshes otherwise break dependent
tests. The full acceptance gate is $123$ standalone verifiers, all passing.

\section{Local-algebraic obstructions}

\subsection{The Dolan--Grady defect}\label{sec:dg}
The Dolan--Grady relations
\begin{equation}
[A,[A,[A,B]]]=16[A,B],\qquad [B,[B,[B,A]]]=16[B,A]
\label{eq:dg}
\end{equation}
generate the Onsager algebra and are the algebraic core of the two-dimensional
solution~\cite{dolangrady1982,davies1990_onsager}.

\begin{theorem}[Dolan--Grady defect; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:dg}
For every finite simple graph $\Gamma$, with $d(v)=\deg(v)$ and neighbourhood $N(v)$, the first
relation in \eqref{eq:dg} holds identically, and the second has the exact residual
\begin{equation}
R(\Gamma)=[B,[B,[B,A]]]-16[B,A]
=24i\sum_{v\in V}Y_v\Bigl((d(v)-2)\sum_{u\sim v}Z_u
+2\!\!\sum_{\{u,u',u''\}\subseteq N(v)}\!\!Z_uZ_{u'}Z_{u''}\Bigr).
\label{eq:defect}
\end{equation}
Hence $R(\Gamma)=0$ if and only if $d(v)\in\{0,2\}$ for every $v$; the quartic part of $R(\Gamma)$
vanishes if and only if $\Delta(\Gamma)\le2$; and the number of four-body Pauli terms is exactly
$\sum_v\binom{d(v)}{3}$, the number of induced claws in the frustration graph.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Expand the nested commutators term by term in the Pauli basis. Only edges incident to a selected
vertex $v$ contribute, because $[Z_iZ_j,X_v]=0$ unless $v\in\{i,j\}$, and every surviving term
carries the factor $Y_v$ at that single vertex. Distinct vertices therefore cannot cancel one
another, and the coefficient bookkeeping at a fixed $v$ gives the degree-linear term
$(d(v)-2)\sum_{u\sim v}Z_u$ and the neighbour-triple term. Both coefficients are integers, so
$R(\Gamma)=0$ forces every local coefficient to vanish, which for $d(v)\ge3$ is impossible because
the triple sum is nonempty and its Pauli strings are linearly independent of the linear ones.
\end{proof}

Two points deserve emphasis. First, the isolated-vertex case matters: the frequently stated
equivalence ``Dolan--Grady holds iff $\Gamma$ is $2$-regular'' is false, since $d(v)=0$ also
annihilates the defect; a triangle plus an isolated vertex is the decisive counterexample. Second,
the quartic operator $Y_vZ_{u_1}Z_{u_2}Z_{u_3}$ produced by a degree-three vertex is the same
operator that obstructs the tridiagonal family in \S\ref{sec:td}, which is why those two no-gos,
though logically independent, always fail together.

\begin{corollary}[{[COMPUTATION]}]
The identity \eqref{eq:defect} was verified term by term, with exact integer coefficients, on sixteen
graph families including paths, cycles, stars, complete graphs, trees, square grids, a torus layer,
and a cubic graph.
\end{corollary}

Simultaneous deformation was also tested and closed: within the invariant supports $F_1$--$F_4$,
no simultaneous nonlinear deformation of both generators restores \eqref{eq:dg}, and the finite $F_3$
deformation that cancels the second relation on the $3\times3$ torus provably fails the first. The
two varieties are simultaneously empty for the minimal orbit basis, with Gr\"obner basis
$\{p^2,pq,q^2,pr,qr,r^2\}$ and all saturations trivial.

\subsection{Tridiagonal and Askey--Wilson relations}\label{sec:td}
The tridiagonal condition, the structure behind the Onsager algebra and the Askey--Wilson
relations, is tested through
\[
T_0=\beta T_1+\gamma^*T_2+\rho^*T_3,\qquad
(T_0,T_1,T_2,T_3)=\bigl([B,B^2A+AB^2],[B,BAB],[B,BA+AB],[B,A]\bigr),
\]
which is linear in the three unknown parameters at fixed $A,B$.

\begin{theorem}[Representative tridiagonal no-go; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:td}
For the boundaryless torus layers $3\times3$, $3\times4$, $4\times4$, $3\times5$, $4\times5$,
$5\times5$, and for the open $2\times3$ and $3\times3$ layers, the second tridiagonal relation has no
solution over $\Qb$, hence none over $\Cb$. The conclusion is invariant under every affine
redefinition $A'=aA+s$, $B'=bB+t$ with $ab\ne0$.
\end{theorem}

For the $4\times4$ torus a single Pauli coefficient is a complete certificate:
\[
W=Z_0Y_5Z_6Z_{10},\qquad [T_0]_W=-48,\qquad [T_1]_W=[T_2]_W=[T_3]_W=0 .
\]
No universal one-row witness exists; some geometries require the stored four-row certificate, and
claiming otherwise was an early over-generalisation in this program.

\subsection{Term-wise Majorana bilinearity and the claw}\label{sec:claw}
The frustration graph of the standard generating set is the subdivision $S(\Gamma)$: each $X_i$
anticommutes exactly with the bond terms incident to $i$, while the $X$ terms commute among
themselves and the bond terms commute among themselves.

\begin{theorem}[Claw obstruction; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:claw}
If a set of Hermitian Pauli terms is mapped injectively, after a unitary change of basis, to Majorana
bilinears $i\gamma_p\gamma_q$, then its frustration graph contains no induced $K_{1,3}$. Hence the
standard layer generating set admits a term-wise Majorana-bilinear representation only if
$\Delta(\Gamma)\le2$. Every square-grid layer with a vertex of degree three or four fails.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Two bilinears anticommute exactly when their two-element index sets meet in one element. Suppose a
bilinear $b_0$ has three pairwise commuting neighbours $b_1,b_2,b_3$ in the frustration graph. Each
$b_k$ shares exactly one index with $b_0$, and $b_0$ has only two indices, so two of the $b_k$ share
the same index with $b_0$; but then those two share an index with each other, so they anticommute,
contradicting that they commute. Hence no induced claw.
\end{proof}

This is the graph-invariant criterion of the free-fermion-solvability
literature~\cite{chapman2020,elman2021,chapman2023}, specialised to the Ising layer; note that
free-fermion solvability can survive in ``disguised'' form for other generating
sets~\cite{fendley2019}, which is exactly why the spectral tests of \S\ref{sec:spectral} are needed.

A quantitative companion covers enlarged Gaussian realizations.

\begin{proposition}[Mode-count bound; {[THEOREM]}]\label{prop:modes}
If the generated real Lie algebra $\alg$ embeds in the span of bilinears of $m$ fermionic modes,
i.e.\ $2m$ Majorana operators, then
\[
\dim\alg\le\dim\mathfrak{so}(2m)=m(2m-1),
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
m\ \ge\ \frac{1+\sqrt{1+8\dim\alg}}{4}.
\]
\end{proposition}
On the physical Hilbert space of a layer with $n$ sites one has $m=n$, so any exact lower bound on
$\dim\alg$ exceeding $n(2n-1)$ excludes a same-space Gaussian realization outright. This is a
dimension obstruction; it does not exclude an embedding into a strictly larger space.

\subsection{Conserved quantities}\label{sec:lc}
\begin{theorem}[Pauli centralizer; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:flux}
Let $\Gamma$ be connected and $G=\{X_i:i\in V\}\cup\{Z_iZ_j:(ij)\in E\}$. The Pauli strings
commuting with every element of $G$ are exactly $\{I,\ \prod_{i\in V}X_i\}$, and the same holds for
the summed pair $\{A,B\}$ within the Pauli group.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Encode a string as $(a\mid b)\in\Fb_2^{2n}$. Commuting with every $X_i$ forces $b=0$; commuting with
every bond forces $a_i=a_j$ along each edge. Connectedness leaves $a=0$ or $a=(1,\dots,1)$.
\end{proof}

This closes the Kitaev-style family of same-space Pauli fluxes, and only that family: non-Pauli
symmetries, enlarged-space constructions, and ordinary lattice translation sectors are outside its
scope, and translation on a triangle is an explicit counterexample to the stronger statement that was
once claimed here.

The genuinely all-size statement is about finite-support operators on the infinite lattice.

\begin{theorem}[Local commutant; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:lc}
Fix $a,b$ with $ab\ne0$ and let $H_g=a\sum_xX_x+b\sum_{\langle xy\rangle}Z_xZ_y$ on $\Zb^3$. In the
finite-commutator-derivation sense, the only compact finite-support operator commuting with $H_g$ is
a scalar. The simultaneous $A$/$B$ centralizer is a corollary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Take a support site $v$ with maximal first coordinate and its private outside neighbour $w=v+e_1$.
Matching the $Z_w$ slot of the commutator forces $b\,[Q,Z_v]=0$-type structure; matching the $Y_v$
slot then forces $2ia\,Q_Z=0$; support induction removes the site and repeats. Both generator
coefficients are used, which is why $a=0$ or $b=0$ is excluded.
\end{proof}

Explicitly outside scope: extensive translation sums of local densities, quasilocal or
infinite-support charges, and the finite-torus operator $\prod_xX_x$. The extensive class was treated
separately: for translation-covariant $Q=\sum_x\tau_x(q)$ with finite density $q$, the derivation
vanishes iff the local commutator is a lattice divergence, so the nontrivial quotient dimension is
$\operatorname{rank}S_R-\operatorname{rank}M_R-1$. The one-dimensional chain gives exactly $2R$ for
$R=0,\dots,9$, with the oriented energy current already at $R=1$, while $\Zb^2$ at $R=1,2$ and
$\Zb^3$ at $R=1$ give exactly $1$: only the Hamiltonian density survives. In the $\Zb^3$ radius-two
box the support-size class $s\le5$ is decided exactly --- $21{,}121{,}156$ columns,
$\operatorname{rank}S=14{,}757{,}412$ and $\operatorname{rank}M=14{,}757{,}410$ at both primes
$2147483647$ and $2147483629$, meeting the analytic upper bound $\operatorname{rank}S-2$, so the
quotient is exactly $1$. The general absence of local conserved quantities in $d\ge2$ for this model
class is external~\cite{chiba2024}; the finite-shape reconstruction here is an independent exact
computation, not a rival proof.

\section{Exact characteristic-zero structure of layer algebras}\label{sec:char0}

\subsection{The two-generator algebras and the Onsager-quotient no-go}
Failure of Dolan--Grady for the displayed generators does not preclude the generated algebra from
being an Onsager-algebra quotient in some disguise. That loophole is closed by computing the algebra
exactly and comparing with the classification of Onsager
quotients~\cite{date_roan2000_onsager,roan1991_onsager}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact char-$0$ structures; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:char0}
Over $\Qb$, for the two-generator algebras of the open layers,
\begin{align*}
2\times3:\quad &\dim=263=1+\bigl[C_7+C_3+C_3+A_8+A_5\bigr],\quad\text{Killing rank }262,\\
2\times4:\quad &\dim=2952=\Qb z+\mathfrak{so}(F_{000})+\mathfrak{so}(F_{010})+\mathfrak{so}(F_{100})
+\mathfrak{sl}_{24}+\mathfrak{sl}_{28}+2\,\mathfrak{sl}_4,\\
&\text{absolute type }D_{21}+2B_{13}+A_{23}+A_{27}+2A_3,\quad\text{radical}=\text{centre},\\
3\times3:\quad &\dim=8034=\Qb z+\mathfrak{sl}_{33}+\mathfrak{sl}_{48}+\mathfrak{sl}_{59}
+\mathfrak{sl}_3+\mathfrak{sl}_{16}+\mathfrak{sl}_{30},\quad\text{type }A_{32}+A_{47}+A_{58}+A_2+A_{15}+A_{29}.
\end{align*}
At $2\times3$ the $C_7$-versus-$B_7$ ambiguity is resolved by an exact invariant alternating form of
determinant $16384$. Each decomposition was obtained by two independent exact-$\Qb$ closures, and a
clean-room verifier that imports nothing from the producer re-derives the controlling minors.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Not an Onsager quotient; {[THEOREM]} + {[EXTERNAL]}]\label{cor:oa}
The Levi subalgebra of any finite-dimensional quotient of the Onsager algebra is a sum of copies of
$\mathfrak{sl}_2$~\cite{date_roan2000_onsager}. None of the three algebras above has that Levi type.
Hence the $2\times3$, $2\times4$ and $3\times3$ layer algebras are not Onsager-algebra quotients in
any disguise: no generating pair of them satisfies Dolan--Grady.
\end{corollary}

The corollary is stated for the three named layers, because it rests on exact computations for those
layers. For the open $4\times4$ layer only $\dim_\Qb\alg\ge1794$ is proved, by $1794$ stored literal
Pauli words; its exact dimension, type, centre, radical and Killing rank are open, blocked by a
$268591168$-dimensional container.

\subsection{The $2\times L$ alternation family}\label{sec:altern}
The local-term algebras of open ladders form the most structured family found so far. Let $n=2L$,
let $J=(1,\dots,1)\in\Fb_2^n$, let $d$ be one checkerboard class of the ladder, and set
\begin{equation}
S_L=\bigl\{(x\mid z)\ :\ |z|\text{ even},\ z\ne J,\ (J-z)\cdot x=1+z\cdot d\bigr\},
\label{eq:setlaw}
\end{equation}
together with the full fibre $\{(x\mid J):x\in\Fb_2^n\}$ when $L$ is odd.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform forward closure; {[LEMMA]}]\label{lem:closure}
For every $L\ge2$ the set $S_L$ is closed under every nonzero adjoint action of an $X_i$ or a ladder
bond $Z_uZ_v$. Hence $\alg_{2\times L}\subseteq\operatorname{span}_\Qb(S_L)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Three cases. An $X_i$ action preserves the affine fibre defining \eqref{eq:setlaw}. A bond action
preserves the law because every ladder bond crosses the checkerboard class. The exceptional case
$z+z_g=J$ either has vanishing commutator, when $L$ is even, or lands in the full $z=J$ fibre, when
$L$ is odd.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All-$L$ containment; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:containment}
For every $L\ge2$, with $n=2L$, and with respect to the signed checkerboard form $\Omega_L$ on the
two $\prod_iX_i$ parity halves,
\[
\alg_{2\times L}\subseteq
\begin{cases}
\mathfrak{so}_{2^{n-1}}(\Qb)\oplus\mathfrak{so}_{2^{n-1}}(\Qb), & L\text{ even},\\[2pt]
\mathfrak{sp}_{2^{n-1}}(\Qb)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{2^{n-1}}(\Qb), & L\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
\qquad
\dim\alg_{2\times L}\le 2^{n-1}\bigl(2^{n-1}-(-1)^L\bigr).
\]
The alternation is forced by the identity $\Omega_L^{T}=(-1)^L\Omega_L$, and membership in the form
is the same parity equation as the set law, $|x|\equiv1+z\cdot x+z\cdot d \pmod 2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[All-$L$ classification; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:altern}
Equality holds in Theorem~\ref{thm:containment} for \emph{every} $L\ge2$. Writing $m=2^{n-1}$ with
$n=2L$,
\[
\alg_{2\times L}(\Qb)=
\begin{cases}
\mathfrak{so}_m(\Qb)\oplus\mathfrak{so}_m(\Qb), & L\text{ even},\\[2pt]
\mathfrak{sp}_m(\Qb)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_m(\Qb), & L\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
\qquad
\dim_\Qb\alg_{2\times L}=2^{n-1}\bigl(2^{n-1}-(-1)^L\bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The reverse inclusion is proved by an explicit production system on Pauli labels. Encode a monomial
as $(a\mid b)\in\Fb_2^{2n}$ with symplectic pairing $\langle(a\mid b),(c\mid e)\rangle=a\cdot e+
b\cdot c$; the generators are $(e_v\mid0)$ for sites and $(0\mid e_u+e_v)$ for bonds, and the single
production rule is that $p,q$ reached with $\langle p,q\rangle=1$ implies $p+q$ reached, which is
exactly the statement that a nonvanishing Pauli bracket lies in the algebra. The induction carries an
explicit invariant $I(L)$ and uses two independent moves: a length-two route across one square, which
yields all four pure-$X$ three-corner labels, and a square-triple second move. A transport lemma
shows a complete pair fibre moves along a two-token edge with no rank loss, the bond filter
contributing $D_{\rm source}\cap\ker(h)$ while the site term at the newly added endpoint supplies the
missing direction; a leaf induction establishes token-graph connectivity including the boundary
columns $c=1$ and $c=L$; complete pair fibres then generate every odd pure-$X$ label and every
higher even-$z$ fibre. This is an all-size argument, not an extrapolation.
\end{proof}

The exact types and dimensions at small $L$ are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|r|l}
L & n & \dim\alg_{2\times L} & \text{exact type over }\Qb\\ \hline
2 & 4 & 56 & \mathfrak{so}_8\oplus\mathfrak{so}_8\\
3 & 6 & 1056 & \mathfrak{sp}_{32}\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{32}\\
4 & 8 & 16256 & \mathfrak{so}_{128}\oplus\mathfrak{so}_{128}\\
5 & 10 & 262656 & \mathfrak{sp}_{512}\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{512}\\
6 & 12 & 4192256 & \mathfrak{so}_{2048}\oplus\mathfrak{so}_{2048}
\end{array}
\]
Each algebra is perfect and splits into two ideals selected by the $\prod_iX_i$ parity halves; at
$L=3$ the diagonal Killing signature is $(544,512)$ with $528$-dimensional ideals, and at $L=4$ the
signature is $(8192,8064)$. Independent confirmation: exact integer breadth-first closure enumerates
$262656$ labels at $L=5$ and $4192256$ at $L=6$ with zero set-law violations, the latter in
$18.6$ process seconds at $458$ MB peak resident size, and the production/rank construction covers all
$8191$ ordinary even-$z$ support rows at $L=7$, giving $67117056$. The $L=7$ figure is \emph{not} a
full enumeration and is labelled as such in the artifact.

\begin{remark}[Structural cause of the alternation]
The $\mathfrak{so}$/$\mathfrak{sp}$ alternation is not an accident of the checkerboard bookkeeping. A
second, independent route makes it classical. Order the $n$ sites along the perimeter Hamiltonian path
and apply Jordan--Wigner, introducing $2n$ Majoranas. Every site term and every path bond becomes a
Majorana bilinear, and consecutive bilinears generate all of $\mathfrak{so}(2n)$; each remaining rung
becomes a single Clifford monomial of grade $2(q-p)$, which bipartiteness forces to be
$\equiv2\pmod4$. Since a grade-$k$ monomial reverses with sign $(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}$, which is $-1$
exactly for $k\equiv2\pmod4$, the span of that grade class is the $(-1)$-eigenspace of Clifford
reversal, i.e.\ the algebra preserving the spinor bilinear form on each half-spin space --- symmetric
when $n\equiv0\pmod4$ and alternating when $n\equiv2\pmod4$. With $n=2L$ that is exactly $L$ even
versus $L$ odd, and the dimension count agrees identically:
\[
\sum_{\substack{0\le k\le 2n\\ k\equiv2\ (4)}}\binom{2n}{k}
\;=\;2^{n-1}\bigl(2^{n-1}-(-1)^{L}\bigr),\qquad n=2L .
\]
\end{remark}

Theorem~\ref{thm:altern} combined with Proposition~\ref{prop:modes} gives an unconditional all-$L$
obstruction, with no genericity and at the physical isotropic point.

\begin{corollary}[All-$L$ quadratic no-go; {[THEOREM]}]\label{cor:alllquad}
For every $L\ge2$ the $2L$ site terms and $3L-2$ bond terms of the open $2\times L$ layer cannot all
be Majorana-quadratic, in any basis of the physical $2^{n}$-dimensional space: already
$\dim\alg_{2\times L}\ge n(2n-1)+1>\dim\mathfrak{so}(2n)$. Quantitatively, any exact quadratic
realization on an invariant code needs
$m\ge\lceil(1+\sqrt{1+8D_L})/4\rceil$ modes, and for $L\ge3$
$m>2^{2L}/\sqrt{5(4L+1)}$, so the mode count grows exponentially in $L$; the exact conservative
floors at $L=2,\dots,8$ are $6,18,65,216,991,3168,15354$.
\end{corollary}

The $L=2$ case is worth isolating, because the claw obstruction of Theorem~\ref{thm:claw} does not
apply there: the $2\times2$ ladder is a $4$-cycle with maximum degree two, yet
$\dim\alg_{2\times2}=56>28=\dim\mathfrak{so}(8)$, forcing $m\ge6$. Two scope limits are explicit: the
enlarged-space statement assumes exact quadratic intertwining on an invariant code, so a
non-invariant compression is not covered, and this is a statement about the algebra generated by the
local terms, not about the spectrum of a single transfer matrix. The corresponding two-generator
bounds are separate: $\dim\langle A_L,B_L\rangle\ge K_L-W_L$ with $K_L=(2^{2L-1}+3\cdot2^{L})/4$ and
$W_L=0,2,10,66,364,1822$ for $L=3,\dots,8$, giving $6562$ against the ceiling $2L(4L-1)=496$ at
$L=8$, and no all-$L$ law for $W_L$ is yet proved.

\subsection{A general trichotomy: paths, cycles, and branching}\label{sec:trichotomy}
The Clifford-grade mechanism behind Theorem~\ref{thm:altern} is not specific to ladders, and pushing
it to general graphs produces the sharpest structural statement in this paper. It also produces an
exact refutation of the naive generalisation, which is recorded first because it is what fixes the
hypotheses.

Let $\Gamma$ be a finite simple bipartite graph on $n$ vertices possessing a Hamiltonian path, let
$N=2n$, and let $\alg_\Gamma$ be the local-term algebra acting on the full $2^n$-dimensional space.
Number the path positions $0,\dots,n-1$ and Jordan--Wigner in that order. A chord joining positions
$p<q$ becomes a single Clifford monomial of grade $2(q-p)$, and bipartiteness forces $q-p$ odd.

\begin{lemma}[Branching equivalence; {[LEMMA]}]\label{lem:branch}
For a graph carrying a Hamiltonian path the following are equivalent: some chord is not the pair of
path endpoints; some chord has grade $k\le N-4$; and $\Delta(\Gamma)\ge3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every interior path vertex already has path degree two, so a chord other than the endpoint pair meets
an interior vertex and raises its degree to at least three. Conversely if $\Delta\le2$ no chord meets
an interior vertex, so $\Gamma$ is the path or the path plus its endpoint edge, i.e.\ a cycle. The
endpoint edge has path distance $n-1$, grade $N-2$; every other chord has distance at most $n-2$ and
grade at most $N-4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact trichotomy; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:trichotomy}
With $\Gamma$, $n$, $N$ as above, exactly one of the following holds.
\[
\begin{array}{l|l|r}
\text{branch} & \text{generated Clifford grades} & \dim_\Qb\alg_\Gamma\\ \hline
\Gamma\text{ is the path} & \{2\} & \binom N2=n(2n-1)\\
\Gamma\text{ is an even cycle} & \{2,\,N-2\} & 2\binom N2=2n(2n-1)\\
\Delta(\Gamma)\ge3 & \text{every }k\equiv2\!\!\pmod 4,\ k\ne N &
\begin{cases}2^{2n-2}-(-1)^{n/2}2^{n-1}, & n\text{ even}\\ 2^{2n-2}-1, & n\text{ odd}\end{cases}
\end{array}
\]
\end{theorem}

Three features of this statement deserve emphasis.

\paragraph{The naive version is false, and the counterexample is instructive.}
``Bipartite, Hamiltonian path, at least one chord'' does \emph{not} imply the exponential branch,
because it fails to exclude the cycle. The hexagon $C_6$ is the minimal witness: its single chord is
the endpoint pair, of grade $N-2=10$, and two grade-$10$ monomials in $12$ Majoranas must share at
least eight indices, of which only the odd overlap $9$ survives in a commutator, giving grade $2$
only. The grade set is $\{2,10\}$ and $\dim\alg_{C_6}=132=2\binom{12}{2}$, not the $1056$ that the
naive statement would predict. $C_8$ gives $240$. The $2\times2$ ladder is $C_4$ and therefore lies in
the cycle branch, but at $N=8$ the grades $\{2,6\}$ happen to exhaust the whole class
$k\equiv2\pmod4$, so its dimension $56$ coincides with the branching formula and hid the missing
hypothesis.

\paragraph{In the branching branch the answer depends only on $n$.}
Neither the number of chords nor their positions enter. The open $2\times5$ ladder and the decagon
with one non-endpoint chord both have $n=10$ and both give exactly $262656$; the $3\times3$ grid and
the $3\times3$ grid minus an edge both give $65535$. So the local-term algebra sees only the vertex
count and the presence of branching. For odd $n$ the volume element is the global $X$ parity operator
rather than a scalar; it is a nonidentity Pauli string that is not generated, which is the exact
origin of the $-1$.

\paragraph{The trichotomy is the solvability dichotomy, made quantitative.}
The two low branches are precisely the free-fermion-solvable geometries: the open chain and the ring,
i.e.\ the one-dimensional model and the two-dimensional Ising layer. Their algebras are
\emph{quadratic} in $n$ and sit comfortably inside the Gaussian bound of
Proposition~\ref{prop:modes}, requiring only a linear number of modes. The branching branch is
exponential and forces $\Theta(2^n)$ modes. So the boundary between solvable and unsolvable layer
geometry appears as a dimension jump from $\Theta(n^2)$ to $\Theta(4^{n})$, occurring exactly at
$\Delta(\Gamma)\ge3$ --- the same threshold that appears in the Dolan--Grady defect
(Theorem~\ref{thm:dg}), the claw obstruction (Theorem~\ref{thm:claw}), and the tridiagonal no-go
(Theorem~\ref{thm:td}). Four independent mechanisms, one threshold.

\begin{theorem}[Non-bipartite dichotomy; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:nonbip}
Let $\Gamma$ be connected, simple and \emph{not} bipartite, on $n$ vertices. If $\Gamma$ is an odd
cycle then its grades are $\{2,2n-2\}$ and $\dim\alg_\Gamma=2n(2n-1)$. Otherwise, equivalently when
$\Delta(\Gamma)\ge3$, $\alg_\Gamma$ is the full noncentral even Clifford algebra, of dimension
$2^{2n-1}-2$. Hamiltonicity is not needed: the proof uses an odd-cycle-plus-leaf seed, a
full-even leaf-extension lemma, and a spanning construction.
\end{theorem}

An odd chord distance contributes a grade $\equiv0\pmod4$, which is what enlarges the closure from the
$k\equiv2\pmod4$ class to all even grades. Raw verifications: $C_5$ gives $90$, the paw $126$, $C_5$
with a chord $510$, the non-Hamiltonian triangle with two leaves $510$, and $C_6$ with a short chord
$2046$. Every simple-cubic layer graph is bipartite, so the physical case is
Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy}; Theorem~\ref{thm:nonbip} shows the method is not fragile, only
parity-sensitive.

\begin{theorem}[Complete bipartite branching branch; {[THEOREM]}/{[EXTERNAL]}]\label{thm:bipcomplete}
Let $\Gamma$ be any finite connected simple bipartite graph with colour-class sizes $r,s$ and
$\Delta(\Gamma)\ge3$. Writing Pauli labels as $(a,b)\in\Fb_2^n\times\Fb_2^n$, its local-term
algebra has basis the nonradical part of
\[
\{(a,b): |b|\equiv0,\ Q_c(a,b)=a\cdot b+|a|+c\cdot b=1\},
\]
where $c$ is either bipartition indicator. Consequently
\[
\dim\alg_\Gamma=
\begin{cases}
2^{2n-2}-1,&n\text{ odd},\\
2^{2n-2}-(-1)^r2^{n-1},&n\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
No Hamiltonian-path hypothesis is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Choose a branch-preserving spanning tree. Its $n$ fields and $n-1$ tree bonds form a basis of the
label space $H=\{(a,b):|b|\text{ even}\}$, and their commutation graph is the subdivision of the
tree. A branch vertex supplies an induced $E_6$. Nonzero Pauli commutation by a raw root is exactly
the symplectic transvection on labels. Seven's orbit theorem~\cite{seven2003_transvections} therefore
identifies the generator orbit with the complete nonradical $Q_c=1$ level. Extra edges cannot enlarge
the algebra beyond the quadratic container. Counting the $a$-fibres gives the displayed formula.
\end{proof}

Thus the grade-compatible-order obstruction was a limitation of the Hamiltonian proof, not of the
algebra. In particular $\dim\alg_{K_{1,3}}=72$, and a six-vertex tree has dimension $992$ or $1056$
according to colour-class parity. The complete connected-bipartite classification is now path,
even cycle, and Theorem~\ref{thm:bipcomplete}.

\begin{corollary}[Every finite simple-cubic box; {[THEOREM]}]\label{cor:box}
An explicit alternating-layer snake shows that every finite open $a\times b\times c$ box has a
Hamiltonian path, and every genuine box with $a,b,c\ge2$ has $\Delta\ge3$. Hence the branching
formula of Theorem~\ref{thm:bipcomplete} gives the exact local-term algebra of every finite
three-dimensional box. Instances: the $2\times6$ and $3\times4$ layers both give $4192256$, the
$3\times5$ layer $268435455$, and the open $4\times4$ layer $1073709056$ --- the last being a
local-term dimension, not to be confused with the separate two-generator algebra of the $4\times4$
layer, for which only $\dim\ge1794$ is known.
\end{corollary}

Scope, stated plainly: Theorems~\ref{thm:trichotomy}, \ref{thm:bipcomplete} and
\ref{thm:nonbip}, and Corollary~\ref{cor:box}, concern the algebra generated by local terms over
$\Qb$ on a finite graph. They are not statements about the spectrum of any transfer matrix, about the two-generator
algebra, about the thermodynamic limit, or about solvability of the three-dimensional model; the
spectral obstructions of \S\ref{sec:spectral} are the complementary half of that question.

\subsection{The two-generator algebra behaves differently}\label{sec:twogen}
The transfer operator is built from the two \emph{sums} $A$ and $B$, not from the individual terms,
so the physically decisive object is $\langle A,B\rangle_{\rm Lie}$, and it does \emph{not} inherit
the trichotomy. Certified exact dimensions, against the quadratic ceiling $n(2n-1)$:
\[
\begin{array}{l|c|r|r}
\text{layer} & n & \dim\langle A,B\rangle & n(2n-1)\\ \hline
2\times2 & 4 & 11 & 28\\
2\times3 & 6 & 263 & 66\\
2\times4 & 8 & 2952 & 120\\
3\times3 & 9 & 8034 & 153
\end{array}
\]
The $2\times2$ row already shows the dimension test can be inconclusive: $11<28$, so for the
four-cycle this route says nothing, even though the local-term route excludes it
($56>28$). More importantly, branching is \emph{not} sufficient here.


Collective-spin Schur--Weyl reduction goes two sizes farther:
\[
\dim\langle A,B\rangle_{K_{4,4}}=137>120,\qquad
\dim\langle A,B\rangle_{K_{4,5}}=471>153.
\]
The equalities squeeze finite-field word ranks between independently derived characteristic-zero
sector containers. For opposite-parity parts an all-size container has dimension at most
$1+S_mS_n/4-\lceil m/2\rceil\lceil n/2\rceil$, with
$S_j=\binom{j+3}{3}-[\,2\mid j\,]$. Its largest block already clears the quadratic ceiling in the
audited symbolic range, so complete-bipartite symmetry does not furnish an eventual below-ceiling
counterfamily. No universal threshold is proved.
\begin{theorem}[Branching does not imply large; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:kcounter}
There are bipartite branching graphs with a Hamiltonian path whose two-generator algebra falls below
the quadratic ceiling: $\dim_\Qb\langle A,B\rangle=44<45$ for $K_{2,3}$ and $63<66$ for $K_{3,3}$.
For $K_{3,3}$ the cause is symmetry: the automorphism-fixed local-term container has dimension $64$,
already below the ceiling $66$, so no generating pair of sums can exceed it.
\end{theorem}

A complete fixed-path census sharpens the picture: at $n=4$ there is no branching case; at $n=5$
two of three branching graphs clear the ceiling, $K_{2,3}$ being the sole exception; at $n=6$
thirteen of fourteen clear, $K_{3,3}$ being the sole exception; and at $n=7$ all $63$ clear. That last
row is a finite computation, not an induction, so no universal threshold $n_0$ is claimed --- only
that if one exists then $n_0\ge7$. What \emph{is} proved for the physical family: every open
$2\times L$ ladder with $L\ge3$ clears its ceiling, and among non-ladder grids $3\times2$,
$3\times3$ and $3\times4$ clear theirs.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-basis transfer corollary; {[THEOREM]}]
Whenever $\dim\langle A,B\rangle_{\rm Lie}>n(2n-1)$, the pair $A,B$ cannot be simultaneously
Majorana-quadratic in one fixed basis of the physical space, so the layer transfer operator is not a
free-fermion transfer matrix in the standard sense. This does \emph{not} exclude a Gaussian spectrum
of a single transfer product arising by an unrelated or coupling-dependent coincidence; see the
remark on varying conjugators in \S\ref{sec:allsize}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[A refuted method, recorded; {[THEOREM]}]
Since $A$ is a sum of bilinears, $\mathrm{ad}_{iA}$ preserves Clifford grade, with eigenvalue
$2i(b-a)$ and multiplicity $\binom na\binom nb$ on grade $k$. It is therefore tempting to separate
$B$ into its chord components by a Vandermonde argument on $\mathrm{ad}_{iA}^k(B)$. That fails
exactly: every Ising edge monomial $Z_pZ_q$, path bond or chord and regardless of grade, occupies one
Majorana at each endpoint and both at every interior site, so its $\mathrm{ad}_{iA}$ spectrum is
always $\{0,\pm4i\}$. The separation recovers only three global components and never an individual
chord.
\end{remark}

For the ladder family the two-generator question is nevertheless settled completely.

\begin{theorem}[All-$L$ ladder no-go; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:ladderallL}
Let $Q(L)$ be the number of nonempty partitions with all parts at least $2$ and total at most $L$.
Then $\dim\langle A_L,B_L\rangle_{\rm Lie}\ge Q(L)$ for every $L$, by an explicit block-triangular
walk family indexed by those partitions, and $Q(L)>8L^2-2L+1=n(2n-1)$ for every $L\ge32$. Combined
with exact certificates at every smaller size --- exact dimensions at $L=3,4$, a word certificate
$191$ at $L=5$, $K_L-W_L$ at $L=6,\dots,9$, and sector-capped state certificates at
$L=10,\dots,31$, each attaining rank exactly $8L^2-2L+1$ --- the conclusion holds for every $L\ge3$:
the ladder transfer generators $A_L$ and $B_L$ are not simultaneously Majorana-quadratic in any fixed
basis. At $L=2$ the test is inconclusive, since $11<28$.
\end{theorem}

The seam is contiguous by exactly one step: $Q(31)=6841<7627$ while $Q(32)=8348>8129$, so the
certificate range $10\le L\le31$ meets the asymptotic theorem at $L=32$ with no gap. The index set
must be read carefully --- zero entries count toward the length, equivalently all parts are at least
$2$ after the shift $\nu_i=a_i+2$ --- and the exhaustively verified family ranks $21,29,41$ at
$L=8,9,10$ confirm that reading against the smaller plain-partition count $11,15,20$.

\begin{remark}[A conjectural law for $W_L$; {[CONJECTURE]}]
The certified annihilator sequence is $W_L=0,2,10,66,364,1822,8586$ for $L=3,\dots,9$, with
$K_L=(2^{2L-1}+3\cdot2^{L})/4$. A single closed form reproduces all of it with no fitted parameters:
\[
\dim U_L=\tfrac12\Bigl(\tbinom{2L}{L}+2^{L}\Bigr)-[\,4\mid L\,],\qquad W_L=K_L-\dim U_L ,
\]
giving $14,42,142,494,1780,6562,24566$ at $L=3,\dots,9$. It was identified from the $L=9$ sector
vector alone and then held out against all seven totals, all sector splits, and both parity
corrections, with zero failures; it predicts $W_{10}=38950$ and cyclic dimension $92890$. It remains
a conjecture. The correction is nevertheless structural: on
$\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Lambda^m\Qb^L)$ the symmetrised top-wedge trace has a unique
$SL_L$-invariant corank-one kernel precisely when $L\equiv0\pmod4$ and $m=L/2$, and never in another
interior sector. Thus the exceptional term $[4\mid L]$ is the only possible canonical defect.
This does not identify the physical vacuum module with that exterior-algebra model. In fact the
balanced polarization, and every signed monomial reflection-equivariant map, has rank strictly below
the conjectured sector rank at every interior $(L,m)$, already $4<6$ at $(3,1)$, and its image leaks
under each of $D,F,D^\dagger$. Any proof needs a non-monomial action or direct hard-core sector ranks.
Under the law $K_L-W_L\ge2^{L}$ for every $L$, while
$\dim U_L\sim4^{L}/(2\sqrt{\pi L})$ and $K_L\sim4^{L}/8$, so $W_L/K_L\to1$ and no bound
$W_L\le(1-c)K_L$ with constant $c>0$ can hold. The right proved all-$L$ object is the direct
dimension lower bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:ladderallL}.
\end{remark}



\section{Basis-independent spectral obstructions}\label{sec:spectral}

\subsection{The Gaussian ceiling and the subset-product criterion}
A Gaussian (free-fermion) operator with positive spectrum on $m$ modes has spectrum
\begin{equation}
\Bigl\{\lambda_0\prod_{k\in S}u_k\ :\ S\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}\Bigr\},\qquad u_k\ge1,
\label{eq:subsetproduct}
\end{equation}
a subset-product multiset. Two exact consequences are used.

\begin{lemma}[Forced eigenvalue; {[LEMMA]}]\label{lem:forced}
If the three lowest eigenvalues of \eqref{eq:subsetproduct} are distinct then
$\lambda_1\lambda_2/\lambda_0$ is also an eigenvalue.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Gaussian ceiling; {[LEMMA]}]\label{lem:ceiling}
The number of distinct unordered pair products of \eqref{eq:subsetproduct} is at most $3^n-2^n$.
Within one parity half of a $2m$-Majorana Gaussian the corresponding maximum is
$A_{\rm same}(m)=\sum_{2\le r\le m,\ r\ \mathrm{even}}\binom{m}{r}2^{m-r}$, so
$A_{\rm same}(12)=261625$.
\end{lemma}

Both are counting statements about \eqref{eq:subsetproduct} and involve no basis choice, which is
what makes the resulting no-gos immune to ``free fermions in disguise''.

\subsection{Inertia certificates}
\begin{theorem}[Theorem S; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:S}
At $t=1/3$, equivalently $q=e^{2K}=5/3$, the open $2\times3$ layer transfer operator is not a
fermionic Gaussian operator on six modes in any Majorana basis. The three lowest eigenvalues are
certified distinct with relative gaps $53.0\%$ and $40.9\%$; the forced value
$\lambda_1\lambda_2/\lambda_0$ lies in the exact rational window
$[0.0347241721739,\ 0.0347241728052]$; and exact rational Sylvester inertia counts at the two
outward-rounded endpoints are both $8$, so the window contains no eigenvalue.
\end{theorem}

The control chains of lengths four and six show the opposite inertia pattern, as expected for the
two-dimensional free-fermion model. The asymmetry is essential and is stated in the artifact: equal
inertia counts prove \emph{absence}; unequal counts show only that some eigenvalue lies in the
window, never that the forced value is attained. Theorem S extends to $t=1/5,2/5,1/2$ on $2\times3$
and to the $2\times4$ layer at $t=1/3$ through certified symmetry sectors. A companion theorem for
the two physical $P=\prod_iX_i$ sectors shows they are not the even/odd halves of any single six-mode
Gaussian, in either assignment.

\subsection{The pair-product invariant}
The inertia route needs an ordering and a positivity hypothesis. The pair-product route needs
neither: count distinct unordered products $\lambda_i\lambda_j$ and compare with
Lemma~\ref{lem:ceiling}. Concretely, if $C(z)=\prod_{i<j}(z-\lambda_i\lambda_j)$ then the number of
distinct products is $\binom{N}{2}-\deg\gcd(C,C')$, and after monic integral normalisation every
modular $\gcd$ degree is an upper bound for the characteristic-zero one, which is the direction that
yields a lower bound on the count.

\begin{theorem}[Ordering-free no-gos; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:pairproducts}
With $N$ the block dimension, at the stated couplings and at both primes $1000003$ and $2000003$:
\[
\begin{array}{l|r|r|r|r}
\text{layer} & N & \deg\gcd & \text{distinct products}\ \ge & \text{Gaussian ceiling}\\ \hline
2\times3\ (t=1/3) & 64 & 385 & 1631 & 665\\
2\times4\ (t=1/3) & 256 & 9329 & 23311 & 6305\\
3\times3\ (t=1/3) & 512 & 59641 & 71175 & 19171\\
2\times5\ (t=1/3) & 1024 & 124921 & 398855 & 58025
\end{array}
\]
In each case the spectrum is not a full $n$-mode Gaussian subset-product multiset. Open-chain

\subsection{All-size theorems and the isotropic gap}\label{sec:allsize}
\begin{theorem}[Theorem F, all-size anisotropic; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:F}
Let $G$ be a finite simple graph with a vertex of degree at least three and $n=|V|\ge4$. For the

inhomogeneous layer is not Gaussian, with an excess that is the constant fraction $16/27$ of $3^n$.
\end{theorem}

The mechanism is a ceiling lemma, an exact tensor identity
$r=(3^m-2^m)\,r_{\rm all}(\text{core})+2^m\,r(\text{core})$, a claw seed with
$r_{\rm all}\ge129>81$, and monic specialization. The exceptional set is a proper algebraic
hypersurface, hence Lebesgue-null.

\begin{remark}[The isotropic gap: a split verdict; {[THEOREM]}/{[UNRESOLVED]}]
Theorem~\ref{thm:F} is genuinely anisotropic, and the two halves of the isotropic repair now have
opposite verdicts. The \emph{core} half succeeds: at $t=1/3$, at both primes, three isotropic cores
are certified strictly above their own Gaussian ceiling --- the paw ($r_\Qb\ge119>65$), the five-site

graph-piece tensor factorization is not an identity on the isotropic curve, since the crossing-factor
minor is $q(t)^{2c}-1$ with cleared numerator $(1+t^2)^{2c}-(2t)^{2c}>0$ for $0<t<1$; and the natural

theorem is claimed; what is now known is that any proof must be non-localizing. Note that the
isotropic all-size \emph{algebraic} statement is obtained independently in
\S\ref{sec:trichotomy}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The group route to the isotropic case is also closed; {[THEOREM]}]
Given Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy}, the natural remaining attack is structural rather than
counting: the physical family $V(a,b)=e^{aA}e^{bB}$ is a curve in a group whose Lie algebra is now
known to be exponentially large, so one would like to argue that such a curve cannot lie in the union
of conjugates of the spin subgroup. That argument is \emph{false}, and the counterexample is already
one-dimensional. For the one-mode spin torus, $M(a,b)=e^{aH}e^{bX}$ is pointwise conjugate into
$\mathrm{Spin}(2,\Cb)$ wherever its discriminant is nonzero, and at every real parameter, while
$\langle H,X\rangle_{\rm Lie}=\mathfrak{sl}(2,\Cb)$; at $b=0$ the exact identity $DX=[Y(r),D]$ with
$Y(r)=(r^2-1)^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-r^2\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ shows the
varying conjugator absorbs the entire transverse derivative and becomes singular at the identity. A
second obstruction compounds it: in multiplicative coordinates $x=e^{a}$, $y=e^{2b}$ the isotropic
family is the proper curve $(y-1)x^2-(y+1)=0$, so ambient two-parameter noncontainment would not
imply noncontainment along it. A large fixed Lie algebra therefore excludes a
\emph{parameter-independent} quadratic basis only, never pointwise varying Gaussian conjugacies. Both
named routes to the isotropic all-size spectral theorem --- localizing decoupling and group rigidity
--- are now exactly closed, and a third mechanism is required: either a conjugacy-invariant spectral
polynomial proved nonzero modulo the physical curve, or a rigidity theorem with hypotheses the Ising
family actually satisfies.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Every-coupling non-bipartite isotropic no-go; {[THEOREM]}]\label{thm:isotrace}
Let $G$ be any finite simple non-bipartite graph on $n$ vertices. For every real physical isotropic
$0<t<1$, its layer spectrum is not a full $n$-mode subset-product multiset, even with arbitrary
nonzero complex mode parameters. The exceptional physical set is empty.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $N=2^n$, $M=N/2$, and define the homogeneous conjugacy invariant
\[
\Phi_N(W)=e_N(W)^{M-1}e_1(W)^M-e_{N-1}(W)^M.
\]
Complementary slots of every subset-product multiset have constant product, which forces
$\Phi_N=0$. For the determinant-one Ising transfer representative,
\[
\Phi_N=\text{nonzero common factor}\,
\bigl(Q_G(y)^M-Q_G^*(y)^M\bigr),\qquad Q_G^*(y)=y^{|E|}Q_G(1/y).
\]
The trace skew has the exact Eulerian expansion
\[
Q_G(y)-Q_G^*(y)=2^{n+1-|E|}
\sum_{\substack{F\subseteq E\ {\rm Eulerian}\\ |F|\ {\rm odd}}}
(y-1)^{|F|}(y+1)^{|E|-|F|}.
\]
A graph has an odd Eulerian set iff it is non-bipartite. On the physical curve
$y=q(t)=(1+t^2)/(2t)>1$, every term is positive, so $\Phi_N\ne0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact blind locus]
For every bipartite graph, flipping one colour class gives $Q_G=Q_G^*$, so this invariant vanishes
identically. Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:isotrace} supplies an all-size, every-coupling connected branching
family and proves nonvanishing modulo the physical curve, but does not settle any rectangular
simple-cubic layer. The bipartite isotropic problem remains the sharp gap.
\end{remark}

A separate exact analysis settles what happens at the boundary of an absence interval: the forced
value crosses the spectrum at real algebraic couplings, four exact crossing brackets are isolated,
each containing exactly one crossing by five-variable Krawczyk contraction, and absence is proved on
three connected crossing-free rational intervals, the widest of width $1/1048576$. Global crossing
exhaustiveness remains open.

      order-independent algebra by transvection orbits. $K_{1,3}=72$ is a confirmation of the
      corrected parity formula, not an exception to the branching branch.
      \item \textbf{Susceptibility ratio wording corrected.} The coefficient ratios are
      nonincreasing on the audited prefix, not strictly decreasing:
      $a_2/a_1=a_3/a_2=5$. The first clean-room verifier exposed this; producer, artifact, proof and
      verifier were corrected before acceptance. No endpoint claim depended on the wording.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}
In descending order of value-times-tractability as judged from the current evidence.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The bipartite isotropic spectral theorem.} Theorem~\ref{thm:isotrace} proves every
      non-bipartite graph at every coupling, but its invariant vanishes on every physical rectangular
      layer. The next invariant must survive the colour-flip reciprocity identity; neither
      localization nor group rigidity can do so.
\item \textbf{Two-generator grids beyond ladders.} The physical family needs all $3\times L$ and
      rectangles with $\min(a,b)\ge4$. Rank-one frozen-row restriction and $\lvert+\rangle$
      compression are exactly closed by a depth-three seam anomaly; a continuation needs a
      higher-rank invariant code or direct word family.
\item \textbf{Prove or refute the exact $W_L$ law.} The exceptional hyperplane is canonical, but
      natural signed-monomial intertwiners are impossible. A proof needs a non-monomial action or
      direct hard-core sector ranks; $L=10$ is the first untested row, not permission to fit.
\item \textbf{Memory 14.} Compact suffix encoding should make the next finite-memory SAW bound
      feasible; the current tuple/cache implementation projects roughly $620{,}000$ states. This is
      a preflight, not an observed wall. The inaccessible $4.7114$ proof remains excluded.
\item \textbf{Upper endpoint below the incumbent.} The first four-point power-simplex level also
      projects to the old two-point class. A live route needs phase-sensitive Fourier identities,
      spin-realizability/DLR constraints, or multi-edge/sourced random currents.
\item \textbf{Kac--Ward branch $11111$} over $\Qb$ and $\Cb$. Nullstellensatz and orbit quotient
      routes are impossible; non-unit valuations, zero reductions, chart-degenerate loci, higher
      $5$-adic digits and other primes remain.
\item \textbf{Higher-dimensional/spectral Lax equations.} Large local-term algebra does not imply
      all-auxiliary rigidity: exact permutation and XXX controls refute that shortcut. Progress
      requires the actual nonidentical/spectral/IRF/dynamical equations.
\item \textbf{Series and charges}: high-temperature $v^{30}$, low-temperature $x^{60},x^{62}$ and
      the $s=6$ radius-two extensive-charge class. The series are resource frontiers; the charge
      class needs a new structural reduction, not either closed symmetry compression.
\item \textbf{Forced interlayer data.} Evaluate or bound the actual square-lattice $W_8,W_{10}$
      sums and prove a vertical-coupling convergence domain; universal closure by lower cumulants
      is impossible.
\item \textbf{Larger spectral and Lee--Yang certificates.} The $3\times4$ pair-product computation
      needs explicit process-time budgeting. Lee--Yang scaling needs a no-earlier-root,
      interlacing/argument-principle or correction-amplitude theorem rather than larger fits.
\end{enumerate}
