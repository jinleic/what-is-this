## 1. Krumm 2016 — the degree ≤ 2 anchor for Hilbert-symbol–restricted square-class values

- **Exact reference.** David Krumm, *Squarefree parts of polynomial values*, Journal de Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux **28** (2016), no. 3, 699–724. DOI 10.5802/jtnb.959. arXiv:**1407.4890** (v1 2014-07-18). [VP: arXiv full text + Numdam/JTNB issue listing]
- **Refuted.** No Krumm paper titled 'Hilbert symbol conditions and primes represented by polynomials' (or similar) exists; the intended reference is the paper above. [VP: direct title/author searches + JTNB volume listing]
- **Content.** $\mathcal{S}(f)$ = set of squarefree parts of all *rational* values $f(r)$, $r\in\mathbb{Q}$. Statement A(n): for separable $f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$ of degree n and any good prime p, every m coprime to p is represented mod p by infinitely many $d\in\mathcal{S}(f)$; stronger prime variants I(n)/P(n) produce *primes* $q\in\mathcal{S}(f)$ with $q\equiv m\ (p)$ (i.e. $f(r)=q\cdot(\text{rational square})$ with q prime in a prescribed class — Krumm's closest shape to H).
- **Unconditional range: degrees 1 and 2 only.** Theorem 1.3: A(1), A(2) hold unconditionally; proved as Proposition 3.4 (A(1)) and Proposition 3.5 (A(2)). Prop 3.5's mechanism is quintessentially local/Hilbert: primes q with q ≡ m (mod p) AND q ≡ 1 (mod 8δ) — which force the quaternion symbol (δ,q)_v ≡ +1 — are rationally represented by the binary quadratic form x² − δy² (Legendre's theorem + Dirichlet via Lemma 3.3). [VP]
- **Degree 3: conditional.** Proposition 3.8: A(3) follows from the **Parity Conjecture for elliptic curves over Q** (rank parity, BSD-type); Prop 3.10: A(4) for f with a rational root, same hypothesis. This is NOT the sieve-theoretic 'parity problem'. Remark 3.9: the conjecture cannot be bypassed via torsion points (Silverman). For arbitrary degree only a *local* analogue exists (Theorem 1.4, §4), and even it requires odd degree or an irreducible factor with **abelian Galois group**. [VP]

(JTNB page numbering expected identical to arXiv's; not diffed page-for-page.) [VP for arXiv; SS for JTNB-vs-arXiv numbering identity]

## 2. No degree-3 extension exists; what degree 3 does have

- **Negative.** No unconditional extension of Krumm's square-class/local-condition result to degree 3 exists; Krumm's own degree-3 case is conditional (Prop 3.8, elliptic Parity Conjecture). Unconditional degree-3 fragments exist only for special curves (Monsky: $\mathcal{S}(x^3-x)$ hits 5,6,7 mod 8 infinitely often — cited within Krumm). [VP for the prop; absence-of-candidate for 'no extension', after targeted search]
- **Closest unconditional degree-3 results (prime argument, no class prescription).**
  - H. A. Helfgott, *Square-free values of f(p), f cubic*, Acta Math. **213** (2014) no. 1, 107–135. arXiv:**1112.3820**. Abstract: for separable cubic f with no local q²-obstruction, infinitely many primes p have f(p) squarefree. [VP]
  - Thomas Reuss, *Power-Free Values of Polynomials*, Bull. London Math. Soc. **47** (2015) no. 2, 270–284. arXiv:**1307.2802**. Abstract: asymptotic for #{p<x : f(p) is (d−1)-free}, f irreducible of degree d ≥ 3. [VP]

## 3. Best CURRENT unconditional degree bound (H's shape)

- Square-class / squarefree-part of polynomial values under finitely many local (Hilbert-symbol ≙ congruence) conditions: **degree ≤ 2 is the ceiling**, Krumm op. cit. Props 3.4–3.5 (rational arguments; prime squarefree parts obtainable via I(1)/P(2)). Degree 3: open unconditionally (only elliptic-Parity-conditional). Degrees 4 and 8: fully open. [VP for the positives; SS for the open status]
- At prime arguments the only unconditional theorems are squarefree/power-free values (Helfgott deg 3; Reuss (d−1)-free, d ≥ 3); **nothing prescribes a square class of f(p)** — squarefree f(p) itself is open for deg f ≥ 4, and even at integer arguments squarefree values are open unconditionally for deg ≥ 4 (Granville IMRN 1998 handles any degree only under abc). [SS]
- Bottom line: the degree-8 input in H (square class of an irreducible deg-8 polynomial at prime arguments in a fixed AP) is beyond the proved range on **both** sides: unsolved shape (class prescription) × unsolved degree (≥ 4) at prime arguments, and unsolved degree (≥ 3) at rational/integer arguments.

- **Theorem 1.4 (= Corollary 5.11), verbatim [VP]:** Suppose that K is finitely generated over a perfect field. If D₁, D₂ ⊆ Kⁿ are diophantine sets with rk^∃_K(D₁) > 0 and rk^∃_K(D₂) > 0, then rk^∃_K(D₁ ∩ D₂) ≤ rk^∃_K(D₁) + rk^∃_K(D₂) − 1. ## 5. Why degree 8 with nonabelian (D8 wr C2) Galois group is beyond all current methods (one sentence)

Every unconditional theorem of this shape — Harpaz–Skorobogatov–Wittenberg, *The Hardy–Littlewood conjecture and rational points*, Compositio Math. **150** (2014) no. 12, 2095–2111, DOI 10.1112/S0010437X14007568, arXiv:1304.3333, Thm 3.1 & 3.3 and Cor 3.4 (pencils; Br_vert density), and Krumm's local analogue — requires the algebraic closure of Q in the bad fibres' function fields (equivalently the splitting behaviour of the value polynomial) to be an **abelian extension of Q** (Thm 3.1(b), verbatim: 'the algebraic closure of Q in its field of functions is an abelian extension of Q'), because the only proved substitute for Schinzel's Hypothesis (H) is the Green–Tao–Ziegler theorem, which covers **linear** polynomials (abelian splitting fields); a single irreducible degree-8 polynomial with wreath-product Galois group D8 wr C2 (nonabelian, order 128) falls in the regime where only Schinzel's (H) would suffice — Colliot-Thélène–Skorobogatov–Swinnerton-Dyer, *Rational points and zero-cycles on fibred varieties: Schinzel's hypothesis and Salberger's device*, J. reine angew. Math. **495** (1998) 1–28 — and no unconditional method applies. [VP: Thm 3.1(b) and both bibliography entries read verbatim from the paper]
