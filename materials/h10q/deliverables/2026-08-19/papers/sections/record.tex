\section{The conditional record and the Schinzel audit}
\label{sec:record}

\subsection{The statement, with its hypothesis exposed}

The hypothesis used below is not an unnamed appeal to genericity.  It is the
following cellwise assertion, quoted in the form in which it is recorded in
the ledger.

\begin{definition}[CONDITIONAL: Hypothesis H]
For every odd prime $w$ and every rational $z$ with $v_w(z)\geq 1$ there
exists a verified aligned class for the cell $(w,z)$---prime-$b$ data
$(a,\varepsilon,1,q,N)$ from the L11 family, or escape data
$(a,\varepsilon,f,q,N)$ with $f\mid\operatorname{num}(z)$ from the L12
family---and a prime member $Q\equiv q\pmod N$ outside its finite excluded
set, at which the emergent set is empty: every odd prime
$l\notin S\cup\{Q\}$ satisfying
$v_l(P(\varepsilon fQ))\neq 0$ has $v_l(P(\varepsilon fQ))$ even.  Here $S$
is the frozen controlled set belonging to the verified class.
% src: CONDITIONAL.md:76-109
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[CONDITIONAL: Theorem C]
Assume H.  Then $\Q\setminus\Z$ is Diophantine in $\Q$ with six unknowns,
realized by the L6 formula
\[
 F(z):=\exists b,s\;\bigl[(1+2s,b)\in\Phi_1^{\{2\}}\ \wedge\
 \Theta_*\bigl(1+2s,b,h(1+2s,b,z^3),s\bigr)\bigr],
\]
where, with $A=1+4a^2$ and $B=2b$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Theta_*(a,b,c,s):=\exists y,r\quad&
 [c^2-Ay^2-16Br^2=16-16ABs^2]\\
 &{}\vee[c^2-Ay^2-16ABr^2=16A-16A^2Bs^2].
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently $\Z$ is definable in $\Q$ by a formula with six universal
quantifiers,
$\Z=\Q\setminus(F\vee\mathfrak m_2)$, and
$\operatorname{efd}_{\Q}(\Q\setminus\Z)\leq 5$.
% src: CONDITIONAL.md:14-25
\end{theorem}

This is the ledger's exact conditional conclusion.  It is \emph{not} an
unconditional definition and makes \textbf{no record claim}.  Soundness of
the displayed formula consumes no hypothesis; completeness is exactly H.
A finite collection of closed cells, however exhaustive within its stated
horizon, cannot replace H's quantification over every odd $w$ and every
eligible $z$.
% src: CONDITIONAL.md:55-70
% src: THEOREMS.md:1503-1511

The quantifier saving is attributed precisely to Nicolas Daans, Philip
Dittmann, and Arno Fehm, \emph{Existential rank and essential dimension of
diophantine sets}, arXiv:2102.06941v5, Theorem~1.4 (equivalently
Corollary~5.11)~\cite{ddf}.  Their intersection theorem turns the ranks
three and two on the common base into $3+2-1=4$ witnesses; the two base
coordinates then give six unknowns.  The recorded rank--essential-dimension
identity gives the bound five.  These counts remain \textbf{CONDITIONAL}
because the set equality to $\Q\setminus\Z$ uses H.

\subsection{What is proved, and what Schinzel must supply}

\textbf{PROVED (finite alignment settled).}  Once an aligned class has been found, all
controlled Hilbert symbols are frozen and the moving prime is restricted to
one reduced progression.  For every canonical row audited here this
progression is
\[
 q_1(t)=q_{1,0}+Nt,\qquad \gcd(q_{1,0},N)=1.
\]
The stored $q_{1,0}$ is proven prime by the repository's proven-primality
engine.  Dirichlet's theorem therefore supplies infinitely many prime values
of $q_1(t)$.  This is the linear, unconditional half of the member-existence
problem; neither a refusal by the primality engine nor a probable-prime test
is used as evidence.
% src: THEOREMS.md:953-971
% src: data/l17_schinzel_audit.jsonl:1-294

\textbf{OPEN/CONDITIONAL (cofactor side).}  Put
\[
 F_{\mathrm{cell}}(t)=P\bigl(\varepsilon f q_1(t)\bigr),
\]
using the primitive integer representative after removal of the fixed
rational scale recorded in the audit.  Requiring $F_{\mathrm{cell}}(t)$ to
be prime simultaneously with $q_1(t)$ is the two-polynomial instance of
Schinzel's Hypothesis~H \cite{schinzel} (or the corresponding Bunyakovsky-type prime-value
input).  The polynomial $F_{\mathrm{cell}}$ has degree eight.  A prime
cofactor is a sufficient route to an emergent-free member, because the
class has already frozen the controlled places and reciprocity forces the
remaining single symbol.  This sufficient prime-cofactor route must not be
confused with a proof of H, whose conclusion asks only for one
emergent-free member and is formally weaker.  In the ledger's shorthand,
after using the progression prime itself as variable, the pair is written
$\{t,F_{\mathrm{cell}}(t)\}$.

\subsection{PROVED finite audit of the Schinzel pair}

The exact audit contains $293$ canonical records, split into $190$ L11
classes and $103$ escape classes.  The fields
\texttt{F\_irreducible}, \texttt{F\_value\_gcd}, and
\texttt{pair\_product\_gcd} respectively certify irreducibility on
$293/293$ rows, value gcd one on $293/293$ rows, and pair-product gcd one
on $293/293$ rows.  Every irreducibility certificate is a degree-eight
Frobenius certificate modulo a certified prime; both gcds were computed
exactly on $0\leq t\leq 2000$.  The pair-product computation is already a
certificate that no prime divides the product for every integer $t$: any
such fixed divisor would divide the computed gcd.  Thus all canonical
pairs pass the fixed-divisor and irreducibility conditions.  This is
\textbf{PROVED} for the stored pairs, not a prime-value theorem.
% src: data/l17_schinzel_audit.jsonl:1-294

For a prime $p$ dividing $N$, the corrected residue count is
\[
 \nu_p=\#\{t\in\F_p:q_1(t)\not\equiv0\pmod p,
                  \ F_{\mathrm{cell}}(t)\not\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]
The first condition is essential.  Since $p\mid N$ and
$\gcd(q_{1,0},N)=1$, the linear polynomial is the nonzero constant
$q_{1,0}$ modulo $p$; it must not be replaced by the parameter $t$.
Consequently the count is $p$, rather than $p-1$, whenever the cofactor has
no root modulo $p$.  The audit enumerated every $p\leq1000$ dividing the
row's modulus and obtained exactly this full count in every occurrence.

\textbf{OPEN (published boundary).}  The exact square-class reference is
David Krumm, \emph{Squarefree parts of polynomial values}, Journal de
Th\'eorie des Nombres de Bordeaux \textbf{28} (2016), no.~3, 699--724,
arXiv:1407.4890~\cite{krumm}.  Its Theorem~1.3 and Propositions~3.4--3.5
give the unconditional degree-one and degree-two cases.  Proposition~3.8
makes the degree-three case conditional on the Parity Conjecture for
elliptic curves over $\Q$---rank parity, not the sieve ``parity problem.''
% src: data/litscout_h10q.md:6-13

At prime arguments, the nearest unconditional results reach the cubic
boundary without prescribing a square class: Helfgott proves square-free
values of $f(p)$ for separable cubic $f$ \cite{helfgott}, while Reuss
proves $(d-1)$-free values rather than prime or prescribed-square-class
values \cite{reuss}.  Neither yields the required degree-eight
conclusion.
% src: data/litscout_h10q.md:15-28

The conic-bundle literature does not remove the gap.  The unconditional
Harpaz--Skorobogatov--Wittenberg theorem requires abelian constant fields
in the bad fibres \cite[Theorem~3.1 and Corollary~3.4]{hsw}.  The general
Schinzel reference in \emph{J. reine angew. Math.} \textbf{495} (1998),
1--28 is by Colliot-Th\'el\`ene, Skorobogatov and Swinnerton-Dyer
\cite{ctssd}, not by Colliot-Th\'el\`ene and Sansuc; the latter pair's
Schinzel paper is \cite{ctsansuc}.  Thus no unconditional
result is available for the degree-eight prime-value side or for the
required degree-eight square-class side.  The six-quantifier theorem above
remains \textbf{CONDITIONAL}, and no unconditional record is claimed.
