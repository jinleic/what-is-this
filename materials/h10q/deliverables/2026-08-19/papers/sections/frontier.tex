\section{Unconditional density, algebraic walls, and the off-grid frontier}
\label{sec:frontier}

This section separates the unconditional analytic gain from the remaining
parity problem, records the algebraic and fibration walls found in L23--L24,
and then states the finite off-grid horizon.

\subsection{An unconditional local bound}

Fix an aligned class with parameters $(\varepsilon,f,q_1,N)$ and write
$F(k) = P(\varepsilon f (q_1 + kN))$ for its degree-$8$ class
polynomial, and $u(k) = 2\alpha\varepsilon f(q_1+kN)$ for the unit whose
Legendre symbol decides bad signature. For an odd prime $p$ let $m_p$
denote the Haar mass in $\Zp$ of the set of $k$ with $v_p(F(k))$ odd and
$\leg{u(k)}{p} = -1$: the local mass of the emergent obstruction.

\begin{theorem}[Local sieve bound; PROVED]
\label{thm:localbound}
For every aligned class of the grid,
\[
   m_3 = m_5 = m_7 = 0,
   \qquad\text{and}\qquad
   m_p \;\le\; \frac{8}{p+1} \;<\; 1
   \quad\text{for all } p \ge 11 ,
\]
the bound holding for singular roots as well, counted with
multiplicity.
% src: data/l17_stepii.jsonl (meta.labels.theorem_replays = PROVED)
\end{theorem}

The vanishing at $p \in \{3,5,7\}$ is exactly the frozen-alignment
condition of the class certificate: those places are controlled, and
their symbols are pinned to $+1$ by construction. For $p \ge 11$ the
bound is the root count of a degree-$8$ polynomial: at most eight
residues can be roots, each simple root contributes Haar mass
$1/(p+1)$ for odd valuation, and a singular root contributes at most
its multiplicity times that mass, which is again bounded by the total
degree. The bound was verified independently on every stored local
pair: $102{,}439$ pairs with $p \le 10^4$ across the $293$ classes,
zero violations.
% src: data/l17_stepii.jsonl (verification.positive_local_pairs_checked_p_le_10000 = 102439, bound_violations = 0)

Theorem~\ref{thm:localbound} settles a natural hope in the negative,
and this is the important structural point.

\begin{corollary}[No uniform positive mass; PROVED for the current classes]
\label{cor:nouniform}
For each of the $293$ classes the splitting condition governing the
bad-signature event has Chebotarev exponent $c = 1/2$: the class
polynomial is irreducible with a replayed degree-$8$ certificate, and a
replayed witness shows the bad unit is a nonsquare at a simple bad
root. Consequently the truncated clean product obeys
\[
   \prod_{p \le X} (1 - m_p) \;\asymp\; (\log X)^{-1/2},
\]
so no cutoff-uniform positive lower bound on the clean mass exists:
the small-layer clean probability decays, slowly, to zero.
% src: data/l17_stepii.jsonl (verdict; serialized_witnesses_replayed = 293)
\end{corollary}

The measured exponent agrees: the root-harmonic estimate gives
$c \approx 0.4778$, the product estimate $c \approx 0.4801$, and the
same-member sieve between $10^4$ and $10^5$ gives $c \approx 0.4590$
(EVIDENCE).
% src: data/l17_stepii.jsonl (evidence.root_harmonic_c_mean, product_c_mean, same_member_sieve_c_10000_100000)

The consequence for the member property $\mathcal M$ is a sharpening, not a defeat. The
number of admissible prime members up to height $X$ in a class grows
like $X/(\log X)^{2}$ by the Dirichlet--Bateman--Horn heuristic
\cite{batemanhorn}, while
each carries clean probability $\asymp (\log X)^{-1/2}$; the expected
count of emergent-free members therefore still diverges, at rate
$X/(\log X)^{5/2}$. What Corollary~\ref{cor:nouniform} rules out is the
clean shortcut --- a uniform positive constant --- and what it leaves is
a divergence statement of exactly the shape the measured counting law
already exhibits (first emergent-free member at $k=0$ in $207$ of $293$
classes).

\subsection{The L23 half-sieve and its exact parity wall}

Write the primitive L20 linear--octic pair as
\[
 Q(t)=q_1+Nt,\qquad G(t),
\]
and let a prime \(p\) be \emph{bad-root} at \(t\) when
\(G(t)\equiv0\pmod p\) and the associated unit \(u(t)\) has negative
quadratic character at \(p\).  Finite exceptional primes are first put
into a compatible fixed CRT class.

\begin{theorem}[Half-dimensional small-prime sieve; PROVED]
\label{thm:halfsieve}
For every fixed admissible L20 pair, the bad-root sieve has dimension
\(\kappa=1/2\):
\[
 \sum_{p<z}g_{\rm root}(p)\log p
   =\frac12\log z+O(1).
\]
Bombieri--Vinogradov supplies level
\[
 D=\frac{X^{1/2}}{(\log X)^B}.
\]
Consequently the semilinear beta sieve, with \(z=X^{49/100}\), gives
\[
 \#\left\{X<t\le2X:
   \begin{array}{l}
      Q(t)\ {\rm is\ prime},\\[-2pt]
      G(t)\ {\rm has\ no\ bad\ root\ prime}\ p<z
   \end{array}\right\}
 \gg \frac{X}{(\log X)^{3/2}} .
\]
The implied constant may depend on the fixed class.
\end{theorem}
% src: data/l23_half_sieve.jsonl (prime-sequence-sieve and beta-sieve-parameters rows)

These are \emph{small-prime-clean members of the progression only}.  The
theorem does not say that they are emergent-free members of
\(\mathcal M\).  Indeed \(|G(t)|=X^{8+o(1)}\), so after sieving only to
\(X^{49/100}\) a value may retain as many as \(16\) large prime factors,
with multiplicity.  Reciprocity says that the number of large
odd-valuation bad factors is even, leaving
\[
 0,2,4,\ldots,16,
\]
not just \(0\).  Even Elliott--Halberstam level would leave as many as
eight factors.  Kao's degree-eight prime-argument theorem reaches only
\(P_{12}\), whereas reciprocity would close the argument only at
\(R_{\rm bad}\le1\).
% src: data/l23_half_sieve.jsonl (beta-sieve-parameters and almost-prime-threshold rows)

Thus the first missing analytic input is a parity-sensitive saving for
the sector with two large bad odd-valuation divisors
\(p_1,p_2\ge X^{1/2-\epsilon}\) and \(p_1p_2>D\), beyond the
Bombieri--Vinogradov range.  A finite no-hit scan cannot supply that
estimate.

\subsection{The L23 fibration and norm walls}

On the canonical branch the tied quaternion has the conic-bundle model
\[
 U^2-2bV^2=P_{a,Z}(b)W^2 .
\]
For irreducible \(P\), its closed degenerate-fibre degrees are
\(1+8+1\), at \(b=0\), \(P(b)=0\), and \(b=\infty\).  On the refined
L20 choice \(a\not\equiv1\pmod w\), the target reduction has the simple
root
\[
 \beta=(2As^2)^{-1},\qquad
 2\beta=(As^2)^{-1},
\]
which is a nonsquare because \(\leg{A}{w}=-1\).  Such a refinement is
available from the exact general character count
\[
 \#\{a\bmod w:\leg{1+4a^2}{w}=-1\}
   =\frac{w-\leg{-1}{w}}2 .
\]
If \(v_w(z)\) is odd, the left Newton edge gives an odd valuation for
\(2b\) for every admissible \(a\).  Hence \(2\theta\) is nonsquare in
\(\Q(\theta)\), where \(P(\theta)=0\), throughout these scopes.
% src: data/l23_fibration.jsonl (target-prime-capell-no-go row)

It follows that the natural relatively minimal bundle has non-split rank
\(10\), not rank at most \(2\), and
\[
 \operatorname{Br}(X)/\operatorname{Br}(\Q)
   \cong\Z/2\Z,\qquad\text{generated by }(A,b).
\]
Thus the Harpaz--Wei--Wittenberg rank-\(\le2\) theorem and the checked
low-rank variants do not apply.  The pre-existing fixed-class stratum
with \(v_w(z)\) even and \(w\mid s\) remains OPEN without an exact row
certificate; this does not obstruct the global class construction,
because the free L20 refinement and the L22 Hilbert selection avoid that
stratum.
% src: data/l23_fibration.jsonl (conic-bundle-geometry, vertical-brauer-group,
% src: target-prime-capell-no-go, unconditional-theorem-match, and summary rows)

The accompanying exact algebra rules out the most direct section
escapes.  Exact norm matching is \(\Phi\)-empty by its \(2\)-adic
valuation, and there is no polynomial-in-\(b\) norm section.  The loci
\(P=\pm2b\,y^2\) are \(\Phi\)-empty, while writing \(P\) as \(A\) times
a norm forces the \(2\)-adic symbol to be \(-1\).  A fixed
\(P\)-square construction leads to a genus-one locus.  The first
\(\Phi\)-admissible factor ansatz,
\[
 L=-A\rho^2,
\]
does factor \(H\), but it reduces the problem to scoped bad Hilbert
symbols and genus-four curves rather than producing a section.  Fixed
nonsquare twists merely relocate the quadratic character.

When \(a\) is allowed to move with the prime parameter, the smallest

are not a minimum, and no general minimum \(21\) is claimed.

\subsection{The L24 diagonal route is refuted}

The two self-coupled diagonal orientations define exact finite-flat
degree-\(8\) covers and would have tied the extra rank at most one.  They
therefore looked like a possible route to a five-unknown definition.
Both images are, however, uniformly empty on \(\Phi\) over \(\Q_2\).

For orientation I the normalized eliminant has coefficient valuations
\((4,0,0)\).  Its roots have \(v_2(u)=4\) or \(0\); if \(u\) is a square,
then \(A+u\) is respectively \(5\) or \(6\bmod8\), never a square.  For
orientation II the eliminant is
\[
 u^2-32hu+16e=0,\qquad h,e\in\Z_2^\times .
\]
Every root has \(v_2(u)=2\).  Substituting \(u=4t^2\) gives the complete
modulo-\(16\) contradiction
\[
 8+2Ab(s^2-1)\not\equiv0\pmod{16}.
\]
Thus the putative five-count is false, not OPEN.
% src: data/l24_diagonal_geometry.jsonl (orientation-I/II-Phi-obstruction rows)
% src: data/l24_diagonal_arithmetic.jsonl (Phi-two-adic-obstruction and summary rows)

The obstruction is genuinely dyadic: at every odd target place,
orientation II is locally soluble in the standard \(k=1<3v_w(z)\)
stratum.  Geometrically the generic arithmetic monodromy is
\(V_4\wr C_2\) of order \(32\); the audited slices have genera \(35\)
and \(53\), and neither orientation has a base-rational section.
The \(4{,}026{,}282\)-attempt search found no rational hit, but that
finite zero-hit count is EVIDENCE only; the uniform negative theorem is
the preceding \(2\)-adic proof.

\subsection{The L25 scaled couplings are not dyadically killed}

L24 kills the two \emph{unit} self-couplings.  The next family changes
only fixed rational multiples of the tied norm witnesses and introduces
no new free variable.  On the square branch
\[
 X^2-\rho^2=A,\qquad u=\rho^2,
\]
the type-I coupling \((y,r)=(\kappa_1X,\kappa_2\rho)\) eliminates to
\[
 A\kappa_1^2u^2
 +(A^2\kappa_1^2-c^2-16AB\kappa_2^2)u
 +16A^2Bs^2-16A=0.
\]
The identity, and the type-II analogue, were replayed on \(1{,}200\)
exact instances.
% src: data/l25_scaled_coupling.jsonl (identity-replay row)

\begin{theorem}[Scaled dyadic escape; PROVED]
\label{thm:scaledescape}
On \(\Phi\), the fixed coupling \((y,r)=(2X,\rho)\) has a
\(2\)-adically admissible branch exactly when \(s\) is even, and the
fixed coupling \((y,r)=(2X,\rho/2)\) has a \(2\)-adically admissible
branch exactly when \(s\) is odd.  Both statements are uniform in odd
\(b\), in the declared scalar unit classes, and in every \(c\) with
\(v_2(c)\ge4\).  Hence their two-formula disjunction is nonempty on
every \(\Phi\) stratum.
\end{theorem}
% src: data/l25_scaled_coupling.jsonl (dyadic-escape rows)

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
After dividing by \(4A\), put \(u=4t^2\).  In each case the unit
quartic has the form
\[
 g(t)=4\mu^2t^4+Lt^2+M_0,\qquad M_0=2Abs^2-1,
\]
with \(L,M_0\in\Z_2^\times\).  For odd \(t\), \(v_2(g'(t))=1\), so a
root modulo \(8\) lifts to a \(2\)-adic root.  The two reductions are
\[
 g(1)\equiv2Abs^2\pmod8,\qquad
 g(1)\equiv2b(As^2-1)\pmod8
\]
for \(\kappa_2=1\) and \(1/2\), respectively.  The first vanishes
exactly for even \(s\); the second exactly for odd \(s\).  The other
possible root valuation of the original \(u\)-quadratic is \(0\), which
makes \(A+u\equiv6\pmod8\), so the quartic condition is also necessary.
The declared residue classes were all replayed machine-exactly.
\end{proof}

At an odd target prime \(w\), choose the freely chosen unit-\(b\)
unramified reduction \(c\equiv0\pmod w\) and the residues
\[
 (s,A,X,\rho,b)\equiv(0,5,3,2,\kappa^{-2}),\qquad \kappa\in\{1,1/2\}.
\]
Both scaled covers hold modulo \(w\), and the \((\rho,X)\)-Jacobian
determinant is the nonzero integer
\[
 -16AX\rho\,(X^2+\rho^2-4B\kappa^2)=-2400.
\]
Reduction modulo every odd \(w\ge7\) is smooth, so Hensel's lemma
supplies a \(w\)-adic point.  At \(w=3,5\) the completed bounded scans
find mod-\(w\) points but no smooth point, so this streamlined proof has
a named small-prime exception; it does not prove target local emptiness.
This is not the L24 low-positive aligned stratum, and compatibility with
a preselected L20 residue class remains separate.

% src: target-residue-scan rows)

Over \(\mathbb R\), the two guarded bridge samples
\[

\]
have \(A_2>0\), \(L_2<0\), and a nonnegative discriminant, hence a
positive root \(u\); thus \(u\) and \(A+u\) are positive reals.  These
are named certificates, not an all-parameter real theorem.  The crucial

\subsection{Two new non-diagonal constructions}

The preceding scalings do not exhaust the available zero-cost
specializations.  Two further constructions sharpen different sides of
the frontier.

\begin{theorem}[Reciprocal even-pullback tie; PROVED]
\label{thm:reciprocaltie}
Put \(Z=z^2\), \(D=1-Z-a^2Z^2\), and tie the third block coordinate to
\[
 \eta=\frac{Z(b+1)}{Db}.
\]
On the canonical bridge domain \(b\ne0,1\), this adds no guard beyond
the existing guards.  If
\[
 M=16-\delta_\tau c^2-32Ab\eta^2,\qquad
 P_{\rm rec}=D^2A^2b^4M,
\]
then
\[
 P_{\rm rec}(b)=b^4T_\tau(b+b^{-1}),
\]
where
\[
 T_\tau(u)=16A^2D^2-\delta_\tau a^4Z^4
 [16a^4-A(u-2)^2]^2-32A^3Z^2(u+2)
\]
has degree \(4\).  On \(\tau^\dagger\), \(P_{\rm rec}\) is a
\(\Q_2\)-square throughout \(\Phi\), and every standard guarded W1
target lifts.  Away from the fixed \(b=-1\) primes,
\[
 \leg{2b}{p}=\leg{2(u+2)}{p}.
\]
\end{theorem}
% src: data/l26_reciprocal_tie.jsonl

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
The identity follows from
\[
 N_g(b)=b^2[16a^4-A(b+b^{-1}-2)^2]
\]
and \(b^3(b+1)^2=b^4(b+b^{-1}+2)\).
For \(r=v_2(Z)\), one has \(v_2(D)=0\) for \(r\ge0\) and \(2r\) for
\(r<0\); hence \(v_2(\eta)\ge1\), \(v_2(c)\ge4\), and
\(M/16\in1+8\Z_2\).  At a standard odd target,
\(v_w(\eta)=v_w(Z)-1\ge1\), \(v_w(c)=2v_w(Z)-2\ge2\), and
\(M\equiv16\pmod w\).  Finally
\((u+2)=(b+1)^2/b\) proves the character identity.
\end{proof}

If \(T_\tau\) is irreducible with root \(\theta\), the character lives
in the quartic algebra \(\Q(\theta)\); only when
\(2(\theta+2)\) is nonsquare is the associated quadratic extension
nontrivial.  Neither hypothesis is uniform here.  A single guarded
specialization has irreducible octic and trace quartic reductions modulo
\(37\), which is EVIDENCE only.

\begin{theorem}[One-piece aligned shear; PROVED local theorem]
\label{thm:triangularshear}
On the square branch \(X^2-\rho^2=A\), the fixed shear
\[
 (y,r)=(2X+28\rho,\ sX+\rho)
\]
has determinant \(2(1-14s)\), nonzero on \(\Phi\).  It has a
\(\Q_2\)-point for every \(s\in\Z_2\).  Moreover, on the standard
aligned target stratum
\[
 v_w(B)=1,\qquad \leg{A}{w}=-1,\qquad v_w(c)\ge4,
\]
it has a smooth \(\Q_w\)-point for every odd prime \(w\).
\end{theorem}
% src: data/l27_triangular_shear.jsonl

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
With \(\rho=2t\) and \(X(t)^2=A+4t^2\), the normalized equation is
\[
 788t^4+56t^3X+
 \left(A-4B(s^2+1)-\frac{c^2}{4A}\right)t^2
 -4BsXt-1=0.
\]
At \(t=1\) its value is \(0\bmod8\), while its derivative has
valuation exactly \(1\); strong Hensel applies uniformly in \(s\).

At an odd target, put \(x=\rho^2\) and
\(\rho(2X+28\rho)=4\epsilon\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\).  Then
\[
 N_{0,\epsilon}=195x^2-56\epsilon x+4=Ax,\qquad
 N_{1,\epsilon}=N_{0,\epsilon}-x=(A-1)x.
\]
For \(\epsilon=1\), the indicator
\[
 \frac{(1+\chi(x))(1+\chi(N_1))(1-\chi(N_0))}{8}
\]
and the Weil bounds for two cubic, one quartic and one quintic character
sum give at least
\[
 \frac{w-11\sqrt w-26}{8}>0
\]
smooth residues for \(w\ge197\).  The correction \(26\) removes the
two possible singular \(x\)-values and the zero-root weights.  Exact
exhaustion over both signs supplies a nonzero-Jacobian row for all
\(43\) odd primes below \(197\).
\end{proof}

The exact \(\lambda=X+\rho\) eliminant has degree \(8\), leading
coefficient \(225A\), and constant coefficient \(169A^5\).  The guarded
sample \((a,b,Z)=(1,-1,-10)\) supplies one real point by a sign change.
No rational root of the global degree-eight cover, member theorem,
five-count, or unconditional record follows.  L26 and L27 change the
algebraic and local frontiers only.

Two negative or delimiting findings carry structural content.

\begin{theorem}[The canonical $5$-wall; PROVED]
\label{thm:fivewall}
On the canonical escape route ($z=-w$, $a=1$, $\tau=3/5$, $f=w$), write
$e = v_5(D)$. Then $v_5(x_0) = -3-2e$ is odd and $v_5(d_0)=0$, so
\[
   \mathrm{Hilb}_5 \;=\; -\leg{w}{5}\leg{q_1}{5},
   \qquad
   \mathrm{Hilb}_{q_1} \;=\; \leg{5}{q_1} ,
\]
and both equal $+1$ for some admissible $q_1$ if and only if
$\leg{w}{5} = -1$. Since the L11 route requires a root of
$1+4r^2 \equiv 0 \pmod w$, i.e.\ $w \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, the canonical
protocol is obstructed exactly for
\[
   w \equiv 3 \pmod 4 \ \text{ and } \ \leg{w}{5} = +1
   \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
   w \equiv 11, 19 \pmod{20}.
\]

\end{theorem}

The criterion was audited on all $53$ primes $w \in [101,400]$ with both
character branches of $q_1$ covered: $26$ cells have the L11 route, $13$
the canonical escape route, $14$ are obstructed, and there were zero
identity or classification mismatches.

The wall is a statement about the canonical parameter, not about the cells.
In fact it is always breakable on the class side.

\begin{theorem}[Every canonical $5$-wall is breakable; PROVED]
\label{prop:break}
Let $w\equiv11,19\pmod{20}$ be prime and set $z=-w$, $f=w$.  There is an
odd integer $a$ such that the $\tau=0$ fixed-factor family has an aligned
class for infinitely many positive primes $q_1$.  This theorem concerns
class existence; it does not assert an emergent-free member.  If $a$ is
fixed to $7$, the class-side break exists exactly when
$\leg{w}{197}=-1$.
\end{theorem}
% src: data/l19_tauzero.jsonl (criterion and always-breakable theorem rows)

\begin{proof}
Because $w\equiv3\pmod4$, the character sum
$\sum_{r\bmod w}\leg{1+4r^2}{w}=-1$ has no zero terms.  Exactly
$(w+1)/2$ residues therefore give character $-1$; choose one for $a\bmod
w$.  By CRT also impose
$a\equiv0\pmod{\operatorname{rad}(w^3+2)}$ and choose an odd lift.  For
$A=1+4a^2$, $Z=-w^3$, and $D=1-Z-a^2Z^2$, the identity
\[
 4D\equiv(Z-2)^2\pmod p\qquad(p\mid A)
\]
then gives $\gcd(A,D)=1$ while preserving $\leg{A}{w}=-1$.

At every prime occurring to odd order in $A$, prescribe
$\leg{-2\varepsilon wq_1}{p}=+1$; impose the exact $2$-adic class
$\varepsilon wq_1\equiv a\pmod4$; and make
$-2A\varepsilon wq_1$ a square at each of $3,5,7$ not dividing $A$.
These independent local conditions define a reduced CRT class, so
Dirichlet gives infinitely many primes $q_1$ in it.  After finitely many
exclusions and a real-sign threshold, every frozen, moving, fixed-factor,
and infinite symbol is $+1$, and the exponent lemma freezes the resulting
progression.  The factor-by-factor identity
\[
 \prod_{p\mid A}(x,d)_p\,\leg{A}{q_1}
   =\leg{-2\varepsilon}{A}\leg{w}{A}
\]
shows that for odd $a$ the break criterion is $\leg{w}{A}=-1$.
Putting $a=7$ gives $A=197$ and the stated fixed-parameter criterion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Composite $w$]
Composite $w$ is not admissible in the architecture: $w$ indexes a
place, and the residue-field construction has no meaning for composite
modulus --- the break occurs already at the field constructor. The
downstream arithmetic does generalize once $w$ is decomposed into
genuine prime places; as an illustration the pseudo-cell
