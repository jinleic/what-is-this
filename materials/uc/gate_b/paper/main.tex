\title{A Tensorization Obstruction to a Scalar\\
Closure-Defect Repair for a Union-Closed Entropy Functional}
\author{Research draft}
\date{25 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We analyze a scalar repair proposed for a Shapley-averaged entropy functional
arising in an approach to the union-closed sets conjecture.  For a finite set
family $\F$, let $A_+(\F)$ combine the iid OR-entropy and an optimized one-sided
causal Bellman coupling, and let $\eps_\vee(\F)$ be the probability that the
union of two independent uniform members leaves $\F$.  The question was
whether $-A_+(\F)/\eps_\vee(\F)$ is universally bounded over cap-$2/5$,
Reimer-admissible families.  We prove that it is not.  A 45-member normalized
family on seven coordinates has a certified value $A_+<-7/250$.  Both iid and
optimized causal costs are additive under Cartesian products, whereas closure
success is multiplicative.  Its $k$-fold powers are normalized and preserve
the cap and Reimer conditions, but their repair ratios exceed $7k/250$.
A standalone 256-bit Arb verifier certifies the sharper base value, directly
checks the 2,025-member square, and evaluates the actual square Bellman
recursion for two contrasting global orders.  Independently, a standard-library
160-bit dyadic checker relaxes every nondegenerate action interval to $[0,1]$
and still proves $A_+<-1/40$, without clamp decisions or Arb.  The same
dyadic primitive certifies a separate 25-member family on six coordinates,
evaluating all 720 orders without a symmetry quotient and proving
$A_+<-1/80$.  Its Cartesian powers have repair ratio greater than $k/80$.
These two constructions rule out every fixed scalar multiple of this
ordered-pair defect.
They neither produce a union-closed counterexample nor settle a separate
small-defect stability question.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and definitions}

Let $\F\subseteq2^{[n]}$ be a nonempty finite simple family, $m=|\F|$, and
let all random members be uniform on $\F$.  The admissibility conditions are
\begin{align}
 \max_{i\in[n]}|\{A\in\F:i\in A\}|&\le \left\lfloor\frac{2m}{5}\right\rfloor,
 \label{eq:cap}\\
 \sum_{A\in\F}|A|&\ge \frac{m\log_2m}{2}.
 \label{eq:reimer}
\end{align}
A family is called normalized here when every coordinate column is nonzero and
the columns are pairwise distinct.  Normalization is not required in the
supremum below, but all members of our sequence satisfy it.

Fix a coordinate order $\pi$.  At coordinate $i$ and predecessor prefix $u$,
write
\[
 p_i(u)=\Prob[X_i=1\mid X_{\operatorname{pred}_\pi(i)}=u].
\]
For two independent prefixes $u,v$, define
\[
 Q_\pi(\F)=\sum_i\E_{u,v}
 h\bigl(p_i(u)+p_i(v)-p_i(u)p_i(v)\bigr),
\]
where $h$ is binary entropy in bits, and put $Q(\F)=\E_\pi Q_\pi(\F)$.

For the causal term, at a pair of prefixes let $p=p_i(u)$ and $r=p_i(v)$.
Set
\[
 s^*(p,r)=\max\{p,r,\min(p+r,1/2)\},\qquad U(p,r)=\min(1,p+r).
\]
A one-sided action chooses $s\in[s^*,U]$, with next-bit probabilities
\[
 P_{00}=1-s,\quad P_{10}=s-r,\quad
 P_{01}=s-p,\quad P_{11}=p+r-s.
\]
The fixed-order Bellman value $C_{+,\pi}(\F)$ is the maximum expected sum of
$h(s)$ over all such causal policies.  We average over all orders to obtain
$C_+(\F)$.  The coefficient is the fixed, dimension-independent rational
\[
 \alpha=0.0356069=\frac{356069}{10^7},
\]
and the functional is
\begin{equation}
 A_+(\F)=(1-\alpha)Q(\F)+\alpha C_+(\F)-\log_2m.
 \label{eq:Aplus}
\end{equation}

The ordered-pair closure defect is
\begin{equation}
 \eps_\vee(\F)=
 \Prob_{X,Y\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\F)}[X\cup Y\notin\F].
 \label{eq:defect}
\end{equation}
The Gate B quantity is
\begin{equation}
 c_{\rm cl}^\star=
 \sup_{\substack{\F\ \text{satisfies \eqref{eq:cap}, \eqref{eq:reimer}}\\
                  A_+(\F)<0}}
 \frac{-A_+(\F)}{\eps_\vee(\F)}.
 \label{eq:cstar}
\end{equation}
The quotient is interpreted in the extended nonnegative reals: an admissible
family with $A_+(\F)<0$ and $\eps_\vee(\F)=0$ has value $+\infty$; otherwise
the quotient is taken only at positive defect.  Our construction has strictly
positive defect, so the theorem is independent of this convention.
No upper bound on $\eps_\vee$ occurs in \eqref{eq:cstar}.

\section{Certified negative base}

Partition the seven coordinates as $\{5,6\}\sqcup\{0,1,2,3,4\}$ and let $\B$
be the union of the seven weight cells
\begin{equation}
 (0,1),(0,3),(1,0),(1,2),(1,5),(2,0),(2,1).
 \label{eq:cells}
\end{equation}
The exact row-mask list is stored in the accompanying machine-readable
candidate.  Direct counting gives
\[
 |\B|=45,\qquad d_i=18\quad(0\le i<7),\qquad
 \sum_{A\in\B}|A|=126.
\]
Thus the cap holds with equality.  The exact Reimer threshold is 124.  The
columns are active and pairwise distinct.  There are exactly 1,600 missing
ordered join pairs, and hence
\begin{equation}
 \eps_\vee(\B)=\frac{64}{81},\qquad
 1-\eps_\vee(\B)=\frac{17}{81}.
 \label{eq:base-defect}
\end{equation}
The family is not union-closed: rows 1 and 2 occur but row 3 does not.

\begin{proposition}[Certified base]
For the functional \eqref{eq:Aplus},
\begin{equation}
 A_+(\B)
 \le -0.0286491867944683178160269789255044875\ldots
 <-\frac7{250}.
 \label{eq:base-negative}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\noindent
The proposition is a finite computational lemma.  A standalone checker
reconstructs $\B$ from the cell predicate in \eqref{eq:cells}, verifies that it
equals the stored row list, rebuilds the exact constraints and the 240-element
automorphism group, reduces the 5,040 coordinate orders to 21 equal-size
orbits, and produces a rigorous upper certificate for the one-sided Bellman
maximum with 256-bit Arb arithmetic. At each of 982 clamp-ambiguous states the
unconstrained concave maximum is retained, which upper-bounds the constrained
maximum. The checker imports no implementation module from the original
discovery code.

\begin{proposition}[Independent clamp-free certificate]
There is a standard-library-only, outward-rounded dyadic certificate that
proves
\[
 A_+(\B)
 <-0.0271742911034863966862278304907
 <-\frac1{40}.
\label{eq:independent-base}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed order, define a relaxed backward value $W$ as follows.  At a
nondegenerate state, replace the feasible one-sided action interval by all of
$[0,1]$.  Given child values $W_{ac}$, put
\[
 D=-W_{00}+W_{10}+W_{01}-W_{11}.
\]
The unrestricted concave maximum is
\[
 W_{00}-rW_{10}-pW_{01}+(p+r)W_{11}
 +\log_2(1+2^D).
\]
At a degenerate state the action is forced and the boundary recurrence is
used.  Backward induction gives $C_{+,\pi}\le W_\pi$: before enlarging the
action interval, all four feasible transition coefficients are nonnegative,
so replacing each true child by an upper child preserves the inequality;
maximizing over a superset can only increase it.

The checker reconstructs $\B$ from \eqref{eq:cells}, computes $Q$ directly by
the predecessor-subset Shapley formula, and exactly partitions all 5,040
orders into the 21 equal orbits of the verified 240-element automorphism
group.  It obtains the outward upper endpoints
\[
 Q(\B)<5.447354977380321988160699884419,\qquad
 \E_\pi W_\pi<5.933885055704082998831935023544,
\]
which imply \eqref{eq:independent-base}.  Natural logarithms use a rational
atanh series with an exact tail bound; exponentials use range reduction, a
rational Taylor tail bound, and repeated squaring.  Thus no floating-point
library or Arb operation enters this second certificate.
\end{proof}

\section{Tensorization}

For families on disjoint coordinate blocks define
\[
 \F\boxprod\G=\{A\cup(n+B):A\in\F,\ B\in\G\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Closure success]
\begin{equation}
 1-\eps_\vee(\F\boxprod\G)
 =(1-\eps_\vee(\F))(1-\eps_\vee(\G)).
 \label{eq:defect-product}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A product join lies in the product family if and only if both block joins lie
in their respective families.  Under two independent uniform product rows,
the two events are independent.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cost tensorization]
\begin{equation}
 Q(\F\boxprod\G)=Q(\F)+Q(\G),\qquad
 C_+(\F\boxprod\G)=C_+(\F)+C_+(\G).
 \label{eq:cost-product}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a global coordinate order $\pi$ and write $\pi_F,\pi_G$ for its induced
relative block orders.  A uniform product row has independent blocks.
Conditioning on predecessor coordinates in the other block therefore does not
alter a within-block conditional-one probability.  Each iid local term is the
corresponding factor term, giving
\[
 Q_\pi(\F\boxprod\G)=Q_{\pi_F}(\F)+Q_{\pi_G}(\G).
\]

For the optimized causal term, split every pair of product prefixes into its
two block states.  We prove by backward induction on the remaining global
suffix that the Bellman value decomposes as $V=V_F+V_G$.  The terminal case is
immediate.  Suppose the next coordinate belongs to the $F$ block.  Its
conditional marginals, action interval, and transition probabilities depend
only on the $F$ prefixes.  By induction, every child value is its $F$-child
value plus the same unchanged constant $V_G$.  For every feasible action,
\[
 \sum_{a,c}P_{ac}
 =(1-s)+(s-r)+(s-p)+(p+r-s)=1,
\]
and hence
\[
 \max_s\left\{h(s)+\sum_{a,c}P_{ac}(V_{F,ac}+V_G)\right\}
 =V_G+\max_s\left\{h(s)+\sum_{a,c}P_{ac}V_{F,ac}\right\}.
\]
The common shift also cancels from
$-V_{00}+V_{10}+V_{01}-V_{11}$, so it cannot change the optimizer.  The
remaining maximization is exactly the $F$ Bellman step.  The other block is
symmetric.  In particular, a policy cannot gain by
using other-block prefixes: their continuation contribution cancels from the
Bellman slope and hence from the optimizer.  Thus
\[
 C_{+,\pi}(\F\boxprod\G)
 =C_{+,\pi_F}(\F)+C_{+,\pi_G}(\G).
\]
A uniform global order induces uniform relative orders in each block.
Averaging proves \eqref{eq:cost-product}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\begin{equation}
 A_+(\F\boxprod\G)=A_+(\F)+A_+(\G).
 \label{eq:A-product}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Combine \eqref{eq:cost-product} with
$\log_2|\F\boxprod\G|=\log_2|\F|+\log_2|\G|$ in \eqref{eq:Aplus}.
\end{proof}

\section{Unbounded repair ratio}

Let $\F_k=\B^{\boxprod k}$.  Then $|\F_k|=45^k$ and the dimension is $7k$.
Every coordinate occurs in
\[
 18\cdot45^{k-1}=\frac25\,45^k
\]
rows, so the cap holds with equality.  The total incidence is
$126k45^{k-1}$ and the average row size is $14k/5$.  Since
\[
 45^5=184{,}528{,}125<268{,}435{,}456=2^{28},
\]
we have $\log_2 45<28/5$, and \eqref{eq:reimer} holds strictly for every $k$.
Activity and separation within a block are inherited from $\B$; columns in
different blocks are distinct because base columns take both values and blocks
vary independently.  Hence every $\F_k$ is normalized.

Equations \eqref{eq:base-defect}, \eqref{eq:defect-product}, and
\eqref{eq:A-product} give
\[
 \eps_\vee(\F_k)=1-\left(\frac{17}{81}\right)^k,
 \qquad A_+(\F_k)=kA_+(\B).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Gate B]
The quantity in \eqref{eq:cstar} is infinite.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:base-negative},
\[
 \frac{-A_+(\F_k)}{\eps_\vee(\F_k)}
 >\frac{7k/250}{1-(17/81)^k}
 >\frac{7k}{250}\longrightarrow+\infty.
\]
Every member of the sequence is admissible, so the supremum is infinite.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A second base}

The conclusion does not rely on the seven-coordinate search output.  Partition
six coordinates into two triples and let $\mathcal D$ be the union of the weight cells
\[
 (0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,2),(2,1).
\]
It has 25 rows, every coordinate count is 10, total incidence 60, and exact
Reimer threshold 59.  Exactly 444 of its 625 ordered joins are missing, so its
closure-success probability is $181/625$.  A third-party-free 160-bit dyadic
checker evaluates the clamp-free relaxation for all $6!=720$ orders, without
an automorphism quotient, and certifies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q(\mathcal D)&<4.616383859857110260038923460896,\\
 \mathbb E_\pi W_\pi(\mathcal D)&<5.034353951629763221689815640761,\\
 A_+(\mathcal D)&<-0.0125897106568747589615082453240<-\frac1{80}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For $\mathcal G_k=\mathcal D^{\boxprod k}$, the product identities give
\[
 A_+(\mathcal G_k)=kA_+(\mathcal D),\qquad
 \eps_\vee(\mathcal G_k)=1-\left(\frac{181}{625}\right)^k.
\]
The cap holds with equality.  The average row size is $12k/5$, and
$25^5<2^{24}$ proves the strict Reimer inequality.  Hence this second sequence
is normalized and admissible and independently gives
\[
 \frac{-A_+(\mathcal G_k)}{\eps_\vee(\mathcal G_k)}
 >\frac{k/80}{1-(181/625)^k}>\frac{k}{80}\longrightarrow+\infty.
\]

\section{Computational controls}

The proof uses computation only to establish the strictly negative finite base.
The standalone verifier additionally performs the following adversarial checks.

\begin{enumerate}
\item It explicitly constructs the 2,025-row square and counts 3,920,000
missing ordered joins, obtaining
$\eps_\vee(\F_2)=6272/6561=1-(17/81)^2$.
\item It evaluates the actual square-family Bellman recursion in an exact
factored-fiber representation for consecutive and alternating global orders.
Both Arb enclosures agree with twice the base-order optimum, with residual
radius below $1.4\times10^{-70}$.  The alternating computation visits
2,985,831 states and encounters 405,641 ambiguous clamps, retaining the
unconstrained upper bound at each.
\item It records $\alpha=356069/10^7$ independently of dimension and checks the
cap, incidence, Reimer witness, and exact defect arithmetic for powers
$1\le k\le8$.  The all-$k$ conclusion is the proof above, not extrapolation
from these instances.
\item A separate regression suite attacks the fixed-order product identity for
all 225 ordered pairs of nonempty two-coordinate families and all 24 global
orders of each product: 5,400 interleaved Bellman comparisons.  It checks the
iid and one-sided identities with the legacy float implementation and the
defect identity exactly.  It also compares the independent Arb and legacy
float Bellman evaluators and brute-checks the $n=8$ order quotient.
\item A non-factored audit materializes the same square as a plain list of
2,025 fourteen-bit masks and runs generic prefix-fiber evaluators on it, with
no product-aware shortcut, for five declared global orders: consecutive,
block-swapped, reversed, alternating, and a seeded interleaving.  The directly
computed $C_{+,\pi}$ and $Q_\pi$ match the induced block sums to float64
rounding, worst gaps $8.9\times10^{-16}$ and $3.6\times10^{-15}$, while the
values take three distinct magnitudes.
\item A resumable falsification search attacks additivity on 292 ordered
products, 205 with unequal block dimensions, including dead columns, duplicate
columns, singletons, full power sets, middle layers, and seeded random pairs,
over every global order of each product.  It found no counterexample; the worst
gap is $1.8\times10^{-15}$ and the exact ordered-defect product never failed.
\item The shared algebra is verified symbolically: the four transition weights
sum identically to one, the step objective equals
$\text{const}+sD+h(s)$ so a common additive shift of the continuation values
leaves $D$ unchanged, and
$\max_s\{sD+h(s)\}=\log_2(1+2^D)$ at $s=1/(1+2^{-D})$ with second derivative
$1/(s(s-1)\ln2)<0$.
\item The powers themselves are instantiated for $k\le3$ and re-audited from
the constructed rows.  At $k=3$ the family has 91,125 members, all 21
coordinate counts equal the cap bound 36,450, incidence 765,450 exceeds the
Reimer threshold 750,668, the columns are active and pairwise distinct, and a
brute-force count over all $8{,}303{,}765{,}625$ ordered pairs finds
$8{,}227{,}000{,}000$ missing joins, i.e. exactly
$526528/531441=1-(17/81)^3$.  That count uses no product shortcut.
\end{enumerate}

Complete smaller-dimension controls remain separate from the theorem.  The
full labeled $n=4$ scan (which also contains lower-dimensional families via
zero columns) retained 358 nontrivial cap-$2/5$ families.  An exact $n=5$
producer represented all 463,343 nontrivial admissible labeled families by
5,172 coordinate orbits; a resumable 120-order evaluation found minimum
$A_+=0.006474313148642441\ldots$.  At $n=6$, exhaustive coverage is feasible
only for declared subclasses: all 262,585 admissible families with
$3\le|\F|\le7$ (1,081 orbits), and all 450 orbits in the three two-block
classes.  Enumeration and defects in these controls are exact; their objective
comparisons are float64.  None is used to infer the all-$k$ result.

A complete declared-class $n=8$ search provides a finite control.  It exhausts
all 65,535 families invariant under $S_1\times S_7$, finds 136 admissible full
$S_8$ orbits (130 normalized), and finds two negative values.  Its largest
float64 ratio is $0.034335490331443\ldots$, below the certified seven-coordinate
base.  This is a complete numerical result for that symmetry class, not an
arbitrary-$n=8$ optimum.

\section{Scope and limitations}

The sequence satisfies $\eps_\vee(\F_k)\to1$.  This is no limitation for the
unrestricted supremum \eqref{eq:cstar}, but it means the argument does not
settle a different local-stability problem restricted to
$\eps_\vee\to0$.  Approximate-union-closure results such as
Chase--Lovett~\cite{ChaseLovett} concern that small-defect regime and frequency
bounds, not the present Bellman functional.

The base is deliberately non-union-closed.  The theorem neither refutes the
union-closed sets conjecture nor supplies the UC-specific structural charge
needed by the larger entropy programme.  Its conclusion is narrower and
complete: no fixed scalar multiple of the ordered-pair closure defect can
repair $A_+$ over all cap/Reimer families.

There are three verified finite-base certificates covering two different
families, but they are ordinary interval computations rather than
proof-assistant artifacts, and the tensorization lemmas are human-audited
mathematics supported by the falsification searches above.  The
seven-coordinate sign is established by two arithmetically disjoint checkers:
a 256-bit Arb computation with clamp classification, and a standard-library
dyadic computation that avoids clamp decisions entirely.  The second dyadic
certificate uses the six-coordinate family, evaluates all 720 orders without
symmetry reduction, and needs no third-party package.
