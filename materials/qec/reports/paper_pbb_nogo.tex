\begin{abstract}
Perturbed bivariate-bicycle (PBB) codes are non-CSS stabilizer codes obtained from a CSS
bivariate-bicycle (BB) parent $P$ by adding a $Z$-type perturbation $[C\;D]$ to the
$X$-check block. They have been proposed as a route past the CSS BB rate--distance
envelope, on the strength of a published catalogue of $368$ codes including a non-CSS
$\nk{144,12,12}$ and a $\nk{360,12,\le 24}$ candidate.

We prove that the perturbation degree of freedom is not free. Let
$\Delta=\{\lambda[C\;D] : \lambda[A;B]=0\}$ be the \emph{dressing space} of the perturbation.
We show (i) $\Delta$ is an $R$-submodule of the $Z$-sector for
$R=\mathbb{F}_2[x,y]/(x^{\ell}-1,y^{m}-1)$, (ii) the PBB dimension obeys the exact
identity $k_Q = k_P - \dim\bar\Delta$, and (iii) any minimum-weight $Z$-logical of the
parent that is \emph{not} absorbed by $\Delta$ survives as a logical of $Q$, certifying
$d_Q \le d_Z(P)$.

Because $\Delta$ is a submodule, absorbing one minimum-weight parent logical absorbs its
entire translation orbit. This upgrades the per-perturbation criterion to a
\textbf{parent-level no-go theorem}: with
$T(P)=\dim\big(M(P)+S_Z\big)/S_Z$, where $M(P)$ is the span of the translation orbits of
\emph{all} minimum-weight $Z$-logicals of $P$,
\[
\boxed{\;d_Q > d_Z(P)\ \Longrightarrow\ k_Q \le k_P - T(P)\quad\text{for \emph{every} perturbation } [C\;D]\;}
\]
$T(P)$ depends only on the parent and is computed \emph{exactly} --- not bounded --- by a SAT
enumeration that terminates in a certified UNSAT. When $T(P)=k_P$ the entire family is
closed: no PBB over $P$ retains a single logical qubit while exceeding $d_Z(P)$.

We compute $T$ exactly for $139$ of the $202$ distinct parents in the published
catalogue --- exhaustively for every parent at $n\le 144$. \textbf{$63$ parents are family-closed}, including the Gross code
$A=x^3+y+y^2,\;B=y^3+x+x^2$ itself ($T=12=k_P$) and all $11$ distinct catalogue
$\nk{144,12,12}$ parents. This settles $253$ of the $368$ catalogue rows with no per-row
search at all, and it forecloses the headline direction of the construction over the
most important parent in the family.

The bound is not merely valid but \emph{exact}.  A second elementary identity --- the
dressing space is the image of the left kernel of $[A\;B]$, whose dimension is
$k_P/2$ on every BB parent --- caps $\dim\bar\Delta\le k_P/2$.  Whenever
$T(P)\ge k_P/2$, which holds on $138$ of the $139$ certified parents, the two
bounds \textbf{sandwich}:
\[
\boxed{\;d_Q > d_Z(P)\ \text{and}\ T(P)\ge k_P/2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k_Q = k_P - T(P)\ \text{exactly}\;}
\]
Perturbation does not just pay at least $T$ logical qubits for distance; on
every parent in the theorem's class it pays \emph{precisely} $T$, no more and no
less.  All $7$ independently certified distance-increasing perturbations sit on
the law exactly (slack $0$, hypotheses replay-certified), and a controlled
small-lattice probe --- $64$ sampled parents across four lattices, $26{,}898$
valid $\delta>0$ perturbations of $Z$-support $\le 4$, distances decided by
exact symplectic meet-in-the-middle --- found $496$ further
strict increases, \textbf{every one on the law, zero above it}: instances we now
know were theorem-forced rather than lucky.

On the $X$ side the companion collapse mechanism is classified completely. The
directions that \emph{cannot} demote a parent $X$-stabilizer form an invariant of
the single ideal $I=\operatorname{Ann}_R(A,B)$: with $I^\infty$ its stable power,
$\dim S=2\dim I^\infty$, so a parent is demote-full iff $I$ is nilpotent,
demote-immune iff $I$ is idempotent, and mixed iff neither (Theorem J-G) ---
reproducing the $202$-parent classification in $9$\,s with no module
computation, and sharpening it to $I^2=0$ on all $192$ demoting parents and
$I^2=I$ on all $10$ immune ones. If $\ell$ and $m$ are both odd, $R$ is
semisimple and no parent can demote at all: of the seven published BB instances
exactly one, $\nk{90,8,10}$ on the $(15,3)$ lattice, is protected this way.
Splitting $G=\mathbb Z_\ell\times\mathbb Z_m$ into $2$-part and odd part shows
exact vanishing costs two support points per occupied $2$-coset, whence
\textbf{no pair with $\operatorname{wt}(A),\operatorname{wt}(B)\le3$ --- the
entire family used in the literature --- is ever mixed} (Theorem J-I), while
weight $4$ already realizes it: $A=(1+x)(y+y^2)$, $B=Ay$ on $(2,3)$ is mixed
with $240$ of $255$ classes demoting (Theorem J-J, three independent routes). An
exhaustive census of $653{,}022{,}021$ weight-$\le3$ pairs over $18$ lattices
confirms the weight law with zero exceptions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and conventions}\label{sec:conventions}

Let $R=\mathbb{F}_2[x,y]/(x^{\ell}-1,\,y^{m}-1)$ and let $n=2\ell m$. A polynomial
$f\in R$ acts as an $\ell m\times \ell m$ binary matrix via the commuting cyclic shifts
$x,y$. For $A,B\in R$ the CSS \textbf{bivariate-bicycle} parent is
\[
H_X=[\,A\;\;B\,],\qquad H_Z=[\,B^{\mathsf T}\;\;A^{\mathsf T}\,],
\]
on $n$ qubits split into two blocks of $\ell m$. Commutation is
$H_XH_Z^{\mathsf T}=AB+BA=0$, automatic since $R$ is commutative.

A \textbf{perturbed bivariate-bicycle} (PBB) code adds a $Z$-type perturbation $[C\;D]$,
$C,D\in R$, to the $X$-check block:
\[
H_Q=\begin{pmatrix} A & B & \big| & C & D\\[2pt] 0 & 0 & \big| & B^{\mathsf T} & A^{\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix},
\]
in the binary symplectic representation $(x\,|\,z)$ with symplectic product
$\langle (x|z),(x'|z')\rangle_s = xz'^{\mathsf T}+zx'^{\mathsf T}$. Validity requires
$M+M^{\mathsf T}=0$ with $M=AC^{\mathsf T}+BD^{\mathsf T}$, i.e. \textbf{symmetry of $M$}, not
its vanishing. (This is stated as $AC^{\mathsf T}+BD^{\mathsf T}=0$ in some summaries; the
weaker symmetric condition is the correct one and is what we verify.)

Throughout, $S_Z=\operatorname{rowspace}(H_Z)\subseteq\mathbb{F}_2^{\,n}$ is the parent's
pure-$Z$ stabilizer space, $k_P=n-\operatorname{rank}H_X-\operatorname{rank}H_Z$ is the
parent dimension, $k_Q$ the PBB dimension, and
\[
d_Z(P)=\min\{\,\mathrm{wt}(z) : z\in\ker[A\;B]\setminus S_Z\,\}
\]
is the parent's $Z$-distance. We write $d_Q$ for the (symplectic) distance of $Q$ and
reserve \emph{code distance} strictly for the algebraic quantity --- never a decoder estimate,
never a circuit or detector-graph distance.

\textbf{Terminology.} $C=D=0$ recovers the CSS parent. We call a catalogue row a \emph{reversal}
when $d_Q > d_Z(P)$ is certified, and say the perturbation is \emph{dominated} when
$d_Q \le d_Z(P)$ is certified.

\section{The dressing space and the dimension identity}\label{sec:dressing}

Define the \textbf{dressing space}
\[
\Delta \;=\; \big\{\, \lambda[C\;D] \;:\; \lambda\in\mathbb{F}_2^{\,\ell m},\ \lambda[A;B]=0 \,\big\} \;\subseteq\; \mathbb{F}_2^{\,n},
\]
the set of $Z$-parts of those first-block row combinations whose $X$-part cancels, and
write $\bar\Delta=(\Delta+S_Z)/S_Z$.

\begin{theoremG}
For every parent $P$ and every valid perturbation $[C\;D]$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(Centralizer invariance.)} The pure-$Z$ centralizer of $Q$ equals that of $P$, namely
   $\ker[A\;B]$.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(Dimension identity.)} The pure-$Z$ stabilizer group of $Q$ is $S_Z+\Delta$, and
   \[
   k_Q \;=\; k_P - \dim\bar\Delta .
   \]
\item[(iii)] \emph{(Survival criterion.)} If some minimum-weight $z\in\ker[A\;B]\setminus S_Z$ satisfies
   $z\notin S_Z+\Delta$, then $(0|z)$ is a nontrivial logical of $Q$ of weight $d_Z(P)$,
   hence $d_Q\le d_Z(P)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theoremG}

\begin{proof}
(i) A vector $(0|z)$ commutes with every second-block row automatically ($Z$
against $Z$). Against a first-block row $(a\,|\,c)$ the symplectic product is $a\cdot z$.
Hence $(0|z)$ is in the centralizer iff $[A\;B]z=0$ --- no dependence on $[C\;D]$.

(ii) A combination $\lambda$ of first-block rows is pure $Z$ iff its $X$-part
$\lambda[A;B]$ vanishes, in which case it contributes exactly its $Z$-part
$\lambda[C\;D]$. Adding the second block gives pure-$Z$ stabilizer space $S_Z+\Delta$.
For the dimension, project the row space onto the $X$-coordinate: the image is
$\operatorname{rowspace}[A\;B]$ for both codes, while the kernel is $\{0\}\times S_Z$
for $H_P$ and $\{0\}\times(S_Z+\Delta)$ for $H_Q$ --- the pure-$Z$ stabilizer space,
by the first part of~(ii).  Hence
$\operatorname{rank}H_Q=\operatorname{rank}H_P+\dim\bar\Delta$, and
$k=n-\operatorname{rank}H$ gives $k_Q=k_P-\dim\bar\Delta$.  (Note that
$\operatorname{rowspace}H_P$ is \emph{not} contained in $\operatorname{rowspace}H_Q$
--- parent $X$-stabilizers are demoted --- so the rank identity, not a containment,
is what makes the count work.)  Equivalently, via the
sector identity valid for every stabilizer code,
$\dim(\mathrm{Xcen}/S_X)=\dim(\mathrm{Zcen}/S_Z)=k$: the unchanged pure-$Z$
centralizer (i) minus the enlarged stabilizers $S_Z+\Delta$ is directly
$k_P-\dim\bar\Delta$.  (The shortcut ``the $X$-sector of the quotient is unchanged,
so only the pure-$Z$ quotient loses dimension'' is \emph{false as an argument}: the
pure-$X$ centralizer also shrinks, $\mathrm{Xcen}(Q)=\mathrm{Xcen}(P)\cap\ker[C\;D]$
--- e.g.\ from dimension $40$ to $12$ on \texttt{phase2\_58} --- and both the rank
identity and the sector identity are what make the count work;
\texttt{notes/theorem\_j\_xsector.md}, J.1--J.3.)

(iii) Immediate from (i) and (ii): $z\in\ker[A\;B]$ by (i), and $z\notin S_Z+\Delta$
means its class is nontrivial in the quotient of (ii).
\end{proof}

Part (iii) is the operational content: \textbf{a single unabsorbed minimum-weight parent
logical caps the PBB distance}, with no search over PBB codewords. Machine checks of
(i)--(iii) on catalogue instances are in \texttt{tests/test\_pbb\_survival.py}; the dimension
identity (ii) holds exactly on all tested catalogue rows (\S\ref{sec:repro}).

\section{From per-perturbation to parent-level: the module structure}\label{sec:module}

The step that makes a family-wide theorem possible is the following.

\begin{lemmaone}[submodule]
$\Delta$ is an $R$-submodule of $\mathbb{F}_2^{\,n}$; so is
$S_Z$, and hence so is $S_Z+\Delta$.
\end{lemmaone}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient set $L=\{\lambda : \lambda[A;B]=0\}$ is an ideal of $R$: if
$\lambda A=\lambda B=0$ then $(x^ay^b\lambda)A = x^ay^b(\lambda A)=0$ and likewise for
$B$, using commutativity of $R$. Since $\Delta=L\cdot[C\;D]$ and multiplication by
$[C\;D]$ is $R$-linear, $\Delta$ is closed under the translations $x^ay^b$, i.e. is a
submodule. $S_Z=\operatorname{rowspace}[B^{\mathsf T}\;A^{\mathsf T}]$ is a submodule for
the same reason.
\end{proof}

\begin{cortwo}[orbit absorption]
If $\Delta$ absorbs a minimum-weight $Z$-logical $z$
(i.e. $z\in S_Z+\Delta$) then it absorbs the entire translation orbit
$\{x^ay^b z\}$ of $z$. Translations preserve weight and map $\ker[A\;B]$ to itself, so
every orbit element is again a minimum-weight $Z$-logical.
\end{cortwo}

Now define, \textbf{from the parent alone},
\[
M(P)=\operatorname{span}\Big(\bigcup\nolimits_{z}\ \mathrm{orbit}(z)\ :\ z \text{ a minimum-weight } Z\text{-logical of } P\Big),\quad T(P)=\dim\frac{M(P)+S_Z}{S_Z}.
\]

\begin{theoremH}[parent-level no-go]
For every parent $P$ and every valid perturbation
$[C\;D]$,
\[
d_Q > d_Z(P)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k_Q \;\le\; k_P - T(P).
\]
In particular, if $T(P)=k_P$ then no perturbation of $P$ retains a logical qubit while
exceeding $d_Z(P)$: the whole family over $P$ is closed.
\end{theoremH}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $d_Q>d_Z(P)$. By Theorem G(iii), contrapositively, \textbf{every}
minimum-weight $Z$-logical $z$ of $P$ must be absorbed: $z\in S_Z+\Delta$. By
Corollary 2 the whole orbit of each such $z$ is absorbed, so
$M(P)\subseteq S_Z+\Delta$, whence $\bar M\subseteq\bar\Delta$ and
$\dim\bar\Delta\ge T(P)$. Theorem G(ii) gives
$k_Q=k_P-\dim\bar\Delta\le k_P-T(P)$. The final sentence is the case $T=k_P$, forcing
$k_Q\le 0$.
\end{proof}

Three features are worth emphasising. First, the hypothesis is \emph{only} a distance
increase --- no assumption on $C,D$, on weight, or on locality. Second, $T(P)$ is a
property of the parent, so one computation covers the infinite family of perturbations
over $P$, including every $[C\;D]$ nobody has enumerated. Third, the conclusion bounds
$k_Q$, not $d_Q$: the theorem does not forbid distance increase, it \emph{prices} it.

\subsection{Computing \texorpdfstring{$T$}{T} exactly}\label{sec:Texact}

$T$ is obtained by a monotone SAT enumeration rather than estimated. Maintain
$W=S_Z+M$, initially $S_Z$, and repeatedly ask a solver for
$z\in\ker[A\;B]$ with $\mathrm{wt}(z)\le d_Z(P)$ and $z\notin W$. Nontriviality with
respect to $W$ is encoded exactly as ordinary logical nontriviality --- pair $z$ against a
basis of $W^{\perp}$, since $\exists f\in W^{\perp}: f\cdot z=1$ iff $z\notin W$. Each
SAT hit is necessarily a \emph{minimum-weight} logical (nothing outside $S_Z$ has weight below
$d_Z$), and its full orbit is adjoined to $M$. The enumeration terminates in UNSAT, which
certifies that $W$ contains every minimum-weight $Z$-logical; therefore the resulting $T$
is \textbf{exact}, not a lower bound. If the budget is exhausted first, the partial $T$ is a
certified \emph{lower} bound --- the sound direction, since Theorem H only needs
$\dim\bar\Delta\ge T$.

Two soundness details are enforced in code. (a) Every SAT witness is re-verified through
two independent GF(2) paths and its weight is checked to equal the supplied
$d_Z(P)$ exactly; a mismatch is raised, never absorbed. (b) A translation symmetry break
(anchor support at index $0$ of one block) is applied to the decision queries. This is
satisfiability-preserving precisely because $\ker[A\;B]$, $S_Z$ and every $M$ built from
full orbits are translation invariant --- the solution set of each query is translation
invariant, so any solution can be translated to the anchored form. The premise is
machine-validated in \texttt{tests/test\_translation\_symmetry\_break.py} (automorphism action,
transitivity, and $30$ differential SAT/UNSAT agreements); it buys $6.2\times$ on the
decisive UNSAT proofs ($77.8$ s $\to$ $12.5$ s on the $\nk{144,12,12}$ cap-$11$ instance).

\subsection{The trade is exact: forced saturation}\label{sec:Texact2}

Theorem H bounds $k_Q$ from above. A second, independent observation bounds
$\dim\bar\Delta$ from above --- and for most parents the two meet.

\begin{lemma2}[left-kernel identity]
For every CSS BB parent, with
$L=\{\lambda:\lambda A=\lambda B=0\}$ the left kernel of $[A\;B]$,
$k_P = 2\dim L$.
\end{lemma2}

\begin{proof}
$\operatorname{rank}H_X=\ell m-\dim L$ ($L$ is the left nullspace of
$H_X$). The left nullspace of $H_Z=[B^{\mathsf T}\,A^{\mathsf T}]$ is the
transpose of $K=\ker A\cap\ker B$, so $\operatorname{rank}H_Z=\ell m-\dim K$,
and $k_P=\dim L+\dim K$. The coordinate-reversal map conjugates each cyclic
shift to its transpose, so $\dim L=\dim K$.
\end{proof}

\emph{(Verified on all $202$ distinct parents, \texttt{tests/test\_pbb\_theorems.py}.)}

\begin{lemma3}[dressing ceiling]
$\Delta=L\cdot[C\;D]$ is the image of a
linear map on $L$, so $\dim\bar\Delta\le\dim L=k_P/2$ for every valid
perturbation.
\end{lemma3}
\emph{(Verified on all $368$ catalogue rows via $\dim\bar\Delta=k_P-k_Q$.)}

\begin{theoremI}[forced saturation]
If $T(P)\ge k_P/2$ and $d_Q>d_Z(P)$, then
$\dim\bar\Delta=T(P)$ and $k_Q=k_P-T(P)$ \textbf{exactly}.
\end{theoremI}

\begin{proof}
Theorem H gives $\dim\bar\Delta\ge T$; Lemma 3 gives
$\dim\bar\Delta\le k_P/2\le T$. Squeeze, then apply Theorem G(ii).
\end{proof}

\textbf{Scope.} $T\ge k_P/2$ holds on $138$ of $139$ certified parents. The single
exception is \texttt{9a7638586033} ($n=144$, $k_P=12$, $T=4$, not family-closed),
where increase is only sandwiched to $4\le\dim\bar\Delta\le 6$. On every other
certified parent the exact trade law is a theorem: a distance increase pays
precisely $T(P)$ logical qubits. This subsumes the empirical saturation of \S\ref{sec:saturation}
--- all $7$ reversals have $T=k_P/2$ --- and, retroactively, explains the
small-lattice probe of \S\ref{sec:conjectures}: its $64$ parents all have $T\in\{k_P/2,k_P\}$, so
its $496$ strict increases could not have landed anywhere but on the law.

\subsection{The demote law is an ideal invariant, and no trinomial pair is
mixed}\label{sec:idealinvariant}

The demote mechanism above is decided by a module computation
($S=I^\infty M$ inside $M=\ker H_X/S_Z$). It is in fact governed by the ideal
alone. Decompose $R=\prod_\beta R_\beta$ into local Artinian factors and recall
$I=L_{\rm pre}=\operatorname{Ann}_R((a,b))$ --- \emph{not} the module
annihilator $\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$ that also appears in Theorem J-E. The two
are not interchangeable: $I$ is full exactly where $a_\beta=b_\beta=0$ and zero
where either polynomial is a unit, whereas $\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)=(\bar
a,\bar b)$ --- $H_X$ acts through transposed circulants --- is full exactly on
the unit factors, dual to $I$ with
$\dim I+\dim\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)=\ell m$.

\paragraph{Theorem J-G (ideal invariance).}
$\dim S=2\dim_{\mathbb F_2}I^\infty$, and for $k_P>0$
\[
\textbf{demote-full}\iff I\ \text{nilpotent},\qquad
\textbf{immune}\iff I^2=I,\qquad
\textbf{mixed}\iff 0\neq I^\infty\neq I,
\]
with $M=0\iff I=0$. \emph{Proof.} $I=\prod I_\beta$ and $M=\bigoplus M_\beta$
split componentwise; $I_\beta=R_\beta$ exactly where $A_\beta=B_\beta=0$, and
there the localized $H_X$ vanishes, so $M_\beta=R_\beta^2$; proper
$I_\beta\subseteq\mathfrak m_\beta$ is nilpotent, so $I^\infty=e_IR$. Theorem
J-E${}''$ gives $S=e_IM=\bigoplus_{\rm full}M_\beta$ of dimension
$2\dim e_IR$. For the immune case, $R=\mathbb F_2[G]$ and each of its blocks is
Frobenius, so
$\dim I_\beta=\dim R_\beta/(A_\beta,B_\beta)=\tfrac12\dim M_\beta$ and
$M_\beta=0\iff I_\beta=0$. $\square$

Machine consequences (EXP-053 classifies the whole catalogue in $8.9$\,s,
replacing the module chains of EXP-052; the dual-ideal audit below adds
$\approx4$ minutes): the ideal route reproduces EXP-052 on \textbf{all
$202$} parents with zero mismatches, and sharpens it --- $I^2=0$ on all $192$
demoting parents (nilpotency index exactly $2$) and $I^2=I$ on all $10$ immune
ones, so the catalogue realizes only the two extremes. The dual ideal is
audited independently: $\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)=(\bar a,\bar b)$ on $202/202$
parents by two unrelated computations (the quotient action on $M$ versus the
ideal generated by the two $H_Z$ blocks), with
$\dim I+\dim\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)=\ell m$ everywhere; the bar is essential,
since $(a,b)$ is bar-invariant on only $156$ of the $202$ parents. The two
annihilators are consequently never interchangeable --- substituting
$\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$ would return $2(\ell m-k_P/2)$ ($100$, $172$, $344$
across the ten immune parents) instead of the correct $\dim S=k_P\in\{8,16\}$,
and would misclassify the mixed witness as demote-full.

\paragraph{Corollary J-G1 (odd lattices).} If $\ell,m$ are both odd then
$x^\ell-1,y^m-1$ are squarefree, $R$ is semisimple, every ideal is idempotent,
and \emph{no} parent admits a single-row syzygy demotion: the collapse mechanism
requires an even lattice dimension ($3{,}600$ parents over nine odd lattices,
zero violations). Of the seven published BB instances exactly one is protected
this way --- $\nk{90,8,10}$ on $(\ell,m)=(15,3)$ is immune, while
$\nk{72,12,6}$, $\nk{108,8,10}$, the Gross $\nk{144,12,12}$, $\nk{288,12,18}$,
$[[360,12,{\le}24]]$ and $[[756,16,{\le}34]]$ all have $I^2=0$, i.e.\ every
nonzero class demotes.

Split $G=\mathbb Z_\ell\times\mathbb Z_m=G_2\times G_{\rm odd}$ into $2$-part
and odd part. Then $\mathbb F_2[G_{\rm odd}]=\prod_\chi F_\chi$ is semisimple
and $\mathbb F_2[G_2]$ is local with nilpotent maximal ideal, so the local
factors of $R$ are $R_\chi=F_\chi[G_2]$, in which $G_2$ is an $F_\chi$-basis.

\paragraph{Theorem J-H (exact-vanishing coset law).} Partition the support of
$a\in R$ by $2$-part, $S_h=\{u\in G_{\rm odd}:hu\in\operatorname{supp}a\}$.
Then $a_\chi=\sum_{h\in G_2}\bigl(\sum_{u\in S_h}\chi(u)\bigr)h$; hence
$a_\chi=0$ iff every coset sum vanishes, $a_\chi$ is a non-unit iff the total
sum vanishes, and $a_\chi=0$ forces $|S_h|\ge2$ on every occupied coset. A
polynomial of weight $\le3$ that vanishes exactly somewhere therefore occupies a
single coset, and is a \emph{scalar} --- zero or unit --- at every factor.

\paragraph{Theorem J-I (weight law).} If $\operatorname{wt}(A)\le3$ and
$\operatorname{wt}(B)\le3$ then $(A,B)$ is \textbf{not mixed}, on any lattice:
mixed needs a factor where both vanish (forcing single cosets, hence
zero-or-unit everywhere) and a factor where both are non-units but not both
zero, which is then impossible. A mixed parent requires weight $\ge4$ spread
over $\ge2$ cosets in one of $A,B$.

This explains the all-or-nothing law without enumeration: the published
catalogue is weight-$(3,3)$. Confirmations: an exhaustive census of
$653{,}022{,}021$ translation-normalised weight-$\le3$ pairs over $18$ lattices
--- every catalogue lattice, the previously underexplored $(12,12)$ and
$(15,12)$, and a parity grid --- finds \textbf{zero} mixed pairs (EXP-054, with
$450$ pairs re-decided by an independent route, zero disagreements); and the
single-coset predicate separates the catalogue \emph{exactly}: all $10$ immune
parents are single-coset, all $192$ demoting parents are not. For $m=6$ this
reads off by eye: immunity iff all $A$-monomials share one $y$-parity and all
$B$-monomials share one $y$-parity.

\paragraph{Theorem J-J (mixed exists; the bound $4$ is attained).} On
$(\ell,m)=(2,3)$, $A=(1+x)(y+y^2)$ and $B=Ay$ give $\dim I=4$,
$\dim I^2=\dim I^\infty=2$: a \textbf{mixed} parent with $k_P=8$, $\dim S=4$ and
exactly $2^8-2^4=240$ of $255$ nonzero classes demoting --- verified by the
ideal chain, by the EXP-052 module chain $[8,4,4]$, and by brute-force
evaluation of all $255$ classes through the EXP-047 rank test. Problem J-A is
resolved affirmatively, while Theorem J-I shows mixed can never occur inside the
trinomial family. Open: the sharp weight/lattice map at weight $\ge4$, and
whether any mixed parent has useful $[[n,k,d]]$ (the witness is a $[[12,8]]$
toy).

\section{The exact trade law, its residual, and remaining conjectures}\label{sec:conjectures}

\begin{theoremI}[restated; proved in \S\ref{sec:Texact2}]
If $T(P)\ge k_P/2$ and $d_Q>d_Z(P)$,
then $k_Q=k_P-T(P)$ exactly. This covers $138$ of $139$ certified parents and
all $57$ family-closed ones at $n\le 144$; the saturation observed in \S\ref{sec:saturation} and
the $496/496$ small-lattice increases are instances of it.
\end{theoremI}

Three layers of machine evidence now sit beneath the theorem and delimit its
residual:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Catalogue upper law.} On all $284$ classified rows,
   $\dim\bar\Delta\le T(P)$ with zero exceptions (spread of
   $\dim\bar\Delta-T$ only non-positive, down to $-24$). For $T\ge k_P/2$
   parents this is Lemma 3; the law holding also on the one $T<k_P/2$ parent's
   rows is evidence the residual class is benign.
\item \emph{Small-lattice probe (EXP-040).} $64$ parents across four lattices,
   $26{,}898$ unseen $\delta>0$ perturbations: $\dim\bar\Delta<T$ on $23{,}634$,
   $=T$ on $3{,}264$, $>T$ on \textbf{zero}; all $496$ strict increases at $=T$, with
   actual containment $\bar M=\bar\Delta$ verified, not just dimensions. All
   probed parents had $T\in\{k_P/2,k_P\}$, so these are theorem-forced.
\item \emph{Reversals.} All $7$ catalogue reversals at $\dim\bar\Delta=T=k_P/2$ with
   $\bar M=\bar\Delta$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{conjBprime}[residual saturation --- decided on the known witness]
For parents with $T<k_P/2$ (one known:
\texttt{9a7638586033}, where increase sandwiches to $4\le\dim\bar\Delta\le 6$),
distance increase still forces $\dim\bar\Delta=T$.
\end{conjBprime}
On the only known residual parent the question is now \emph{decided}, and the
answer is stronger than saturation: increase over \texttt{9a7638586033} is
\emph{impossible}, because the dressing rows of every valid perturbation live in a
fixed two-dimensional space (EXP-045): over its entire valid family ($\dim V=112$,
$2{,}533{,}006{,}645$ instances enumerated exhaustively through weight $6$,
per-weight counts matching $\binom{112}{k}$ exactly),
$\dim\bar\Delta\in\{0,2\}\subset[0,2]<4=T$, so the Theorem-H premise is never met
and $d_Q\le d_Z(P)=6$ for \emph{every} valid $[C\;D]$ of that parent. The sandwich
$[4,6]$ is never attained. Whether the residual class contains any other parent at
larger lattices ($\ell\cdot m>40$) remains open (\S\ref{sec:settle}); on the known
witness the $k$-halving confinement picture is therefore complete with no
exceptional direction.

\begin{conjC}[$k$-halving confinement]
Any perturbation with $d_Q>d_Z(P)$
has $k_Q/k_P=1/2$. On every $T=k_P/2$ parent this is immediate from
Theorem I; the conjecture is residual exactly on $T<k_P/2$ parents and on the
$63$ uncertified ones ($n\in\{180,360\}$). Every one of the $284$ classified
rows with $k_Q/k_P\in\{1,3/4,5/6\}$ is capped \emph{a priori} (\S\ref{sec:confinement}, zero
exceptions).
\end{conjC}

\begin{conjD}[distance parity]
Certified reversals gain exactly $+2$ in
distance.
\end{conjD}
\emph{Evidence:} the uniform gap $d_Q-d_Z(P)=2$ across all $7$ reversals,
whose exact $d_Q$ values are certified; consistent with $d_Z$ even throughout.
(The probe stores only the increase decision, not the achieved $d_Q$, so its
$496$ increases are not parity evidence.)

The open problem that remains is not \emph{whether} perturbation pays --- it pays
exactly $T$ --- but \emph{whether anything on the CSS side of the envelope can ever be
beaten this way}: \S\ref{sec:settle}'s domination table says no for all seven known increases.

\section{Related work and novelty}\label{sec:related}

Bivariate-bicycle codes and the Gross code are due to Bravyi et
al.~\cite{bravyi2024faulttolerant}. The PBB construction and the $368$-row catalogue are
from \cite{cruzbenito2026evolutionary}. Cruz-Benito et al.~\cite{cruzbenito2026evolutionary}
(v1, Sec.~III.2, Lemma~0) give the
validity condition; we derive it independently from the binary symplectic Gram matrix and
confirm the correct condition is \emph{symmetry} of $AC^{\mathsf T}+BD^{\mathsf T}$, not its
vanishing. Multivariate bicycle generalisations are in \cite{voss2024multivariate};
existence and characterisation results for BB codes in
\cite{postema2025existence}. ASC \cite{zhang2026asc} certifies
no-depth-$6$ for IBM BB codes; our depth criterion is independent and basis-independent.

To our knowledge the following are new here: (a) the identification of $\Delta$ as an
$R$-submodule and the resulting orbit-absorption argument; (b) the exact dimension
identity $k_Q=k_P-\dim\bar\Delta$ as a certified structural fact rather than an observed
coincidence; (c) the perturbation-independent no-go bound (Theorem H) and its exact
$T$ enumeration with a terminating UNSAT certificate; (d) the closure of the entire
perturbation family over the Gross code; (e) the saturation observation $k_Q=k_P-T$ on
all certified reversals; and (f) the proved confinement of distance increase to a
$k$-halving class.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The PBB construction's extra freedom is real but not free. It is governed by a single
submodule $\Delta$ whose dimension is exactly the dimension deficit, and whose module
structure forces any distance increase to absorb entire translation orbits of the
parent's minimum-weight logicals. This yields a computable, perturbation-independent
obstruction that closes $63$ of $202$ published parents outright --- the Gross code among
them --- caps $253$ of $368$ catalogue rows with no search, confines distance increase to a
$k$-halving class, and is saturated with slack exactly zero on all $7$ certified
distance-increasing perturbations known to us.

The abstract $\nk{n,k,d}$ route past the CSS bivariate-bicycle envelope is, over these
parents, closed by theorem. What remains genuinely open is the circuit-level question:
whether a verified fault-tolerant syndrome-extraction circuit for a non-CSS PBB can beat
the Gross code end-to-end under identical noise. Nothing in our circuit-level evidence so
far favours it.
