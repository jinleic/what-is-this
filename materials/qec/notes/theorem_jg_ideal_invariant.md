# Theorem J-G/J-H/J-I/J-J — the demote law is an ideal invariant, and the
# trinomial family can never be mixed

**Status (2026-08-21):** all four statements **proved**; every quantitative
claim machine-verified (EXP-053, EXP-054). This note supersedes the open
problem J-A of `theorem_je2_demote_trichotomy.md`: mixed parents **exist**, and
the catalogue's zero-mixed outcome is now a *theorem about weight* $\le 3$,
not an artifact of the search.

## 0. Setting and prior input

$G=\mathbb Z_\ell\times\mathbb Z_m$, $R=\mathbb F_2[G]=\mathbb
F_2[x,y]/(x^\ell-1,y^m-1)$, CSS bivariate-bicycle parent $H_X=[A\;B]$,
$H_Z=[B^{\mathsf T}A^{\mathsf T}]$, $n=2\ell m$,

$$M:=\ker H_X/S_Z \quad (\dim M = k_P),\qquad
I:=L_{\rm pre}=\{\lambda\in R:\lambda A=\lambda B=0\}=\operatorname{Ann}_R(A,B).$$

$I$ is an ideal (Theorem H, Lemma 1) and $M$ is a finitely generated
$R$-module. Two prior results are used as input:

> **Warning — two different annihilators, and a bar.** Theorem J-E is stated
> with *both* $L_{\rm pre}$ and $\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$ ("immune $\iff
> 1\in L_{\rm pre}+\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$"), and they are **not**
> interchangeable. $I=L_{\rm pre}=\operatorname{Ann}_R((a,b))$ is *full* exactly
> on the **zero** factors ($a_\beta=b_\beta=0$, where
> $\operatorname{Ann}(0)=R_\beta$) and *zero* where either polynomial is a unit.
> The module annihilator points the other way: since $H_X$ acts through
> *transposed* circulants, $M_\beta=(R_\beta/(\bar a_\beta,\bar
> b_\beta))^2$ and therefore
> $$\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)=(\bar a,\bar b),$$
> the **reciprocal** ideal — full exactly on the unit factors, zero on the zero
> factors, and a proper *nonzero* ideal on each deficient factor. So
> $\dim\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$ is **not** a "unit-factor dimension"; what is
> true is the Frobenius duality $\dim I+\dim\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)=\dim R=\ell m$.
>
> Theorem J-G's formula $\dim S=2\dim I^\infty$ holds for $I=L_{\rm pre}$
> **only**. Substituting $\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$ puts the stable image on the
> unit factors and returns nonsense — machine-measured in EXP-053's
> `duality_audit`: on the ten immune parents the wrong route gives
> $2(\ell m-k_P/2)\in\{100,172,344\}$ instead of $\dim S=k_P\in\{8,16\}$; on the
> demoting parents a positive number instead of $0$; on the mixed witness it
> reports demote-full instead of mixed.
> Never interchangeable on any of the 202 parents.
>
> Both identities are machine-checked (EXP-053, 202/202 parents plus the
> witness): $\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$, computed from the quotient *action* on
> $M$, equals the ideal generated by the two $H_Z$ blocks (an entirely
> independent computation), and $\dim I+\dim\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)=\ell m$
> everywhere. The bar is not cosmetic: $(a,b)$ itself is bar-invariant on only
> $156$ of the $202$ parents, so comparing $\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$ against
> $(a,b)$ instead of $(\bar a,\bar b)$ fails on the other $46$ — the first
> version of this audit did exactly that and was caught by its own test.
>
> Finally, the zero/deficient/unit factor partition is a statement about *which
> factors occur*, not a dimension identity: its machine proxies are
> $\dim I^\infty$ (zero part) and $\dim I-\dim I^\infty$ (nilpotent part carried
> by the deficient factors), with immune $\iff$ the latter vanishes and
> demote-full $\iff$ the former does — checked on 202/202, and the witness is the
> only instance with both parts nonzero ($2$ and $2$).

* **Theorem I** (ledger): $k_P=2\dim_{\mathbb F_2} I$ (verified 202/202).
* **Theorem J-E″** (proved + audited, `theorem_je2_demote_trichotomy.md`): the
  demote-immune fixed set is
  $S=\{y\in M: y\in Iy\}=e_IM=I^\infty M$, where $I^\infty$ is the stable ideal
  power and $e_I$ the idempotent of $I$'s full-factor support.

## 1. Theorem J-G (ideal invariance of the demote law)

> **Theorem J-G.** $\dim S=2\dim_{\mathbb F_2}I^{\infty}$, and for $k_P>0$
> $$\textbf{demote-full}\iff I\text{ nilpotent},\qquad
>   \textbf{immune}\iff I^2=I,\qquad
>   \textbf{mixed}\iff 0\neq I^\infty\neq I .$$
> Degenerate ($M=0$) $\iff I=0$. In particular the whole trichotomy — and the
> exact demote fraction $1-\frac{2^{\dim S}-1}{2^{k_P}-1}$ — is a function of
> the single ideal $I\subseteq R$; the module $M$ never has to be built.

**Proof.** $R$ is finite commutative, hence Artinian: $R=\prod_\beta R_\beta$
with $R_\beta$ local Artinian, idempotents $e_\beta$. Ideals and finitely
generated modules split componentwise, so $I=\prod_\beta I_\beta$ and
$M=\bigoplus_\beta M_\beta$ with $M_\beta=e_\beta M$ the corresponding object
for the localized pair $(A_\beta,B_\beta)$.

*(a) $I^\infty=e_IR$.* On a factor with $I_\beta=R_\beta$ all powers stay
$R_\beta$; on a factor with $I_\beta\subsetneq R_\beta$ we have
$I_\beta\subseteq\mathfrak m_\beta$, which is nilpotent, so $I_\beta^r=0$
eventually. Finitely many factors ⟹ $I^\infty=\prod_{\rm full}R_\beta=e_IR$.

*(b) Full factors are exactly the vanishing factors.* $I_\beta=R_\beta$ means
$1\in\operatorname{Ann}(A_\beta)\cap\operatorname{Ann}(B_\beta)$, i.e.
$A_\beta=B_\beta=0$; conversely if both vanish then every $\lambda$ annihilates
them. On such a factor $H_X$ localizes to the zero map, so $\ker=R_\beta^2$ and
the local $S_Z$ is $0$: $M_\beta=R_\beta^2$, $\dim M_\beta=2\dim R_\beta$.

*(c) Dimension.* By J-E″, $S=e_IM=\bigoplus_{\rm full}M_\beta$, so
$\dim S=2\sum_{\rm full}\dim R_\beta=2\dim e_IR=2\dim I^\infty$ by (a).

*(d) Cases.* $I^\infty=0$ ⟺ no full factor ⟺ $S=0$. For the immune case use
that $R$ is a **Frobenius algebra** (the form $(a,b)\mapsto$ coefficient of
$1_G$ in $ab$ is nondegenerate and $G$-invariant), hence so is every block
$R_\beta$ — distinct blocks are orthogonal for the form, so its restriction to
each block is nondegenerate. Frobenius duality gives
$\dim\operatorname{Ann}_{R_\beta}(J)=\dim R_\beta-\dim J$ for every ideal
$J\subseteq R_\beta$; applied to $J=(A_\beta,B_\beta)$,
$$\dim I_\beta=\dim R_\beta-\dim (A_\beta,B_\beta)=\dim R_\beta/(A_\beta,B_\beta)
 =\tfrac12\dim M_\beta ,$$
the last equality being the standard BB rank count
$\dim M_\beta=2\dim R_\beta/(A_\beta,B_\beta)$ localized (rank $H_X$ = $\dim$ of
the image ideal, and rank $H_Z$ = the same by transposition). Summing gives
$k_P=2\dim I$ (Theorem I) and, per factor,
$$M_\beta=0\iff I_\beta=0 .$$
Therefore $S=M$ ⟺ every factor with $M_\beta\neq0$ is full ⟺ every factor has
$I_\beta\in\{0,R_\beta\}$ ⟺ $I^\infty=I$ ⟺ $I^2=I$. Everything else is mixed.
$\square$

**Machine verification (EXP-053).** For all **202** catalogue parents the ideal
route reproduces EXP-052's module chain exactly: $k_{\rm pred}=k_P$ and
$\dim S_{\rm pred}=\dim S$ on 202/202, cases 192 demote-full / 10 immune /
0 mixed, **zero mismatches**, total wall time $8.9$ s for the whole catalogue.
Sharpened facts, new here:

* on **all 192** demoting parents the nilpotency index is exactly $2$: $I^2=0$;
* on **all 10** immune parents $I^2=I$ with $\dim I=k_P/2$;
* i.e. the catalogue realizes only the two extremes — square-zero or
  idempotent — never an intermediate ideal.

## 2. Corollary J-G1 (odd lattices admit no demotion at all)

> **Corollary.** If $\ell$ and $m$ are both odd then $x^\ell-1$ and $y^m-1$ are
> squarefree over $\mathbb F_2$, $R$ is semisimple, every ideal is generated by
> an idempotent, so $I^\infty=I$ always. Hence **every** BB parent on an
> odd$\times$odd lattice with $k_P>0$ is demote-immune, and mixed is impossible
> there. The single-row syzygy collapse mechanism of EXP-046/047 *requires* an
> even lattice dimension.

**Machine checks.** 3,600 weight-$\le3$ parents over nine odd lattices
$(3,3),(5,3),(7,3),(5,5),(9,3),(9,5),(15,3),(7,7),(15,5)$: every one immune or
degenerate, **zero violations**. Published baselines, classified by the ideal
route:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| $[[72,12,6]]$ | (6,6) | 6 | $6\to0$ | demote-full |
| $[[90,8,10]]$ | **(15,3)** | 4 | $4\to4$ | **immune** |
| $[[108,8,10]]$ | (9,6) | 4 | $4\to0$ | demote-full |

So of the seven published Bravyi-et-al. instances exactly one — the

## 3. Lemma (local factors) and Theorem J-H (exact-vanishing coset law)

Write $G=G_2\times G_{\rm odd}$ (2-part $\times$ odd part; canonical since
$\mathbb Z_{2^s t}\cong\mathbb Z_{2^s}\times\mathbb Z_t$ for odd $t$).

> **Lemma.** $R=\mathbb F_2[G_{\rm odd}]\otimes\mathbb F_2[G_2]$;
> $\mathbb F_2[G_{\rm odd}]=\prod_\chi F_\chi$ is semisimple (one factor per
> Frobenius orbit of characters $\chi$), and $\mathbb F_2[G_2]$ is **local**
> with nilpotent maximal ideal (its augmentation ideal — $G_2$ is a $2$-group
> in characteristic 2). Hence the local factors of $R$ are
> $$R_\chi=F_\chi[G_2],$$
> with residue field $F_\chi$, nilpotent maximal ideal, and $G_2$ an
> $F_\chi$-basis of $R_\chi$.

> **Theorem J-H.** Let $a\in R$ have support $S(a)\subseteq G$; for $h\in G_2$
> put $S_h=\{u\in G_{\rm odd}: hu\in S(a)\}$ (the partition of the support by
> $2$-part). Then in $R_\chi=F_\chi[G_2]$
> $$a_\chi=\sum_{h\in G_2}\Bigl(\sum_{u\in S_h}\chi(u)\Bigr)\,h .$$
> Consequently
> 1. $a_\chi=0\iff\sum_{u\in S_h}\chi(u)=0$ for **every** $h\in G_2$;
> 2. $a_\chi$ is a non-unit $\iff\sum_{u\in S(a)}\chi(u)=0$;
> 3. $a_\chi=0$ forces $|S_h|\ge2$ for every occupied coset, so
>    $\operatorname{wt}(a)\ge 2\cdot\#\{\text{occupied }G_2\text{-cosets}\}$;
> 4. if $\operatorname{wt}(a)\le3$ and $a_\chi=0$ for some $\chi$, then $a$
>    occupies a **single** coset, $a=h\,a_{\rm o}$ with
>    $a_{\rm o}\in\mathbb F_2[G_{\rm odd}]$, and then $a_{\chi'}\in F_\chi'$ is a
>    **scalar** at every factor: $a$ is zero-or-unit everywhere, i.e.
>    $\operatorname{Ann}(a)$ is an idempotent ideal.

**Proof.** The displayed formula is the definition of the projection: each
$g=hu\in G$ maps to $\chi(u)h\in F_\chi[G_2]$, and $G_2$ is a basis, giving 1.
The residue map $F_\chi[G_2]\to F_\chi$ sends every $h\mapsto1$, so the residue
of $a_\chi$ is the total sum, giving 2. For 3, a single nonzero $\chi(u)$
cannot vanish ($\chi(u)\in F_\chi^\ast$), so each occupied coset needs at least
two support points. For 4, $2\cdot2=4>3$ forces one occupied coset; then
$a=h\,a_{\rm o}$ and $a_\chi=\chi(a_{\rm o})\,h$ is a unit multiple of a basis
element if $\chi(a_{\rm o})\neq0$, else $0$. $\square$

## 4. Theorem J-I (weight law: no trinomial pair is ever mixed)

> **Theorem J-I.** If $\operatorname{wt}(A)\le3$ and $\operatorname{wt}(B)\le3$
> then the parent $(A,B)$ is **not mixed** — on any lattice $(\ell,m)$. More
> generally, a mixed parent requires at least one of $A,B$ to have weight
> $\ge4$ spread over $\ge2$ distinct $G_2$-cosets.

**Proof.** Mixed means (Theorem J-G) some factor $\chi_0$ with
$A_{\chi_0}=B_{\chi_0}=0$ and some factor $\chi_1$ where both are non-units but
not both zero; say $A_{\chi_1}\neq0$ is a non-unit. Vanishing at $\chi_0$ plus
J-H(4) (weight $\le3$) makes $A$ single-coset, hence zero-or-unit at *every*
factor; being a non-unit at $\chi_1$ then forces $A_{\chi_1}=0$ — contradiction.
Symmetrically for $B$. $\square$

> **Corollary (trinomial dichotomy + parity criterion).** For weight-$\le3$
> pairs only two outcomes exist: **immune** — iff $A$ and $B$ each occupy a
> single $G_2$-coset and share a common character zero — or **demote-full**
> (resp. degenerate when $k_P=0$).

**Machine verification.**
* **EXP-054**: exhaustive census of **653,022,021** weight-$\le3$ pairs
  (translation-normalised, hence exhaustive up to independent translations)
  over 18 lattices — every catalogue lattice $(3,6),(6,3),(6,6),(9,6),(12,6),
  (15,6),(30,6)$, the two previously underexplored ones $(12,12),(15,12)$, and
  a parity grid $(2,3),(4,3),(2,5),(4,5),(6,5),(2,9),(4,9),(8,3),(6,9)$:
  **zero mixed pairs**, matching J-I. 450 sampled pairs re-decided by the
  independent direct route (build $[A\,B]$, take $L_{\rm pre}$, iterate ideal
  powers): zero disagreements.
* **Coset criterion on the catalogue (EXP-053)**: all 202 parents are
  weight-$(3,3)$; the single-coset predicate separates the trichotomy
  **exactly** — 10 immune ⇔ single-coset, 192 demote-full ⇔ multi-coset, no
  exceptions. (Concretely for $m=6$: $G_2=\mathbb Z_2$ acts through the
  $y$-exponent parity, so immunity ⇔ all $A$-monomials share a $y$-parity and
  all $B$-monomials share a $y$-parity.)
* **Lemma falsification battery (EXP-053)**: 256 random weight-$\le3$
  polynomials over eight lattices — J-H(4) never violated (no multi-coset
  polynomial vanishes exactly; every single-coset polynomial has idempotent
  annihilator).

## 5. Theorem J-J (mixed parents exist; the threshold 4 is attained)

> **Theorem J-J.** On $(\ell,m)=(2,3)$ take $A=(1+x)(y+y^2)$ and $B=Ay$ (both
> weight 4, occupying the two $G_2$-cosets $\{1,x\}$). Then
> $$I=\underbrace{R_1}_{y=1\ \text{factor}}\times\underbrace{(u)}_{\text{radical of }\mathbb F_4[u]/(u^2)},\quad
> \dim I=4,\ \dim I^2=\dim I^\infty=2,$$
> so the parent is **mixed**: $k_P=8$, $\dim S=4$, and exactly
> $2^8-2^4=240$ of the $255$ nonzero classes demote (fraction $16/17$).

**Verification (three independent routes, EXP-053).**
1. ideal chain: `power_dims = [4, 2, 2]`, case `mixed`;
2. EXP-052's module $F$-chain: $[8,4,4]$ → $\dim S=4$ (agrees);
3. brute force: all $255$ nonzero classes decided by EXP-047's exact rank test
   → exactly $240$ demote, matching $2^{k_P}-2^{\dim S}$ to the integer.

Structurally the witness is exactly what J-H predicts: $u=1+x$ is nilpotent
(the even direction), $y+y^2$ vanishes **exactly** on the $y=1$ factor (the odd
direction, where $\mathbb F_2[\mathbb Z_3]$ is reduced) and equals $1$ on the
$\mathbb F_4$ factor, leaving $u\ne0$ nilpotent there — one full factor and one
deficient factor, which is precisely mixed. Weight 4 is forced: two occupied
cosets, two support points each.

**Consequence for problem J-A.** J-A asked whether a mixed BB parent exists at
all. Answer: **yes** (J-J), but never inside the weight-$\le3$ family used
throughout the literature (J-I). The catalogue's all-or-nothing demote law is
therefore *explained*, with no enumeration: it is the weight-3 case of J-I.

## 6. What is still open

  do mixed parents exist, and what is the minimal $n$ of a mixed parent with

  some perturbation directions demote, others cannot. No mixed parent with
  useful $[[n,k,d]]$ is known — the (2,3) witness is a $[[12,8]]$ toy.
* **Beyond single-row.** J-G governs the single-row syzygy channel only; the
  multi-row light channel (EXP-051) is decided separately by CP-SAT exclusion.
