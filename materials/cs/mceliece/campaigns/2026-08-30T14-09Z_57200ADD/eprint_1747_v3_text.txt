      Extending Distinguishing to Key Recovery for
            Subfield Subcodes of GRS codes
                                       working draft

                                           Abstract
        Ghoshal, Ishai, Jain, and Sun recently introduced a novel quasipolynomial-time
      distinguisher for GRS subcodes (including Goppa codes), leaving key recovery as an
      open problem. This note presents an approach for turning the distinguisher into a full
      key-recovery attack. The overall complexity is dominated by a few executions of the
      distinguisher, and the approach is experimentally validated on Goppa codes over 𝔽4 .
      We conjecture that this recovery route applies to binary Goppa codes as well.

  The overall complexity is dominated by the 𝑐 executions of the distinguisher and by the
enumeration in Step 3, which solves 𝑚𝑐−1 𝑞 𝑚(𝑐−2) linear systems; the remaining steps are
negligible. We have experimentally validated the end-to-end attack on Goppa codes over
F4 .
   In its present state the attack has no direct evidence or proofs for binary Goppa codes.
Computing the hold-out kernels of Step 1 even for the smallest such codes requires a
prohibitive number of operations and an equally prohibitive amount of memory. It has,
however, been checked experimentally on non-subfield random subcodes of GRS codes and
on Goppa codes over F4 , and recovered the secret key in every instance. The applicability
of the attack to binary Goppa codes remains only conjectural.

Organization of the paper. The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2
provides the necessary preliminaries. Section 3 recalls the hold-out distinguisher of [7].
Section 4 details our key-recovery algorithm for base-field subcodes (𝑚 = 1). Section 5
relates it to the filtration attack against GRS codes. Section 6 extends the attack to
subfield subcodes (𝑚 ≥ 2). Section 7 reports the experiments for both regimes. Section 8
describes the adjustments required for Goppa codes. Section 9 refines the multiplicity
shortening over F𝑞𝑚 and uses it to align the Frobenius branches.

The updated version of [7], dated August 27, 2026, extends the hold-out distinguisher to a
key-recovery algorithm following the approach of Sidelnikov and Shestakov [13]: after the
multipliers are absorbed, the top-order Hermite interpolation coefficients are shown to
